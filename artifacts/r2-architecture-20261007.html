Pattern collapse, and the systems that compete tonight

This is the sequel to the architecture snapshot of 6 October. Phase N has finished. Its generator is calibrated when it reads real chart histories, but when it writes a whole song on its own, its patterns collapse. This evening we measured what that collapse is, the human judged it blind, three models worked through what the architecture rules out, and tonight complete alternative systems are compared as steps toward a working generator. No results from tonight's runs exist yet. Times are UTC.

1Where yesterday's page left off, and what changed

Yesterday's page ended with phase N training: the natural generator, with no requests and long-range memory switched off. Phase C, which teaches the model to follow requests for LN share and difficulty, was to start from the selected phase-N checkpoint. The checkpoint chosen today is 56M, the one after 56 million training decisions. The research day page covers the morning and afternoon; its plan for the night, an LN fine-tune plus a difficulty-controller pilot, was withdrawn at about 16:05, when the human redirected the evening.

The new primary problem. Phase N does not yet generate playable charts stably over a whole song. Fixing pattern collapse in long-range self-generation comes first. Controls (LN share and length, perceived difficulty, the five style concepts) are a separate, later problem, and they must work while patterns stay playable.

The human also pointed out that the withdrawn fine-tune, like phase N, was trained by teacher-forced cross-entropy (CE) on real histories, which never targets drift as generation gets longer, and asked for targeted, strictly scoped experiments.

2What collapse is, measured

The short version: the training objective is satisfied while generation fails. On real histories the model is calibrated. Its expected number of heads per row is within ±0.05 of the realised number in every difficulty band and song position. On its own histories the whole-song statistics fail. measured

A formal picture

A real chart is hierarchical. A chart-level organisation θ is chosen once per chart: density, chord habit, LN level and length, lane vocabulary, jack regime, hand use. Every row is then written under that θ. The given head skeleton S identifies little of θ.

real charts p*(D | S) = ∫ ∏ₖ p*(aₖ | sₖ, θ) · p*(θ | S) dθ R2 q(aₖ | last 511 rows, exact lane state, local skeleton)

R2 has no θ. It re-estimates the chart's organisation at every decision from its recent rows, in effect the last 64–128. On real histories that estimate is good, because a real chart holds its level. On its own output each chance excursion becomes part of the evidence for the next rows. inferred from the measurements below.

Three components

F1 · Start drawn toward the band mean

Within 64–192 rows a run settles into a regime biased by difficulty band, and the offset then stays flat. Band 2 comes out too busy and too LN-heavy; bands 4–5 too sparse and looping. A 64-row real prefix removes about two thirds of the band-2 excess for the whole song. measured

F2 · No holding of chart identity

Nothing older than 511 rows reaches the decision except lane clocks. Even after a correct start, identity fades past about 512 rows. The model's long-range anchoring is a proxy that breaks on its own output: βcum 0.51 on real prefixes, 0.23 on its own. measured The mechanism, a calibrated short-memory response compounding on its own output, is inferred.

F3 · Local organisation degrades

Dependence between adjacent rows is weaker than in real charts from the start, and it gets weaker as self-generated history accumulates, at a fixed song position (X2). measured on a proxy measure; the blind judgment in §3 did not validate it.

Why βcum is the telling number. In real charts the running statistic since song start adds only about R² +0.05 over the last 64 rows when predicting the next block, a few thousandths of a nat per decision in CE. A window stands in for the chart's level as long as the history holds that level, which real charts do. So CE gives no reason to learn a true anchor, and in generation the window drifts with the model's own output. measured coefficients; inferred mechanism.

Can the skeleton supply θ? No (X3)

48 whole-song skeleton features were fitted to 10 θ coordinates on 11,368 charts and scored on 1,163 held-out charts. Within each band every coordinate has R² below 0.3; the best overall is heads per row at 0.32. Adding the star band as an input lifts heads per row to 0.64 and the 3-or-more-head chord rate to 0.58, but held share, LN length, loop rate and hand balance stay below 0.3 even then. By the threshold fixed in advance (below 0.3), θ must be drawn per chart rather than read off the skeleton. measured

Ruled out as causes or levers

  • The training window and its start rule: the full prefix is encoded, and calibration at rows 0–64 is fine. measured
  • More training: from 48M to 64M, degenerate windows rose from 0.11 to 0.14 and busy-lock runs of 30+ rows from 14 to 70 of 348 runs, at flat NLL. measured
  • Row temperature: T = 0.8 broke composition (91% single-head rows late in the song, against 69.6% in real charts). The test was inconclusive on collapse. measured
  • 8- and 16-bar phrase features: regime changes in real charts cluster at 4-bar starts (1.5–2.1×), which the model already sees, and not further at 8 or 16 bars. measured
  • R1-style fixed-lane runs: 1–4 of 348 runs. measured

3The human's blind judgment (X0)

Every measure above is a proxy until it agrees with what a player sees. X0 checks that. The human judged 12 full songs, 3 per band (bands 2–5), in random order: 8 generated by 56M from the song start and 4 real charts as foils, about 57 minutes of audio in all. The key stayed sealed until the sheet was in. For each song the human marked where collapse starts and what kind it is.

Generated, 8 songs
6 marked collapsed; one band-3 and one band-4 song not marked.
Real charts, 4 songs
1 marked collapsed: a band-5 LN chart of 451 s.

First read unscored, superseded below

The first read put the sheet against the key without scoring. The human's descriptions name LN distribution and organisation, jack organisation (same-lane, mechanical repetitive jacks), repetition and lack of variance, hand balance, and difficulty diverging between parts of a song. Most first marks fall early in the song. The human added that the marked ranges are not precise and that only parts of each chart were checked, so unmarked windows are not reliable negatives.

Scoring: no collapse measure passes robustly

Each candidate measure was scored per 64-row window on the 12 songs, by window-level AUC against the human's marks. The bar set in advance was AUC ≥ 0.75. Item-bootstrap 90% intervals are 0.3–0.45 wide, so differences under about 0.05 are noise. measured

  • Five LN-related measures pass under the primary labelling, and none survives the checks. LN share against the band's source range 0.83, an LN-level drift measure 0.82, the all-four-lanes-busy rate 0.79, the longest one-lane lock 0.78, maximum drift 0.78. They fall to 0.58–0.72 when any overlap counts as marked, when the marks are widened by ±3 s or ±6 s, or when one song is left out; that song supplies 15 of the 26 marked windows. Inside a marked song they locate the marked span at only 0.47–0.68. measured They mostly separate songs by LN level rather than find the collapsed spans. inferred
  • The others fail under every labelling. Envelope-exit count 0.58; stay rate 0.57–0.66 at item level; identity drift in heads per row 0.32, which is inverted; shuffle-corrected adjacency 0.51–0.53. measured
  • The marked real chart is a band-5 LN chart with held share 0.37, against a band-5 source median of 0.004. The LN measures rank it the most collapsed of all 12 songs. measured What the human calls collapse includes some real LN-heavy charting, so LN measures need a reference such as the chart's own level, not the band median. inferred
  • Where the marks fall. In 5 of the 6 marked generated songs the first mark comes before row 512: rows 121–472, 4–28% of the way into the song. The exception is one low-confidence mark at 87% of the song. measured What the human sees is mostly inside the encoder's 511-row history, not identity lost beyond it. The marks are partial (19–80% of each song lies after the last mark), so they may be biased early. inferred
  • The descriptions do show up in some statistics. The song described with repetitive jacks has marked windows with jack rate +2.3 IQR and row repetition +7.8 IQR, which separate its marked from its unmarked windows at AUC about 0.98. The song whose difficulty diverged between parts has a window-to-window SD of heads per row 1.87× the band's sources. One song described for its LN and jack organisation has less LN in its marked windows, so the organisation meant is not LN share. measured

Consequence for tonight proposed, for the human's morning review: X0 did not validate a subset of measures, so the bake-off judges arms on the full set of seven measures registered before the night and reports the LN-share measures separately. The decode-time selection arm runs in two variants (§5). A human blind screen of the winner stays the final judge.

4Architecture choice → what it rules out

The human asked which architecture choice intrinsically constrains which behaviour. The causal chain both design rounds arrived at: measured links, inferred chain

  1. No chart-level input, so the start regresses to the corpus mean given the skeleton (F1).
  2. The TCN and its row-local tokens give chart identity nowhere to live (F2).
  3. Teacher-forced CE never penalises losing it, and will not teach a new anchor unless training forces it.
  4. Single-sample decoding never corrects an excursion.
  5. Selection on short-chart panels and NLL never notices.

Network families

  • No encoder family fixes F2 on its own. The TCN makes a convergent anchor impossible. A GRU, a linear RNN or state-space model (LRU, S4), attention, or a two-rate hierarchy make it possible, but none makes it likely without pressure from the recipe: history dropout, on-policy training, or a held θ. Precedent: R1's GRU over all rows and R2 v1's landmark attention could represent an anchor and went unused. inferred from measured precedent
  • Latency does not discriminate between families. The per-step cost that grows with song position (2.5 µs × k) comes from the sampler replaying the whole prefix at every step, not from the network; an incremental state makes a step about 1.5 ms flat. Per-step encoder costs are at or below the TCN's, against a budget of at least 69 ms per row at the 95th percentile of band 5. measured The sampler's replay is what needs fixing.
  • Training cost differs: forward and backward over 2,304 tokens takes 294 ms for the TCN, 201 ms for a GRU and 1,611 ms for a full causal transformer (5.5×). measured The current window draw re-encodes about 5 positions per scored one, so scoring whole charts should give 2–3× more training per machine-hour for any family. inferred
  • Explicit θ before a VAE latent. Named whole-chart statistics have no posterior collapse and are a direct control surface. A VAE latent faces near-certain collapse under this objective, because the decoder already predicts well from its window. A clustered style token or a small residual latent is an add-on for unnamed style, only if that gap persists. About 4,000 song groups bound any chart-level model. inferred
  • The generation paradigm is orthogonal to F1 and F2. Block-autoregressive generation is plan-then-realise. Windowed masked refinement suits the mapper's local-edit scenario but needs a legality redesign and training from scratch, so it is a separate track. Whole-song non-autoregressive generation breaks the standing constraint that chart decisions and publication stay causal. inferred

Three models produced these findings independently, in two design rounds with network-family addenda and an earlier code and information-path audit. They agreed on the causal chain and that no encoder family fixes F2 alone; they disagreed on whether the θ arm should also carry the self-anchor channels (one round yes, the other no), on the decision rule (rank sum against gap closure), and on whether attention deserves a later test for pattern vocabulary.

5Tonight: a system bake-off toward a working generator proposed · no results yet

Instead of testing one mechanism at a time, tonight compares complete systems on the same long-chart benchmark. The arms are nested, so each difference isolates one change. All trained arms start from 56M with the same schedule (200k warm-up, learning rate 1e-4 cosine to 3e-5), the LN level and length inputs off, and equal training wall-clock of about 1.25 h each; each arm's cosine schedule is set from its measured throughput.

The long-chart panel

Held-out charts, one per song group, 25 groups per band. Bands 3–5 use charts of at least 1,500 rows. Band 2 uses at least 1,000 rows, because only 4 held-out band-2 charts reach 1,500. X0's songs are excluded. Each system runs in two modes: from the song start at three seeds, capped at 2,560 rows, which tests F1 and F2 together; and continuing a real first-third prefix at one seed, which tests F2 alone. B3 is judged with θ drawn from its prior, the mode a user would get; θ copied from the real chart is a diagnostic only.

Decision rule and guards

The pre-registered measures include identity hold at rows 1k+ after a real prefix, the stay rate and band-2 all-lanes-busy exits, the ratio of between-chart spread to real charts (variety), first-to-last-third correlation from the song start, and adjacent-row dependence (F3). Each is turned into a gap closure per band:

g = (m_system − m_source) / (m_B0 − m_source) G = mean of g (0 = like real charts, 1 = like B0)
  • Winner: the guard-passing arm with the lowest G, at least 0.15 below B1, with a chart-bootstrap 90% interval on the difference that excludes zero. It then goes to a human blind screen of 8 long charts.
  • Guards: legal export; NLL with θ unknown at most B0 + 0.02 nats; single-head share and 4-gram ratio inside the source envelope; end-of-song exits at most 1.5× the source's; no head-lock run of 30 rows or more.

6What stays open

  • A measure that tracks the human. None passed X0 robustly. LN measures need a per-chart reference; jack and repetition statistics matched the one song described that way. A better-labelled panel is needed before any measure gates a decision.
  • Start or drift, by band (X1). Does a 64-row real prefix remove the start offset in heads per row? Band 2 is start-dominant (55% removed at both spans); band 4 start-dominant with a wide interval (58–61%); band 3 undetermined; band 5 mixed, just above the drift line (26% removed at rows 512–1k). Every band's interval at rows 512–1k spans at least two verdict zones. measured
  • A learned anchor against a hand-built one: the two-rate encoder arm, not built tonight.
  • Unnamed style such as lane vocabulary and motif habits, if B3 leaves variety compressed: a clustered style token or a residual latent.
  • Later rounds: an on-policy objective, plan-then-realise, block reranking with a learned critic, and a from-scratch encoder comparison with whole-chart scoring.
  • What ends a section. Real section ends do not follow the 8- or 16-bar grid; they likely need audio or the mapper's intent, which are outside R2 v2.
  • Controls return as θ coordinates once natural generation holds.
