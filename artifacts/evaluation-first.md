<a id="d-corpus-referenced"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. Evaluation measures how close generated output is to the corpus, at several scales and views, with metadata as conditions.** The corpus holds the ranked and loved 4K charts at 1 to 8 stars. Metadata such as BPM segments and difficulty enter as conditions; community tags are optional because they are sparse. A model of the corpus at these views is cheaper and easier than a generative chart model, and can target the known failure modes. The evaluator is fitted on the corpus alone; generator outputs do not supply its negatives, at least at first. This replaces the classifier two-sample test of the 2026-10-02 proposal, which trained on generated outputs.

<a id="d-placement-is-timing"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. Musical placement is a beat-tracking problem, owned by the timing work; the beat grid is an evaluation condition, refined by the skeleton (heads and releases).** The pre-V3 timing module (BeatThis plus GridFitter, timing v3) worked on it. The chart evaluator judges a chart given its grid; whether the grid fits the audio is the timing evaluation's question. The human also recalled that feature extraction in legacy v2 failed; the agent reads this as Control V3, twelve handcrafted window statistics rejected on 2026-08-31 as a player state (`8e5e7ad:README.md`).

<a id="d-conditions-compositional"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. Conditions are compositional.** Components: timing (beat grid) or skeleton (grid plus head and release times), context (given parts of the chart), full audio Mel. Any combination is a condition, and every result names its combination.

<a id="d-partial-scope"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. Evaluation works on part of a chart; the given part and the scored part are both free.** For example a continuation (given before t, scored after), a local edit (given outside a span, scored inside), or one passage with nothing given.

<a id="d-operators-reserved"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. The rhythm, arrangement and load operators are not implemented now.** They are to be designed carefully against the properties wanted, and Astra will iterate on them.

<a id="d-fold-rescales-beats"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. Folding a notated BPM into the canonical range rescales the beats.** Halving or doubling a BPM changes beat positions, subdivisions and bars, not only the BPM label.

<a id="d-fold-per-segment"></a>**Decision, human, 2026-10-03. Every BPM segment is folded into the canonical range on its own.** This replaces the agent's single fold per chart taken from the longest segment. A song moving from 158 to 162 BPM is canonical 158 then 81.

## How "accept all ranked" and "reject what ranked charts contain" fit

<a id="h-rate-not-presence"></a>**Hypothesis, agent, open, 2026-10-02.** A ranked chart with a 28-attack anchor is acceptable; a generated chart that does the same is rejected. Both hold if the defect is a matter of rate, length, placement and context: how often such passages occur per song, how long they last, whether they sit where the music or the rest of the chart calls for them, at which difficulty. The lineage's scorers caught extremes and were blind to how often and how long ([s-response-blind](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-response-blind)); its gates measured whole charts while the human judged passages ([s-eval-sensitivity](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-eval-sensitivity)). A distribution-level target ([d-target-distribution](#d-target-distribution)) is the natural home for this: the reference supplies the rate of every unusual thing, and generated output is wrong when it departs from those rates, overall or within a passage. What it would not catch: a pattern at the right rate in the wrong musical place. That needs audio-conditioned comparison and is left for later.

## Part 1 design, converged 2026-10-03

The decisions above are the human's; the rest of this section is the agent's `(proposed)` design built on them, with M0, M1, red-line classification and the evaluation interface built.

<a id="p-eval-object"></a>**What is evaluated (proposed).** A triple: the chart (`.osu`), its condition record and its scope.

- The condition record names the components given to the generator ([d-conditions-compositional](#d-conditions-compositional)): `timing` (red timing points) or `skeleton` (timing plus head and release times; whether releases stay in it is open after [d-no-release-input](r1-verdict.md#d-no-release-input)), `context` (spans of the chart fixed in advance), `audio` (full-song Mel); plus scalar requests such as a target star. The evaluator reads the grid from the record, never from the generated file: the lineage exported a constant 120 BPM.
- The scope is two span sets on the chart's time ([d-partial-scope](#d-partial-scope)): given spans, which measures may read as context, and scored spans. Nothing a condition fixed is scored; under `skeleton`, event times are not scored.
- The reference is the same measurement on corpus charts with the same scope shape, conditioned on what the generator does not choose: target star and the canonical BPM of the grid. The evaluator is fitted on the corpus only ([d-corpus-referenced](#d-corpus-referenced)).

<a id="p-eval-layers"></a>**Layers (proposed).**

| Layer | What it is | State |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M0 R2 corpus | One row per usable 4K chart in `dataset/`: hashes, acquisition, header and osu! API fields, computed star, grid summary, song group, R1 membership, evaluation split. Files that are not 4K osu!mania or have no readable grid are left out at build time ([d-two-filter-layers](#d-two-filter-layers)); the summary names every 4K chart left out | Built; [s-m0-m1](#s-m0-m1) |

<a id="p-operator-properties"></a>**Properties an operator should have (proposed; to be settled with the human before Astra iterates).** **Superseded 2026-10-03** by [operator-properties](operator-properties.md): seven properties after the human's answer, each tied to Control V3, September-control or lineage-evaluator failures with mechanical checks; the list below is kept as first written.

- Defined on any scope, reading given spans as context.
- Scores nothing the condition fixed.
- Unchanged under mirrored lanes, under renotation at half or double BPM, and under a shift of chart and grid together.
- Compared with the corpus as a conditional distribution (star, canonical BPM), aggregated over a song as rate and length, not presence ([h-rate-not-presence](#h-rate-not-presence)).
- Calibrated both ways before it accepts anything: false alarms on held-out corpus at the nominal rate, and a dose-response curve for each injected defect family; an operator with no measured sensitivity is dropped.
- Never a training signal or a selector for a generator. Challenged 2026-10-03: the human's direction uses the evaluation system as a preference source for training ([d-beyond-row-ce](r1-verdict.md#d-beyond-row-ce)); what this property guarded against is kept there as a proposal.
- Caution from legacy v2: Control V3's window statistics failed as a generative player state; they omitted transitions, durations, four-finger relations and memory across windows (`8e5e7ad:README.md`). That says nothing direct about such statistics as evaluators; the calibration harness measures that.

<a id="p-canonical-beats"></a>**Canonical beats (M1, built 2026-10-03, revised the same day).** Each musical segment's notated BPM is moved into [80, 160) by its own factor 2^k ([d-fold-per-segment](#d-fold-per-segment)), and the factor rescales the segment's positions, subdivisions and bars ([d-fold-rescales-beats](#d-fold-rescales-beats)): a notated 1/4 at 240 BPM is a canonical 1/8 at 120. Beats restart at every segment's offset. Bars are kept apart from beats: the grid lists bar starts, and a bar holds the meter in canonical beats, so at a notated 240 BPM a 4/4 canonical bar spans two notated bars. A chart renotated at half or double BPM, as a whole or one segment at a time, gets identical canonical positions, subdivisions and bars. Each event gets the smallest canonical subdivision in {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96} within 2 ms, or none, and a signed residual in ms. At 2 ms the denominators above 16 cover much of the time axis by chance, so grid statistics count only denominators up to 16. A supplied grid is valid when every segment folds into the canonical range; raw notated limits do not apply to it (`2044c2b`, [d-astra-not-accepted](operator-properties.md#d-astra-not-accepted)). Code: `src/ensomi_model/evaluation/beats.py` on branch `eval/corpus-beats` of `ensomi-model`.

<a id="p-red-lines"></a>**Musical red lines (built 2026-10-03; rules are the agent's, [d-musical-red-lines](#d-musical-red-lines)).** Every red line is read as written, implausible values included, and labelled. Musical: the first usable line, a change of tempo or offset the notes need, or a bar line at the BPM in force that moves the canonical beat phase (a meter change at a notated BPM above 160 sits on a half canonical beat). Redundant: continues the grid in force on its canonical beats; it may still start a bar. Expressive, kept out of the grid: superseded by another line at the same offset; BPM outside 20 to 1000; no event and shorter than one canonical beat; a bar line shorter than its own canonical bar (barline art); or a detour, a run of lines after which the grid in force resumes (or the chart ends) while every note of the run lies on a binary subdivision of that grid and is described by it at least as simply as by the run's own lines (sum of log2 of the denominators). Lines at the BPM in force are never detours, since only a BPM change alters the scroll speed. Triplets do not count toward a detour, because a real 3:2 tempo change puts the notes on the old grid's triplets. Musical and redundant lines start bars unless they carry osu!'s omit-first-barline flag. Checks on the 5,784 ranked and loved charts with several red lines: no chart loses on-grid notes against its raw red lines; whole-chart renotation changes no role. Rejected on the way: allowing the grid in force a per-line saving of 4 bits dropped a genuine three-note ritardando (*Qilin's Prance*, Normal); aligning bar lines to notated beats was not invariant under renotation; the detour test without the binary rule dropped the return to the main tempo in 198-132-198 and 225-150-225 BPM songs. Code: `src/ensomi_model/evaluation/redlines.py`.

<a id="p-eval-interface"></a>**Evaluation interface (built 2026-10-03).** `src/ensomi_model/evaluation/case.py`. `Chart.from_osu` keeps every red line; `Chart.section` cuts by span; spans parse osu! editor timestamps (`00:12:345..00:40:000`, open ends allowed), milliseconds, or bars of a grid, with union, intersection and complement. Conditions compose with `|` from `Timing` or `Skeleton`, `Context` and `Audio` ([d-conditions-compositional](#d-conditions-compositional)); `condition_from_chart` builds the condition a generator would receive from a corpus chart. `Scope` holds free given and scored spans, never overlapping ([d-partial-scope](#d-partial-scope)). `EvalCase` refuses scored spans that overlap the condition's context, takes its grid from the condition or else from the chart's musical red lines, and assigns a long note's release to where its head lies. A `Skeleton` gives head times, and release times only when asked, following [d-no-release-input](r1-verdict.md#d-no-release-input). Operators follow a protocol naming the aspects they judge; `evaluate` skips one that judges an aspect the condition fixed (the grid; head times, and release times when given, under a skeleton). No operator is implemented ([d-operators-reserved](#d-operators-reserved)). Code at `8a38476` and `7333f85`.

<a id="p-split"></a>**Song groups and split (M0, built 2026-10-03; the split rule is the agent's reading of [d-use-all-data](#d-use-all-data)).** Files join one song group when they share a set directory, set id, beatmap id, normalised artist and title (trailing size markers such as "(TV Size)" removed), audio bytes, or R1's own group. By hash of the group id: 15% held-out, 15% calibration, 70% fit. A group holding any chart of R1's TRAIN catalog is never held out, so R1 can be measured on songs it never trained on. No acquisition is reserved wholesale. Groups are formed over every file, including those left out of the corpus. Code: `src/ensomi_model/evaluation/corpus.py`; committed output `data/r2-corpus.parquet` with a summary next to it.

<a id="p-chart-eval"></a>### Earlier Part 1 proposal, 2026-10-02 (superseded)

**Superseded 2026-10-03** by [Part 1 design, converged](#p-eval-object). The classifier two-sample test trained on generated outputs, which [d-corpus-referenced](#d-corpus-referenced) rules out; the passage unit and the measures move into the reserved operators ([d-operators-reserved](#d-operators-reserved)); items 1, 4, 5 and 6 carry over into the split, the calibration harness and the report. Kept as written:

Scope per [d-eval-scope](#d-eval-scope): the object judged is a finished `.osu` file, and every result names its generation condition (which of context, event times and audio were given, and whether the rollout was causal on the model's own history). R1 today runs with given context and given event times, without audio.

1. **Reference split, frozen.** 2 to 6 star ranked and loved 4K charts, split by song group into a calibration part and a held-out part. Neither part is ever used to train a model or tune a generator. A failure set is held out the same way. Answers: evaluators tuned on their own failures, a development panel of five TRAIN songs reused across dozens of experiments ([s-eval-sensitivity](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-eval-sensitivity)).
2. **Unit: the passage, conditioned on difficulty.** Windows of a few seconds as well as whole charts, compared with reference windows of matching chart star band and local density. Answers: whole-song LN ratios met by stuffing one passage.
3. **Measures: rates and lengths, not presence.** Per-window distributions of column runs and anchors, jack lengths, LN share and LN duration in beats, release-to-next-head gaps, chord sizes, head offsets from the song's grid. Compared by tail rates and quantiles with song-level bootstrap intervals. In addition, a classifier two-sample test: a fresh discriminator trained per evaluation on reference against generated windows; its accuracy says how distinguishable the output is, and its most confident windows are the queue for human inspection. The discriminator is evaluation only, never a training signal or selector, or it becomes the next thing to game.
4. **Calibrated both ways before use.** False-alarm rate: held-out reference against calibration reference must pass. Sensitivity: constructed negatives ([d-accept-set](#d-accept-set)) made by injecting each known complaint at controlled doses into held-out reference charts (anchors at k times the corpus rate, short LN and releases just before a head, LN on TAP passages, head jitter off the grid), plus the lineage's real rejected outputs where they can be tied to files. Report the smallest dose detected. An evaluator without both numbers is not used to accept anything.
5. **Reporting rules.** Every table carries rows for held-out reference, R1 with real times, and the candidate. At least the agreed number of songs and seeds, with song-level spread; a difference smaller than seed noise is reported as none ([s-eval-sensitivity](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-eval-sensitivity): seed noise about 0.2 star against promotion margins of 0.1).
6. **Human spot-check.** A small blind sample from the discriminator's queue, judged by the human, kept as files. It tests the evaluator, not the generator.

## Part 2 design (proposed, 2026-10-02)

<a id="p-claim-integrity"></a>### Part 2. Claim integrity for agent work

Failures it answers, all from the review, each written up with its evidence, its strength and its check in [agent-failure-modes](agent-failure-modes.md): the response scorer that was not in the played system ([s-response-not-in-loop](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-response-not-in-loop); [fm-claimed-not-done](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-claimed-not-done)); tests that verified the mechanism and never the claim ([fm-tests-not-binding](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-tests-not-binding)); conclusions drawn from runs below their preconditions ([s-undertrained](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-undertrained); [fm-preconditions](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-preconditions)); repair at the nearest module and questions recognised but not settled ([s-local-search](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-local-search); [fm-local-repair](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-local-repair)); the agent's own reading of Lens pages standing in as an evaluator, evaluators tuned on the failures they were built after, a reused development panel ([s-eval-sensitivity](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-eval-sensitivity); [fm-self-evaluation](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-self-evaluation)); "R1" naming a descendant that had dropped four of its components ([s-lineage-r1-is-not-r1](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-lineage-r1-is-not-r1); [fm-name-drift](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-name-drift)); expert advice recorded as adopted while ignored in substance ([opus/interpretation](lineage-review/opus/interpretation.md#5-the-two-expert-replies-and-what-became-of-them) section 5; [fm-advice-adopted-in-name](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-advice-adopted-in-name)); promotion margins below seed noise ([fm-margin-below-noise](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-margin-below-noise)). The one-line rules a brief copies are in its [standing rules](agent-failure-modes.md#standing-rules-for-briefs).

1. **Frozen evaluator.** Evaluation code and the reference split are a separate, versioned unit. Experiment jobs may run it and may not edit it; every report carries its content hash. Changing the evaluator is its own reviewed change, never part of an experiment. An evaluator added after a complaint carries its date and the charts it was tuned on, and is never scored on them; "pass" always names the evaluator hash and the threshold. Answers [fm-self-evaluation](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-self-evaluation) and the "pass" half of [fm-name-drift](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-name-drift).
2. **Pre-registered claims, with a stop rule.** Before a run, the brief states the claim, metric, threshold, songs, seeds, and the stop rule (a validation plateau over a stated number of checkpoints, or a stated number of passes over the data) together with the kind of conclusion the run may support. The result is judged against that statement. A post-hoc metric may be reported only as such. A conclusion about architecture, initialisation or direction from a run below its stop rule is reported as "endpoint rejected at budget" and nothing more; a structural finding cites the code line that shows it. Answers [fm-preconditions](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-preconditions).
3. **Execution and identity receipts.** Each generated output records the entry point, the session class, every scorer, planner, guidance or preference object instantiated with its resolved config, the checkpoint hash, the resolved config, the seed and the output hash. Every path the human uses writes the same receipt, the demo included, and keeps the chart. The launcher refuses to mark a report complete when an output it names has no receipt. A claim that a component is "in the system" is checked against the receipt, not the source code. The receipt also resolves every model name to its checkpoint hash and module list; a release name is reused only when the module list equals the release manifest's, and "held out" means absent from every ancestor's fit and selection sets, checked against ancestry manifests every run writes. Answers [fm-claimed-not-done](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-claimed-not-done) and [fm-name-drift](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-name-drift).
4. **End to end through the human's entry point.** A component result is reported only together with end-to-end output on the fixed panel, produced by the same path the human plays or qualification scores. "Works in a study script" is reported as exactly that. Answers [fm-claimed-not-done](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-claimed-not-done).
5. **Essential-constraint checks on every output.** Legality, causality of decisions, the target distribution, the agreed support rules: checked automatically, and a violation is reported with the result, not after it. The support settings and the share of gradient steps above the clip are in the receipt and in the claim line. Answers [fm-preconditions](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-preconditions).
6. **Independent verification before the human sees a claim.** A fresh, read-only agent recomputes the headline numbers from raw artifacts and labels each number checked or claimed, as the lineage review did ([README](lineage-review/README.md#state)); it matches every "in the system" sentence against the receipts, labels a claim with no claim test "untested", and rejects a report that uses the agent's page reads as a gate. Answers [fm-claimed-not-done](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-claimed-not-done), [fm-self-evaluation](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-self-evaluation), [fm-margin-below-noise](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-margin-below-noise).
7. **Claim tests and input-lesion tests.** Every claim in a report names the test or evaluator run that fails if the claim is false. Every model forward has a lesion test per named input (perturb it, assert the output changes, or document the input as unused in the same test). Each evaluator ships with a held-out failure witness that must fail and a held-out ranked chart that must pass. Mechanism-only tests are engineering and are not cited as evidence for a result. Answers [fm-tests-not-binding](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-tests-not-binding).
8. **Question and advice ledgers in every brief.** Each open question the agent recognises is settled with an artifact, scheduled as a named experiment, or parked by the human; a repair at the module nearest a symptom needs a ledger row naming the upstream alternative and why it is deferred; three consecutive repairs in one package without a row stop the job and escalate. Each recommendation from an expert or the human has a row with its condition, status and the artifact that shows the status; a conditional whose condition held and was not followed is flagged by the main thread before the next brief. Answers [fm-local-repair](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-local-repair) and [fm-advice-adopted-in-name](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-advice-adopted-in-name).
9. **Noise floor before criteria.** The first run after any evaluator change measures seed and song spread on the baseline rows; every promotion or non-regression criterion is then stated as a multiple of that spread; a claim about a recipe or an architecture needs two training seeds or carries the label "one training seed". The reporting side is Part 1 item 5. Answers [fm-margin-below-noise](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-margin-below-noise).
