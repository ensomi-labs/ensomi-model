# R2 phase N: why the natural model does not produce corpus-like LN distributions

Supersedes `report-partial.md` (same directory). Control-plane subagent (Claude), 2026-10-07 14:30-15:10 UTC.
Analysis of existing outputs only: no training, no new model runs or sampling, no tracked file edited.
Phase N = `artifacts/r2-runs/r2-phaseN-20261006`, checkpoints 48M (`ckpt-0048000198.pt`) and 64M
(`ckpt-0064000573.pt`), frozen code on `PYTHONPATH`. Goal as fixed by the human on 2026-10-07: generated charts
should have LN share and LN length *distributions* like the corpus, per star band. Every claim is marked
**[measured]** (from a finished job named in §9) or **[inferred]**.

## 0. Summary

1. **The level's start is roughly right; holding it is what fails [measured, strong].** Over the first
   128 rows, BOS runs have 0.86-0.93× the sources' spread of LN share, and at 48M the same fraction of LN-free
   starts (28 % against 30 %). Along the song the runs lose it. Correlation between the first and last
   thirds is 0.61-0.62, against 0.91 for the same charts' sources. Within-run excursions stay correlated over
   128-256 rows, against about 64 in real charts, and are 1.4-1.9× larger. Of the runs that start LN-free,
   only half stay LN-free; in the sources 81 % do.
2. **This is not a misreading of the model's own history [measured, strong].** At matched history LN
   share, band and row gap, the 48M model gives *less* LN on its own history than on real history (0.053
   against 0.062); 64M matches (0.087 against 0.085). Counterfactual prefix edits find no extra response to
   model-made history. Dense-row LNs count as level evidence exactly like non-dense ones. On real history the
   model's response to the history level matches the data in every window and history segment tested.
   [inferred] Calibrated one-step responses still compound in free runs: the model cannot tell its own chance
   excursions from real LN sections, so the level wanders.
3. **"Compressed" is the wrong word for single runs; per band, the shape is wrong [measured].** On 116
   fit_dev skeletons × 3 seeds, the share SD is 0.77× the sources' at 48M and 1.00× at 64M. The 0.43 quoted
   earlier came from 16 charts with seeds averaged. The real defects are three:
   - the near-zero mode is short: 15.5 % / 10.6 % of runs, against 23-24 % expected;
   - the level barely follows the skeleton: slope on the skeleton prediction 0.36 / 0.18, against 1.19;
   - the shift depends on the checkpoint:
     - 48M is too heavy in band 2 and too light and narrow in bands 4-5 (heavy share 7-11 % against 23-24 %);
     - 64M is too heavy in bands 2-4 (heavy share 33-43 % against 18-24 %).

   At 48M, over all bands pooled, the share distribution almost matches fit_dev (KS 0.055), but only because
   the band errors cancel.
4. **Placement within a level is right on average [measured, moderate].** At matched share, runs put
   relatively fewer LNs on dense rows than real charts (×0.77 / ×0.89) and make fewer short holds. Near-head
   releases per LN are equal.
5. **LN length shows the same failure: right on average, without chart-level styles [measured].**
   - **Mean length is right.** At matched share and skeleton, a run's median LN length equals the corpus'
     (×1.00 at 48M, ×1.06 at 64M). Paired with its own source, it is 0.999× and 1.06×.
   - **Within-chart spread matches** (SD of log length 0.60 against 0.59).
   - **Chart-to-chart styles are compressed.** The across-chart SD of per-chart median log length is
     0.63-0.74× fit_dev in every band, at both checkpoints (90 % CIs below 1), and 0.65-0.96× the same
     skeletons' sources.
   - **Missing chart types.** Charts whose typical LN is 1/8 beat, frequent in bands 4-5, are missing at 48M
     and rare at 64M.
   - **Short holds are rarer per LN but spread over more charts.**
     - Band 5: 2.4-3.7 % of LNs under 60 ms, against 6.6 %.
     - Band 2: 16-22 % of generated charts have at least one hold under 60 ms, against 0.3 % of real
       band-2 charts.
   - **Length style is not held.** Correlation between the first and last thirds' median length is
     0.20-0.36, against 0.52 in the sources.
6. **What decides LN length in the data [measured].**
   - Only 16-34 % of log-length variance lies between charts (ICC, ms and beats, per band).
   - The whole-song skeleton predicts 33 % (ms) / 44 % (beats) of the chart-mean log length.
   - Adding the LN share raises this to 54 % / 61 %: LN-heavy charts use shorter LNs (r = −0.41 to −0.50
     within band).
   - So a level variable already carries part of the length style. The rest is a residual chart-level SD of
     about 0.3 log units, which a length summary would carry.
7. **48M and 64M fail differently along the song.** 48M drifts down: the third-to-third change is −0.020,
   and the continuation from a real prefix is 23 % below the truth. 64M drifts up (+0.043) towards heavy
   charts. Both lack chart-level commitment, which fits the earlier observation that the BOS level swings
   between checkpoints while NLL stays flat [inferred].
8. **Recommendation [inferred].**
   - **What to run.** A prior-mode `ln_level` fine-tune is the best-supported remedy. In the data, once the
     whole-song level is known, the last 512 rows add almost nothing (weights 0.12 on history against 0.90 on
     the level), so a model trained with the input should lean on it and hold a drawn level.
   - **Lengths.** The fine-tune fixes lengths only in part. Draw a per-chart length summary jointly with the
     level from the same prior chart.
   - **Oracle mode.** It is worth one evaluation run on the fine-tuned checkpoint, as an obedience test. It
     no longer needs to probe placement.
   - Details and ranking are in §8.

Evidence strength:
- strong: (b), (c) and the length styles (several independent measures, two checkpoints);
- moderate: (d) and the per-band share shapes (25-31 charts per band, 3 seeds each);
- unverified until a model is trained: everything about remedies.

  - The 1.3-1.6× excess LN hazard in LN-free states makes the zero mode leak.
  - An LN-heavy creep at 64M or a decay at 48M then sets where the wandering level settles.

## 8. Recommendation (inference throughout)

### 8.1 A prior-mode `ln_level` fine-tune

**Would the model hold a drawn level? Probably yes.**
- The data says history is nearly redundant once the whole-song level is known: weights 0.12 on the last 512
  rows and 0.90 on the level, R² 0.65 either way.
- The model already learns the data's history response exactly on real history: slopes, segment weights,
  gain map.
- So with the level available in 70 % of training windows, it should learn a large weight on the level and a
  small one on history.
- In free runs, a chance excursion would then be read against a fixed anchor and decay, instead of
  propagating. That is the failure in (b).
- Own-history misreading (c) is absent, so nothing systematic would push against the anchor. 64M's +8 % in the
  last third is the only such effect.
- **Risks.**
  - The zero-initialised reader enters only through the query path (`hands`: `exact[0]` plus
    `ln_level_reader`). In 12M exposures at lr 1e-4 → 3e-5 it may be under-learned.
  - The logit channel is ±13.8 at a level of exactly 0 or 1. That is good for the exact-zero mode, but an
    unusual input scale.
- The fine-tune's free runs settle this: confirm and refute below.

**Would it fix the per-band share distribution?**
- Largely, through the prior. Astra's prior P(level | band, density tercile) reproduces the fit_train band
  marginal by construction, zero mode and heavy tail included, if the realised share tracks the input.
- **The skeleton coupling is coarse.** Band-5 zero-mode rates by density quartile are U-shaped
  (51 / 28 / 22 / 62 %), and a tercile of rows per second captures that only partly.
- **The neighbour prior is the upgrade.** The skeleton-neighbour distribution, already computed per chart in
  `skel_pred.parquet` (fit_dev; a fit_train version exists), is calibrated with PIT SD 0.291. It would give
  the level-skeleton joint as well. Worth it if dense LN-light charts matter, as with the guard (iv) panel
  charts.

**Would it fix lengths? Partly.**
- Matching the share fixes the share-driven part of length: band 2's shortening, part of band 5.
- The runs already follow the share-length coupling at 75-80 % strength.
- It leaves the residual per-chart length style uncommitted. That is about 40-45 % of the between-chart
  length variance, and runs hold it with r 0.20-0.36 along the song.
- Expected outcome: the per-chart length SD ratio rises from about 0.67 but stays below 1, and band 2's
  sprinkled short holds may persist.
- **Recommended change.** Add a length channel: median log LN length in beats, drawn jointly with the share
  from the same prior observation. Astra's prior already stores each observation's source sha256.
- The data says this summary is chart-level enough to matter (ICC 0.21-0.34 in beats) and not carried by
  skeleton and share (residual SD 0.32).
- Cost: one more query channel and one more prior field.

**Is a single oracle-mode probe still informative?**
- Less than before. Its planned use, placement within a level, is answered observationally: (d) fine on
  average, and (c) refutes the misreading it might have exposed.
- What it still adds, cheaply, on the fine-tuned checkpoint (sampling only, no extra training):
  - an obedience-and-hold test with a skeleton-consistent target per chart: realised share against the
    source's level on the same skeleton (slope, R²), thirds drift and block ACF;
  - per-chart length against the source's at the right level.
- A fixed-level sweep (for example 0, 0.05, 0.2, 0.5 on 20-30 skeletons) is a sharper obedience test and uses
  no hint. Run oracle once on the panel with it; do not treat it as a separate step.

**Confirms** (on F or a larger fit_dev set, three seeds, prior mode):
- per-band share KS against fit_dev ≤ 0.10;
- zero and heavy fractions within ±5 points per band;
- in fixed or oracle mode, realised-against-supplied slope ≥ 0.9 and R² ≥ 0.8;
- third-to-third correlation ≥ 0.85 and |drift| ≤ 0.02;
- within-run ACF at 256 rows ≤ 0.05, and 256-row SD ≤ 1.2× sources';
- of LN-free starts, ≥ 70 % stay below 0.025;
- with the length channel: per-chart length SD ratio ≥ 0.85 per band, band-5 q25 ≤ 0.15 beat, and band-2 "any
  hold under 60 ms" ≤ 5 % of charts.

**Refutes:**
- realised-against-supplied slope < 0.6;
- third-to-third correlation still about 0.6 (history overrides the input);
- zero-mode leak unchanged: the 1.3-1.6× hazard in LN-free states persists against an input of 0.

**Cost.** 12M exposures at phase N's 1,820 decisions/s is about 1.8 h of training. With 4M-interval full
evaluations, about 2.5-3 h. Adding a length channel is small code plus a prior refit (minutes).

## 10. Caveats, failed paths, odd observations

- **Sample size per band.** 25-31 charts × 3 seeds per band. Seeds are correlated, so per-band KS and SD
  comparisons are moderate evidence. The length SD ratios carry chart-level bootstrap CIs; all are below 1.
- **Chart-length filter.** F uses 300 ≤ K ≤ 2,500, and the F sources differ from fit_dev somewhat (per-chart
  length SD in band 3: 0.33 against 0.46). Comparisons against the same skeletons' sources are given
  alongside.
- **Lengths** use snapped releases. Medians are lumpy at 1/8, 1/4 and 1/2 beat.
- **PIT ties at a share of exactly 0** take the mid-rank. The zero-mode fractions are reported directly for
  that reason.
- **Failed path.** The 40-chart smoke of the history-segment regression suggested recency over-weighting on
  own history (0.35 against 0.24 on the last 64 rows). The full run refuted it: 0.34 against 0.42 at 48M,
  0.41 against 0.42 at 64M.
- **Odd observations, unexplained.**
  - The interventional prefix response (§4) is about 20× smaller than the observational slope.
  - At 64M a run starting LN-free in its first 128 rows ends heavy (≥ 0.3) 18 % of the time; the sources
    never do.
  - The F sources' within-chart block ACF at lag 64 is 0.28, against 0.37 for all charts; the K filter and
    demeaning bias on shorter charts are likely causes.
- **Single training run.** 48M and 64M come from one run, and checkpoint oscillation is not separated from
  training progress.
- **Unverified.**
  - Every claim in §8 about what a fine-tune would do.
  - Whether the `ln_level` reader is learned in 12M exposures.
  - Whether a density-tercile prior gives acceptable levels on extreme skeletons such as `fd9cfe4c04`.
  - The causal role of the 1.3-1.6× LN hazard in LN-free states.
