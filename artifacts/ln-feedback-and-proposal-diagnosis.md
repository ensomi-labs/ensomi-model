# Separating LN amount, proposal preference and continuation judgment

The imported critique after the failed memory fit questioned whether modules
were solving the problems their names implied. It identified prefix-balance
feedback, overly strong information isolation, support coverage, fixed-bank
learning and the lack of an automatic native qualifier. Its original authorship
is not independently established; it is retained as [imported material,
private, local](private/human-inputs/01a0ebe8-d368-7ba3-9d56-48da0524c5b6.md#imported-critique-20260927).
Subsequent checks assessed those objections individually.

## Some objections held, others needed correction

The LN controller really does prefer prefix balance beyond a scope-total
request: under a valid .5 total consisting of 64 TAP heads then 64 LN heads,
it reaches its positive bound after 32 TAP heads and ends negative because
clipping discarded debt. A genuine STYX source supplies a feasible TAP/LN
choice where this bias changes odds by exp(2) despite a correct whole ratio.
This is a semantic mismatch, not proof it caused every fragmented LN.

In 36 matched complete outputs, disabling feedback preserved all eighteen
paired H streams but often made amount worse: actor STYX rose from about .50
to .94, and memory Classic fell from about .21 to .07. Inspected organization
did not improve convincingly across two songs. Deletion alone was rejected.
The [feedback ablation](/Users/l/projects/ensomi-model/docs/research/ln_feedback_scope_ablation.md)
at `24061b9` preserves bias and stabilization together.

The suggested five-H-at-40-ms counterexample was already excluded by HH60;
it did not demonstrate an actual support bug. The broader support census did
find exclusion of at least one ranked relationship in 558/8,774 charts (6.36%),
including 281/2,047 in the 4–5★ band (13.73%). Hard support therefore changed
what could be learned. [Recovery coverage](/Users/l/projects/ensomi-model/docs/research/joint_action_spacing.md).
Strict H preference isolation was documented as a revisable modeling restriction,
not a theorem of the V3 formulation.

`226725c` implemented an executable native qualification runner. Its first
amount gates mistakenly treated interrupted/restored fragments as newly
completed LN-total requests. A separate corrected assessment preserved the
original reports and removed that invalid interpretation; every arm still
failed. Control values resuming does not create a new total extent. This
correction is essential when comparing later control experiments.

## Human feedback changed the diagnostic question

The human prioritized the origin of irregular LN fragmentation and sustained
jacks, with explicit attention to ordinary approximately-four-star organization.
High-coverage LN streams remained legitimate. The requested decomposition was
whether proposal learning covers genuine arrangements and whether continuation/
frontier can reject inappropriate actual futures. A later instruction requested
a self-contained formal diagnosis before trying to solve every issue.

[proposal/selection](private/human-inputs/01a0ebe8-d368-7ba3-9d56-48da0524c5b6.md#m0805)

The [formal failure analysis](/Users/l/projects/ensomi-model/docs/research/native_pattern_failure_analysis_zh.md)
began at `9f5ed1d` and was deepened through `2653580`. Its strongest distinctions
are target distribution versus player response, legal support versus good
continuation, observation availability versus use, and factual likelihood
versus the states visited by a learned policy.

Concrete evidence prevents an overly simple explanation:

- Most inspected short tails were not forced by a deadline. Memory Classic
  had 298 of 310 <200-ms tails at H, with zero deadline hits; Blizzard had
  917 of 1,060 at H, with one deadline hit. Almost every inspected H closure
  had a supported keep-holding alternative with the same head signature.
- Source-H substitution greatly regularized memory Classic's millisecond LN
  relationships, yet median H span remained one versus source two. Timing and
  persistence both matter; a source is one alternative, not a mandatory target.
- Six reached prefixes supplied 192 actual futures. In five LN groups,
  159/160 had zero sixteen-second attack excess; each group's first four had
  zero four-second cost, so the existing planner stopped at candidate zero.
  The score could not select among most LN organizations because releases and
  holding were absent from its objective, not because no variation existed.
- Equal H time, controls, occupancy and complete-row support did not imply
  equal neural LN preference across histories. Large differences remained
  despite feedback pushing in the opposite direction. This does not isolate
  learned memory from changed factual clocks or prove a dynamical attractor.

## What the next refits ruled out

`1434924` exposed actual requested LN fraction inside the type-count readout
while retaining its analytic tilt. Two count-only 128-update refits improved
validation by about 3.75%, but their advantage differed by only .000088
nats/row and both regressed native controls. Fixed-history conditional-layout
KL remained approximately zero by construction: count-only learning cannot
directly repair release identities or columns within a count family.
[Matched count study](/Users/l/projects/ensomi-model/docs/research/contextual_ln_count_learning.md).

`0882315` enabled factual missing-history views rather than fabricated alternate
prefixes. Broader full-R1 learning, scope accounting and release-support work
followed because the actual decision owners and accepted data distribution
needed investigation. [Those comparisons](broader-proposal-learning.md) retain
real routing gains alongside lost amount control, instead of reducing this
phase to either complete success or complete failure.

No general solution was established by disabling feedback, changing one LN
input, increasing candidate count or demanding longer holds. These results
also do not validate unconditional deletion of timing/history paths. They
require better proposal learning and a response definition capable of seeing
the consequences under dispute.
