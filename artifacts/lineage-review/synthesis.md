# Lineage review: synthesis

Shareable; no human wording. Written 2026-09-30 by the main relay session `2a66b88f` (Claude, control plane). Second version, the same day: redone after an audit of the first version and two more independent reviews. What changed and why is in [section 9](#revision-2). Setup, briefs and limits of the runs: [README](README.md#state). Nothing here is a decision; [section 5](#for-the-human) lists what only the human can settle.

Inputs: nine Astra slices ([astra/](astra/)), eight independent Opus reviews ([opus/](opus/): the six of the first round, plus [pre-v3-history](opus/pre-v3-history.md) and [controls-ln](opus/controls-ln.md)), and one audit of the first synthesis against sources ([fable/synthesis-audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md)). Every Astra slice now has an independent counterpart.

How strength is stated:

- **replicated**: two or more reviewers computed it independently from code or raw artifacts.
- **single**: one reviewer checked it against code or artifacts.
- **doc**: read in a lineage document only.
- **audited**: the auditor recomputed or re-read it independently of the reviewers.

Most experiments behind these findings are small: 3 to 16 songs, one training seed, one to three sampling seeds. A pooled number can hide one song; where the audit broke a number down by song, the breakdown is given.

## 1. What the review establishes

<a id="s-r1-interface"></a>**Finding, replicated and audited, 2026-09-30. R1 fills lanes into a mapper's rhythm; it is not a row model that lacks only times.** The supplied set R is the union of the source chart's head times and its release-only times; H marks the head rows. R1 also reads the next 16 candidates with their roles, and counts of R and H up to 64 s ahead (`features.py`). Rhythm, the number of head times, rests and the moments where long notes may end therefore come from the real chart. What stays free is substantial: lanes, chord size, TAP or LN, which hold closes where (about 73 legal rows of 256 at a head row, n = 82,021). Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F1, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q1 and Q2, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) 4a.

<a id="s-release-sensitivity"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. R1's LN behaviour follows the release candidates it is offered, and the lineage measured this on its first afternoon.** Adding release-only candidates to frozen R1 moved mean LN share from 0.21 to 0.46 to 0.86 and median LN length from 230 to 164 to 82 ms (72 charts). Release-only times are close to arbitrary between human mappers of the same audio (F1 at 20 ms about 0.09 over 584 pairs, against 0.79 for heads). Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F2, [astra/03](astra/03-audio-timing-skeleton.md) attempts, first row.

<a id="s-r1-quality"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. With real times the restored R1 shows a milder form of complaints A and B, and no human judgment of its quality is on record.** On 16 validation outputs (8 sources, 2 seeds): LN under 100 ms rise from 19% to 32% of LN, one 3.2 star source goes from 0.7% to about 48% short LN, stars drift up to +1.4, same-lane head gaps under 60 ms appear (0 to 66 pairs); long same-lane runs are not longer than in the sources. The three correction residuals were trained on three hand-written rules harvested from 80, 72 and 92 queries, checked by agent inspection. R1 is a one-seed fit of about 0.6 passes in under an hour of CPU. The human saw six reconstructions on 09-21 and asked to publish; whether that was a quality judgment is [H7](#h7). Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F3 to F5 and F7, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q3, Q4, Q7.

<a id="s-lineage-r1-is-not-r1"></a>**Finding, replicated and audited, 2026-09-30. After about three hours the lineage no longer used R1.** The joint model copied 2.44M of R1's parameters and dropped the seed residual, the landmark memory, `frontier2` and the look-ahead input columns; it generated without a real seed and was first trained on 121 charts. Later statements that "R1 prefers" something concern this descendant. Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F8, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q9, [astra/08](astra/08-codex-interpretation.md) checked evidence A.

<a id="s-likelihood-vs-rollout"></a>**Finding, audited, small sample, 2026-09-30. Capacity and exposure did not remove the free-running collapse that R1's rule-trained residuals remove.** The 35M teacher (R1 with seed and memory, no correction residuals, one seed) reaches 1.61 validation NLL at 20M exposures against 1.74 for released R1, then 1.63 at 30M, past its validation minimum. On 16 fixed validation cases it has no fixed-lane run of 30 head rows or more at 5M and 10M, one at 20M (177) and three at 30M (354, 85, 65). Small R1 at the same module stage, before its corrections, has three (77, 39, 37) and two (49, 31); the residuals bring the maximum to 10. The first synthesis said "better likelihood gave worse charts"; the data do not show that. No lineage document reports the teacher's results. Sources: [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C1 and S1, [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F6, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q5 and Q6.

<a id="s-undertrained"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. No audio model in the lineage was trained enough to judge its architecture, and scale was never a controlled variable.** The largest audio fit used 9% of one pass over the ranked corpus; the three-arm clean joint comparison used 6.2% (4,096 updates at batch 2, 3,736 charts; its fresh arm was still improving in likelihood, grid alignment and head F1 when it stopped); every other late run was 32 to 512 updates. The three arms are one architecture with three initialisations. Every logged gradient norm in those arms exceeded the clip, so budget used is not the same as optimised. The joint models trained about 30 times slower than R1; the cause was not profiled. What varied without control points both ways: more data at a matched budget improved held-out likelihood and not rollout; the largest fit got worse on its native panel between 2,400 and 6,000 updates (doc, 40 cases, one seed); a broader row fit cut same-column runs on one panel. Several small-pilot findings are structural and hold at any scale (a deleted consequence path, a one-state code, all-TAP outcome targets, head plans that make a low star request impossible). The same habit is older than the lineage: the pre-V3 mapper was judged at half a pass, the June event-group model at 8 to 500 updates. Sources: [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) F1, F2, F9, [astra/05](astra/05-proposal-training-recipe.md) Q2 to Q4 and Q7, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C6 and O2, [opus/pre-v3-history](opus/pre-v3-history.md) section 8.

<a id="s-not-ordinary"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The training measure was never the ordinary distribution, and "ordinary first" was never run.** From 09-25 the corpus was ranked 2 to 6 star (6,923 charts), but every joint recipe after that drew a quarter of its windows from 112 annotated style charts, and the clean joint recipe another quarter from star-by-LN balanced cells; high-LN charts were sampled at 13% to 34% against 5% to 6% naturally. R1's own corpus is 60% to 65% ranked 2 to 6 star, about 20% ranked below 2 star, about 11% loved. The only single-band attempt, the fresh ordinary expert, saw 64 seconds of chart from four songs for 512 updates, did not fit even those, and ran with a support that allowed 1 ms long notes. The data for the real experiment exists: about 1,970 ranked 3.5 to 4.5 star charts, already split by song. Sources: [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) F3 to F5, F7, [astra/05](astra/05-proposal-training-recipe.md) Q1, Q6, Q8.

<a id="s-ranked-contains-rejected"></a>**Finding, replicated across five reports, 2026-09-30. Ranked charts contain things the human rejected in generated charts.** A ranked 4.07 star chart has a 28-attack anchor in about 4 s; a source window of the panel has eleven LN of 80 ms or less; two of the five panel sources have a median LN of one quarter beat; in the ranked 3.5 to 4.5 band 10% of LN last 82 ms or less; 75% of ranked LN end at the next head with no head inside. So neither "short LN" nor "one column used a lot" separates good from bad by itself, and "ordinary" is not the same as membership in the ranked corpus. This is [H11](#h11). Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) section 12, [opus/evaluation](opus/evaluation.md) section 7, [astra/06](astra/06-controls-ln.md) Q6, [opus/controls-ln](opus/controls-ln.md) section 6, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) O3.

<a id="s-no-time-coordinate"></a>**Finding, replicated and audited, 2026-09-30. The lineage had no musical time coordinate, and its head times are far from the song's grid; the same lesson had been recorded before V3.** No timing point, beat or subdivision enters inputs, outputs, losses or export after the first pilot; H is a per-millisecond hazard with a zero-tolerance loss and no peak suppression. In ranked 3.5 to 4.5 star charts 99% have at least 90% of heads within 2 ms of a 1/4, 1/6 or 1/8 subdivision of their own timing points (n = 141). Pooled, 16% of generated heads in the clean joint arms are within 2 ms, the chance rate; the pool is 71% one 210 BPM song, and on the other four songs the share is 1.2 to 2.1 times chance (at most 28% against 13.5%). The fresh arm's alignment was still rising at 4,096 updates. Head F1 at 20 ms against the source is 0.55 to 0.83, against 0.89 to 0.96 between human charts of one song. Before V3 the project had recorded that millisecond time tokens drift off grid and that times should move to beat positions; a beat-relative converter was built in June and never trained on; the pre-V3 mapper, given a nearly correct grid as input, put 11% of its heads on it (one song). The lineage then chose integer milliseconds, citing expressivity and the README boundary; the first expert reply advised against a fixed grid for the first round; the formulation allows internal beat coordinates. Whether this is a cause of the bad charts is [d-grid-cause](#d-grid-cause). Sources: [opus/system-outside-view](opus/system-outside-view.md) 2.1 to 2.5, [astra/03](astra/03-audio-timing-skeleton.md) Q2, Q4, Q7, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C3 and S2, [opus/pre-v3-history](opus/pre-v3-history.md) sections 3 and 8.

<a id="s-ln-open-state"></a>**Finding, replicated and audited, 2026-09-30; replaces the first version's "signature of the representation". What separates generated long notes from ranked ones is not that they end at the next head.** Ending at the next head is ordinary: 75% of ranked LN do it, and medians of LN length and of tails on head times look alike (1,766 ranked against 298 generated charts). Counted in beats, holds of a quarter beat or less are as common in the five panel sources (32% pooled, 0 to 71% by song) as in the final outputs (26% to 40%); the pooled "30% to 40% at 80 ms" was mostly one 210 BPM song. Three things do separate them:

- releases landing 40 ms or less before another head: 6.9% of generated LN against 1.0% of ranked. 61% of these are release-only events, which are three times over-represented, and the rest sit before near-duplicate heads. Both come from release and head hazards sampled separately on a millisecond clock. The lineage's release checks were same-column and never saw the cross-column case.
- holds of 40 ms or less: 4% to 5% of LN in the final outputs against none in their sources.
- too many LN on too dense heads. Without the inference-time amount controller the learned models' LN share runs to near 0 or above 0.9. In the chart behind the 09-28 attribution, 91% of heads are LN at 69 ms spacing in a 6.3 star output for a 4 star request; 97.6% of its short tails end at the very next head. The attribution's flag "could have kept the hold" is true in every case, so it tested nothing, and that chart is a one-seed outlier.

No model attaches a duration at LN birth; that alternative was named on day 1 and never tried. Mapperatorinator also represents a hold as start and end events. Real head times on a late checkpoint did not reduce short holds (three cases). Sources: [opus/controls-ln](opus/controls-ln.md) sections 4 to 6, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C2, S2, S3, [astra/06](astra/06-controls-ln.md) Q4 to Q6, [opus/interpretation](opus/interpretation.md) section 6.

<a id="s-response-not-in-loop"></a>**Finding, replicated and audited, 2026-09-30. No time-horizon response scorer or planner was in the system the human played or that qualification scored; a per-gap rarity preference was.** The demo server and the qualification runner build a plain session; the planner is a study-script path. That plain session applies by default a corpus-calibrated penalty on single gaps below the ranked first percentile and on releasing young holds, so a corpus-rarity response on single gaps was in the loop for every late result and the failures occurred under it. The played checkpoint `a99519c` predates the first player state and planner by two hours, and had just been tuned for 128 updates on 47 fixed prefixes toward a star scalar, with all-TAP targets. Where a time-horizon scorer was asked, better candidates existed: four resamples on fixed H cut the attack cost by 98% on nine outputs. Training the response into the policy did not transfer. Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 1, 4, 5, [astra/04](astra/04-player-response-frontier.md) Q6, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C5 and S4.

<a id="s-response-blind"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The scorers catch extremes and are blind to how often and how long.** Their references are 99th percentiles of each chart's peak inside official star bands. The final "work" scorer accepts both reproduced playtest windows (31 and 28 attacks on one column in 4 s) and a 25 ms LN at zero cost, including in a real planner run. The response state mostly re-encodes the official star rating (R² 0.965; single), and that rating scores a 16 s single-column 12 Hz run at 4.14 stars. The 60/50/50 support, added after a complaint, excludes real relations in 13.7% of ranked 4 to 5 star charts. Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 5, 6, 8, [astra/04](astra/04-player-response-frontier.md) Q4 to Q6, [astra/07](astra/07-evaluation.md) Q6.

<a id="s-response-undefined"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The response target was never defined, and nothing was validated against a human judgment.** The formulation leaves its quantities and comparison rules to mapper evidence; the lineage did not touch the formulation and built at least five response objects on corpus-rarity proxies. A prepared annotation release exists (545 sources, 592 human records) but it holds style observations, not response comparisons. The persistent event-time state machinery matches the formulation's interfaces; its semantics are what is missing. Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 3, 4, 9, [astra/04](astra/04-player-response-frontier.md) Q1, Q3, Q7.

<a id="s-eval-sensitivity"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The evaluators have a false-alarm rate on ranked charts, measured mostly in-sample, and no measured sensitivity to what the human rejects.** Run on 200 ranked 3.5 to 4.5 star charts, all eleven lineage evaluators together flag 5 (1 of the 42 held out). On the 12 charts behind the one rejection that can be tied to files (V68), the gates in force passed 7 outright and failed the other 5 only on LN amount, and the final battery still passes 5; on 19 charts of the same experiment a second reviewer finds 0 flagged by the 20 ms and 80 ms checks. Gates measured whole charts while the human judged passages: four charts of one song met their whole-song LN ratio by putting 27 to 47 LN into a passage that is all TAP in the source. Each evaluator was added after a complaint and tuned on its charts; the development panel was five TRAIN songs reused across 11 to 40 experiment directories by name, 13 to 16 by plan; per-chart seed noise is about 0.2 star against promotion margins of 0.1, and in the one case where 16 fresh seeds were drawn a single-pair effect of -0.35 shrank to -0.04; the agent's own reading of lens pages served as an evaluator and was more lenient than the human. Solid: the star calculator matches official values within 5e-6 on 6,924 charts. Sources: [opus/evaluation](opus/evaluation.md) F1 to F8, [astra/07](astra/07-evaluation.md) Q3, Q4, Q7.

<a id="s-local-search"></a>**Finding, replicated in direction, 2026-09-30. The search was mostly local, and upstream moves mostly followed the human.** Both reviewers find repeated repair at the module nearest the symptom, problems A and B outliving dozens of repairs, day-1 alternatives never tried, and correct diagnoses recognised and not acted on. They name different sharper causes: one reads under-training as the common cause (every failure at pilot scale was read as a missing mechanism), the other a gap between recognising a question and settling it before the next step. Against the hypothesis: the repository's triage skill tells the agent to prefer fail-fast slices and leave direction to the human, several upstream choices were the human's, the agent never promoted a failed model, and many diagnoses were sound. Counts: [d-locality-counts](#d-locality-counts). Sources: [opus/interpretation](opus/interpretation.md) sections 4, 7, 8, [astra/08](astra/08-codex-interpretation.md) Q2, Q3, Q9, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C4, C9, 3a.

<a id="s-controls-order"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. Of the controls, only LN amount ever followed a request, and only through a hand-coded controller at inference; difficulty and style never worked on free-running output.**

- LN amount: with the inference-time feedback controller on, 9 of 11 and 11 of 11 ranges are within 0.10 of the request; off, 5 of 11 and 2 of 11 (same weights and seeds). The 09-28 architectures could not carry the controller, which is where LN control vanished. The late "25% to 30% whatever was requested" is six charts from a 32-update pilot, and the "four-state controller" was a latent plan that had not differentiated after 32 updates.
- Difficulty: over 674 free-running charts realised stars follow the request with slope 0.48 and run 0.57 star too hard; 2 star requests give a median 3.47. Training labels are whole-chart stars; a 16 s passage sits a median 0.54 star below its chart's label, and no ranked chart switches difficulty mid-song, so a scoped or switched request has no training example.
- Style: 3 to 20 prominent cells per concept, and a classifier built before the lineage on the same cells could not recognise two of the five concepts. No readout exists to check a style request against.
- There was no fixed regression suite until 09-27, and its criteria changed per attempt.

Order of work, one reviewer's call at about 70%: a static chart-level difficulty request from the start was reasonable; scoped and mid-song controls, style and the amount controller were premature. The human asked for the controls on 09-25. Sources: [opus/controls-ln](opus/controls-ln.md) sections 2, 3, 7, 8, [astra/06](astra/06-controls-ln.md) Q1 to Q3, Q7, Q8.

<a id="s-outside"></a>**Finding, replicated and audited, 2026-09-30. Reference generators differ from the lineage in scale, in a time grid at output, and in output repair; their quality on these songs is unknown.** Mapperatorinator is about 219M parameters, generates timing points first and snaps to them, and holds an LN as start and end tokens; Mug-Diffusion is about 157M by configuration, fits a grid afterwards, holds an LN as a body channel, and by default rearranges same-lane notes closer than 90 ms. Both condition on whole-chart labels. Neither has a controlled 4K evaluation on record and neither was run here. Neither supports committed-prefix publication, scoped live control or exact replay, which is the fair case for ensomi's different design. The official star rating already keeps a per-column strain with decay; the player response state is new only as a calibrated per-candidate acceptor over continuations, which does not exist yet. Sources: [opus/system-outside-view](opus/system-outside-view.md) sections 4 to 7, [astra/09](astra/09-reference-projects.md) Q1 to Q6, [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C8.

<a id="s-pre-v3"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The pre-V3 system made charts from audio, badly, at half a pass of training; its timing stack was set aside without a recorded reason and is better than its headline error.**

- The mapper (30M parameters with its control encoder, 0.51 passes at batch 2, loss still falling) went end to end. Two outputs survive: one puts 11% of its heads on the song's grid and ignores the nearly correct grid it was given; the other is one chord every 100 ms for 47 s. Two songs, one checkpoint, no human judgment on record.
- The timing stack (BeatThis features and a grid fitter) has a median phase error of 43 ms over 5,026 songs. Most of it is one constant: the fitted grid lags the chart by a median 29 ms, the same for OGG and MP3. After subtracting it, 53% of songs start within 10 ms of the chart grid; tempo is within 0.1 BPM on 73% of single-tempo songs and mostly wrong on songs with several sections (16% of audio). No document mentions the offset. Fitted grids for 5,050 songs survive.
- The README boundary is worded as a statement of authority and contract, not quality. Quality reasons are on record for the mapper and the control targets, none for the timing stack. A pushed, unmerged branch `cleanup/main-r1` would move the legacy code out of `main` and drop the BeatThis dependency.
- An audio-information probe (three seeds, 148 test songs) found that audio adds 0.90 nats per choice over chart history for where the next row goes, with onset novelty carrying the gain over plain Mel. It was never written up or used.
- Part of the pre-V3 record was also written by agent loops.

Sources: [opus/pre-v3-history](opus/pre-v3-history.md) sections 2 to 8, [astra/02](astra/02-pre-v3-history.md) Q2, Q4, Q5, Q7.

## 2. Where reviewers disagree, or a finding is weaker than it sounds

<a id="d-locality-counts"></a>**Disagreement, narrowed by the audit, not settled.** Put on one denominator, both codings say upstream moves mostly followed a human or expert message (12 of 13 in one; 10 to 12 of 19 in the other) and local moves were mostly the agent's (about 25 of 27; 10 of 14). The ratio of local to upstream (27 to 13 against 14 to 19) depends on whether the diagnosis or the action is coded. Neither count can be reproduced from the record. The audit timed eight structural changes that held: all were committed inside or within 80 minutes after a human message window; under a strict reading the agent started two upstream reconsiderations on its own in six days. What would settle it: one episode list, a written codebook, two coders working blind, exact message times. Source: [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) C4 and 3a.

<a id="d-grid-cause"></a>**Open, narrowed.** The missing time coordinate is a measured fact. As a cause:

- of short LN in the fresh expert: yes, through near-duplicate heads (real head times remove 85% of holds of 40 ms or less), with representation and under-training confounded in that model;
- of short holds in the late inherited line: the one saved substitution says no (same checkpoint and seed with the source's head times: no fewer short holds; three cases);
- of releases just before another head: plausibly, since release-only events on a free millisecond clock carry most of them ([s-ln-open-state](#s-ln-open-state)); not tested by substitution;
- of long jacks: no evidence that timing matters;
- of "irregular subdivision": the measurement restates the complaint; no human judgment separates a 5 to 20 ms scatter from wrong rhythm.

As a symptom of under-training: partly; the fresh arm was still converging toward the grid, the inherited arm was flat at a level that looks like a ceiling of the recipe, and a recipe ceiling is not separated from a representation ceiling. The two earlier facts stand with a correction: the old grid fitter is mostly a constant 29 ms off, and the old mapper was off its own grid while writing times in 10 ms tokens. What would settle the first part without training: substitute source head times on the three final clean joint checkpoints over the 28 cases. The second part needs training (head times alone, with and without a beat-phase input, at three budgets). Sources: [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md) 3b and S3, [opus/pre-v3-history](opus/pre-v3-history.md) section 5.

<a id="d-scale-or-objective"></a>**Open.** Whether more data and training alone would have produced ordinary charts. On the audio side the evidence is thin and mixed ([s-undertrained](#s-undertrained)). On the row side, capacity and exposure did not remove the uncorrected drift ([s-likelihood-vs-rollout](#s-likelihood-vs-rollout)); one seed, 16 charts.

<a id="d-key1-vs-key2"></a>**Tension, for the human; reworded after the audit.** Every response quantity the lineage built is corpus rarity inside star bands. If the ranked distribution is both the proposal target and the response reference, key 1 has no target of its own. It would still act as a corrector of free-running drift: a selector with a corpus-rarity cost cut that cost by 98% on an unchanged proposal. Key 1 has its own target only if the response is defined from something else: mapper or human judgments, or play data.

<a id="d-astra-tone"></a>**Withdrawn, 2026-09-30.** The first version told the reader to discount the Astra reports' direction calls because Astra was reviewing its own family's work. The audit checked four places where the first synthesis took an Opus wording over an Astra qualification (the teacher's confounds, chance coverage on the grid, coding by trigger, how the reference projects represent an LN); in all four the Astra qualification was right. The Opus reviews contributed more new measurements; the Astra reports were better calibrated about what a measurement shows. Source: [audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md), "A pattern across C1, C3, C4, C8 and C9".

## 3. The two leads

<a id="v-pilot-scale"></a>**Verdict on [h-scale-and-grid](README.md#h-scale-and-grid) (a), 2026-09-30: confirmed, with a correction.** The numbers check out against logs. The late conclusions cannot discriminate between architectures. The documents mostly hedged; the error is in what was done next, and in these notes, where the key-2 falsifier cited the four-song expert as trained on the corpus (corrected in the feedback index).

## 4. The three keys after the review

Agent reading, proposed.

- **Key 2, ordinary first.** Supported as the right data and recipe decision, and never executed. Not sufficient alone: more fitting and capacity did not remove the uncorrected drift on the row half. It needs a target: "ordinary" is not corpus membership ([H11](#h11), [H1](#h1)).
- **Key 1, player response state.** The machinery exists; the target does not. What ran in the played system was a per-gap rarity preference, and the failures occurred under it. The first question is what the response must distinguish, from contrast cases judged by a person ([H2](#h2), [H3](#h3)). Two long-jack failures were failures of integration and definition, with better candidates available; whether better candidates existed for the LN failures is not known.
- **Key 3, evaluation.** What was missing is concrete: sensitivity on charts the human rejected, checks at the unit the human judges (a passage), a held-out failure set, seed and song variance before promotion margins, and a baseline row (R1 and ranked charts) in every table. All of it depends on human judgments kept as files ([H3](#h3)).

Beside the keys, as untested alternatives the review names and does not endorse: a musical time coordinate for event times ([d-grid-cause](#d-grid-cause)), and an LN with a duration at birth ([H5](#h5)). And one ordering question the review raises: which controls the first system must have ([H12](#h12)).

<a id="for-the-human"></a>
## 5. Items that need the human

Each is a question the reviewers could not settle from evidence. Options are the reviewers' or mine and carry no recommendation unless stated.

<a id="h11"></a>**H11. Is the standard the ranked corpus, or stricter?** Would the human accept as ordinary the ranked source charts of the panel (two with a median LN of one quarter beat; one window with eleven LN of 80 ms or less) and the ranked 4.07 star chart with a 28-attack anchor? If not, the key-2 target is not the ranked distribution and every corpus-quantile reference is aimed at the wrong thing. Asked first because H1 to H3 depend on it. Source: [s-ranked-contains-rejected](#s-ranked-contains-rejected).

<a id="h1"></a>**H1. What "ordinary" means, and where it applies first.** A star band only, or also ranked only, no loved maps, no LN-majority or tech charts, no annotated style charts? And first to the row half with real times (cheap: hours of CPU on about 1,600 charts), or to the whole audio-to-chart system? The two halves fail differently.

<a id="h2"></a>**H2. <a id="revision-2"></a>
## 9. What changed from the first version, and why

The first version (notes commit `b9194d9`) was audited against the fifteen reports, the code and raw artifacts ([fable/synthesis-audit](fable/synthesis-audit.md)). Its transcriptions mostly held; it was weakest where it named causes. Changes made here:

- **Teacher.** "Better likelihood gave worse charts" withdrawn. It compared the teacher with released R1, which has correction residuals the teacher lacks, and joined two checkpoints ([s-likelihood-vs-rollout](#s-likelihood-vs-rollout)).
- **Short LN.** "The signature of the open-state representation" withdrawn. The pooled 80 ms share was mostly one fast song, quarter-beat holds are as common in the sources, and ending at the next head is ordinary ranked practice. Replaced by what the ranked comparison does show ([s-ln-open-state](#s-ln-open-state)).
- **Grid.** "At chance" now carries its per-song breakdown and the training trend; the pre-V3 record of the same lesson is added; the grid as a cause is narrowed by one saved substitution ([d-grid-cause](#d-grid-cause)).
- **Response in the loop.** A per-gap rarity preference was in the played system; the title said none was.
- **Scale.** "Never a variable" is now "never a controlled variable", with the uncontrolled cases in both directions.
- **Reference projects.** "LN as one object" was wrong for Mapperatorinator; "working" was unestablished.
- **Locality.** The two counts were on different denominators; the weight given to the Opus count is withdrawn.
- **Astra.** The instruction to discount Astra's direction calls is withdrawn ([d-astra-tone](#d-astra-tone)).
- **Controls and pre-V3.** Both now rest on two reviewers, with new measurements: the amount controller, the difficulty response over 674 charts, the attribution's empty flag, the 29 ms timing offset, the pre-V3 generated charts.
- **Questions.** H3, H4 and H5 reworded to be neutral; H11 and H12 added; budget folded into H9; the legacy branch added to H6; eight small transcription or scope errors fixed.

Pattern worth remembering: in five places the first version took the stronger wording of one reviewer over a qualification by another, and the qualification was right in the four that could be checked.
