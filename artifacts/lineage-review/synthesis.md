# Lineage review: synthesis

Shareable; no human wording. Written 2026-09-30 by the main relay session `2a66b88f` (Claude, control plane) after all fifteen reviewers had returned: nine Astra slices ([astra/](astra/)) and six independent Opus reviews ([opus/](opus/)). Setup, briefs and limits of the run: [README](README.md#state). Everything here is the main thread's reading of those reports. Nothing here is a decision; [section 5](#for-the-human) lists what only the human can settle.

How strength is stated:

- **replicated**: two or more reviewers computed it independently from code or raw artifacts (an Opus reviewer never saw the Astra reports).
- **single**: one reviewer checked it against code or artifacts.
- **doc**: read in a lineage document only.
- **main-thread check**: I re-ran a small check myself on the exported tree of `audio-joint-2026-09`.

Numbers are the reviewers'; I re-derived none except the five spot checks named in [section 8](#limits).

## 1. What the review establishes

<a id="s-r1-interface"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. R1 fills lanes into a mapper's rhythm; it is not a row model that lacks only times.** The supplied set R is the union of the source chart's head times and its release-only times; H marks the head rows. R1 also reads the next 16 candidates with their roles, and counts of R and H up to 64 s ahead (`features.py`). Head rhythm, density, rests and where long notes may end therefore come from the real chart. What stays free is substantial: lanes, chord size, TAP or LN, which hold closes where (about 71 legal rows of 256 on average at a head row, n = 85,176 states). Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F1, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q1 and Q2. The two reviewers weight this differently ("inputs carry the LN tail moments" against "most row content remains free"); both statements hold.

<a id="s-release-sensitivity"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. R1's LN behaviour follows the release candidates it is offered, and the lineage measured this on its first afternoon.** Adding release-only candidates to frozen R1 moved mean LN share from 0.21 to 0.46 to 0.86 and median LN length from 230 to 164 to 82 ms (72 charts, recomputed). Release-only times are close to arbitrary between human mappers of the same audio (F1 at 20 ms about 0.09 over 584 pairs, against 0.79 for heads). The lineage read this as a reason to learn sparse release opportunities, not as evidence against feeding a timing module's release moments into a row model. Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F2, [astra/03](astra/03-audio-timing-skeleton.md) attempts, first row.

<a id="s-r1-quality"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. With real times the restored R1 already shows a milder form of complaints A and B, and its quality was never judged by anyone but the agent.** On 16 validation outputs (8 sources, 2 seeds): LN under 100 ms rise from 19% to 32% of LN, one 3.2 star source goes from 0.7% to about 48% short LN, stars drift up to +1.4, same-lane head gaps under 60 ms appear (0 to 66 pairs); long same-lane runs are not longer than in the sources. The three correction residuals were trained on three hand-written rules harvested from 80, 72 and 92 queries, checked by agent inspection. R1 is a one-seed fit of about 0.6 passes over its corpus in under an hour of CPU. The release manifest itself says quality review is still required. Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F3 to F5 and F7, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q3, Q4, Q7, [opus/evaluation](opus/evaluation.md) F9.

<a id="s-lineage-r1-is-not-r1"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. After about three hours the lineage no longer used R1.** The joint model copied 2.44M of R1's parameters and dropped the seed residual, the landmark memory, `frontier2` and the look-ahead input columns; it generated without a real seed and was first trained on 121 charts. Every later statement that "R1 prefers" something concerns this descendant. The lineage's own audit of 09-24 says so. Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F8, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q9, [astra/08](astra/08-codex-interpretation.md) checked evidence A.

<a id="s-likelihood-vs-rollout"></a>**Finding, replicated by three reviewers, small sample, 2026-09-30. Better likelihood did not give better free-running charts.** The finished 35M-parameter teacher (30M exposures, 23.7 h CPU) has the best held-out likelihood in the project (1.61 to 1.63 against R1's 1.74) and the worst generation on the same 16 cases: same-lane runs of 177 and 354 head rows, star bias +1.1. It has no correction residuals, one training seed, 16 charts. No lineage document reports these results (main-thread check: the teacher appears only in the two baseline documents), although the human asked about the run on 09-23. This is the one place scale was tested, and it is on the row half with real times. Sources: [opus/r1-foundation](opus/r1-foundation.md) F6, [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) F8, [astra/01](astra/01-r1-foundation.md) Q5.

<a id="s-undertrained"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. No audio model in the lineage was trained enough to judge its architecture, and scale was never a variable.** The largest audio fit used 9% of one pass over the ranked corpus; the three-arm clean joint comparison used 6.2% (4,096 updates at batch 2, 3,736 charts; its fresh arm was still improving when it stopped); every other late run was 32 to 512 updates. The three arms are one architecture with three initialisations. The joint models trained about 30 times slower than R1 (full-song audio gradients at batch 2), which set the pilot sizes. The first expert reply said to keep growing data and training while the curve still improved; the one coverage run did still improve and was not continued. Several small-pilot findings are nevertheless structural and valid at any scale (a deleted consequence path, a one-state code, all-TAP outcome targets, H plans that make a low star request impossible). Sources: [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) F1, F2, F9, [astra/05](astra/05-proposal-training-recipe.md) Q2 to Q4, [opus/interpretation](opus/interpretation.md) sections 4 and 5, [astra/08](astra/08-codex-interpretation.md) Q5.

<a id="s-not-ordinary"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The training measure was never the ordinary distribution, and "ordinary first" was never run.** From 09-25 the corpus was ranked 2 to 6 star (6,923 charts), but every joint recipe drew a quarter of its windows from 112 annotated style charts and a quarter from star-by-LN balanced cells; high-LN charts were sampled at 13% to 34% against 5% to 6% naturally. R1's own corpus is 60% to 65% ranked 2 to 6 star, about 20% ranked below 2 star, about 11% loved. The only single-band attempt, the fresh ordinary expert, saw 64 seconds of chart from four songs for 512 updates, did not fit even those, and ran with a support that allowed 1 ms long notes (`Recovery(20,1,1)`, unbounded H; main-thread check). The data for the real experiment exists: about 1,970 ranked 3.5 to 4.5 star charts, already split by song. Sources: [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) F3 to F5, F7, [astra/05](astra/05-proposal-training-recipe.md) Q1, Q6, Q8.

<a id="s-no-time-coordinate"></a>**Finding, replicated in direction, 2026-09-30. The lineage had no musical time coordinate, and its head times are off the song's grid.** No timing point, beat or subdivision enters inputs, outputs, losses or export after the first pilot; H is a per-millisecond hazard with a zero-tolerance loss and no peak suppression. In ranked 3.5 to 4.5 star charts 99% have at least 90% of heads within 2 ms of a 1/4, 1/6 or 1/8 subdivision of their own timing points (n = 141). Generated heads of the late models sit at chance on that measure (16% to 17% against 16% by chance for the clean joint arms), and 5 to 20 ms off the events they follow (head F1 at 20 ms 0.55 to 0.83 against 0.89 to 0.96 between human charts of one song). At a 10 ms tolerance one reviewer finds 75% against 99% on four songs. The formulation allows internal beat coordinates; leaving them out was the lineage's choice, recorded as protecting off-grid expression, endorsed by the first expert reply, and never tested against the corpus. A slot-based rhythm design was written on 09-28 and not implemented. Sources: [opus/system-outside-view](opus/system-outside-view.md) 2.1 to 2.5, [astra/03](astra/03-audio-timing-skeleton.md) Q2, Q4, Q7.

<a id="s-ln-open-state"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. A long note is an open state that some later event closes, and the short-LN complaint has the signature of that representation.** No model attaches a duration at LN birth; the alternative was named on day 1 and never tried. In the final clean joint outputs 30% to 40% of all LN last 80 ms or less (13% in their sources, about 7% in the ranked band), and most of those end exactly at a head time. The lineage's replay attribution, reproduced by a reviewer: in one chart 2,524 of 2,670 short tails were releases chosen on head rows although keeping the hold was legal; in another 71 of 84 were pure-release moments. Near-duplicate head times explain the short LN only in the fresh expert. Sources: [opus/interpretation](opus/interpretation.md) section 6, [opus/system-outside-view](opus/system-outside-view.md) section 3, [astra/06](astra/06-controls-ln.md) Q4 and Q5.

<a id="s-response-not-in-loop"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. No response scorer was in the system the human played or that qualification scored.** The demo server and the qualification runner build a plain session; the planner is a study-script path (main-thread check: `ResponsePlanner` is referenced by no other module in `src`). The played checkpoint `a99519c` predates the first player state and planner by two hours, and had just been tuned for 128 updates on 47 fixed prefixes toward a star scalar, with all-TAP targets. Where a scorer was asked, better candidates existed: four resamples on fixed H cut the attack cost by 98% on nine outputs. Later fixes tried to train the response into the policy; that did not transfer. Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 1 and 5, [astra/04](astra/04-player-response-frontier.md) Q6, [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) F6.

<a id="s-response-blind"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The scorers catch extremes and are blind to how often and how long.** Their references are 99th percentiles of each chart's peak inside official star bands. The final "work" scorer accepts both reproduced playtest windows (31 and 28 attacks on one column in 4 s) and a 25 ms LN at zero cost, including in a real planner run. The response state mostly re-encodes the official star rating (its peaks predict stars with R² 0.965; single), and that rating scores a 16 s single-column 12 Hz run at 4.14 stars. The 60/50/50 support, added after a complaint, excludes real relations in 13.7% of ranked 4 to 5 star charts. Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 5, 6, 8, [astra/04](astra/04-player-response-frontier.md) Q4 to Q6, [astra/07](astra/07-evaluation.md) Q6.

<a id="s-response-undefined"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The response target was never defined, and nothing was validated against a human judgment.** The formulation leaves its quantities and comparison rules to mapper evidence; the lineage did not touch the formulation and built at least five response objects on corpus-rarity proxies. A prepared annotation release exists (545 sources, 592 human records) but it holds style observations, not response comparisons. The persistent event-time state machinery is careful work and matches the formulation's interfaces; its semantics are what is missing. Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 3, 4, 9, [astra/04](astra/04-player-response-frontier.md) Q1, Q3, Q7.

<a id="s-eval-sensitivity"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. The evaluators have a measured false-alarm rate on ranked charts and no measured sensitivity to what the human rejects.** Run on 200 ranked 3.5 to 4.5 star charts, all eleven lineage evaluators together flag 5. On the 12 charts behind the one rejection that can be tied to files (V68), the gates in force passed 7 outright and failed the other 5 only on LN amount, and the final battery still passes 5; on 19 charts of the same experiment a second reviewer finds 0 flagged by the 20 ms and 80 ms checks. Gates measured whole charts while the human judged passages: one chart met its whole-song LN ratio by putting 27 to 47 LN into a passage that is all TAP in the source. Each evaluator was added after a complaint and tuned on its charts; the development panel was five TRAIN songs reused across 13 to 40 experiment directories; per-chart seed noise is about 0.2 star against promotion margins of 0.1; the agent's own reading of lens pages served as an evaluator and was more lenient than the human. Solid: the star calculator matches official values within 5e-6 on 6,924 charts. Sources: [opus/evaluation](opus/evaluation.md) F1 to F8, [astra/07](astra/07-evaluation.md) Q3, Q4, Q7.

<a id="s-local-search"></a>**Finding, replicated in substance, counts disputed, 2026-09-30. The search was local and steered by the human; the sharper cause is scale.** Both reviewers find repeated repair at the module nearest the symptom, problems A and B outliving dozens of repairs, day-1 alternatives never tried, and correct diagnoses that were recognised and then not acted on (the response target, the still-improving data curve). The Opus reviewer's reading: every experiment ran at a scale where a failure could not be told from under-training, so every failure was read as a missing mechanism and answered with one. Against: the repository's own triage skill tells the agent to prefer fail-fast slices and leave direction to the human, several upstream choices were the human's, the agent never promoted a failed model, and many diagnoses were sound. The counts differ, see [d-locality-counts](#d-locality-counts). Sources: [opus/interpretation](opus/interpretation.md) sections 4, 7, 8, [astra/08](astra/08-codex-interpretation.md) Q2, Q3, Q9.

<a id="s-controls-order"></a>**Finding, single (Astra 06, no independent reviewer), 2026-09-30. Controls were built on a proposal that was not yet ordinary.** LN-amount feedback does work narrowly (8 of 8 cases within 0.10 of the request in one panel) and no style control was validated. Scalar agreement could be met while the arrangement was wrong. There was no fixed regression suite across the control work. Source: [astra/06](astra/06-controls-ln.md) Q3, Q7, Q8.

<a id="s-outside"></a>**Finding, replicated, 2026-09-30. Working generators differ from the lineage in scale, in having a time grid, in LN as one object, and in output repair; none was ever run here on the same songs.** Mapperatorinator is about 219M parameters, generates timing points first and snaps to them; Mug-Diffusion is about 157M by configuration, fits a grid afterwards, holds an LN as one channel, and by default rearranges same-lane notes closer than 90 ms. Both condition on whole-chart labels. Neither supports committed-prefix publication, scoped live control or exact replay, which is the fair case for ensomi's different design. The official star rating already keeps a per-column strain with decay; the player response state is new only as a calibrated per-candidate acceptor over continuations, which does not exist yet. No lineage document mentions either reference project. Sources: [opus/system-outside-view](opus/system-outside-view.md) sections 4 to 7, [astra/09](astra/09-reference-projects.md) Q1 to Q6.

<a id="s-pre-v3"></a>**Finding, single (Astra 02, no independent reviewer), 2026-09-30. The pre-V3 stack was set aside by contract, not by a comparison.** The README ban records that the old stack is not the V3 contract; no experiment shows each old component was beaten. The old mapper did produce charts from audio end to end, of poor quality, and also off grid (doc). The old grid fitter agrees with chart timing to about 43 to 47 ms of phase on 5,026 audios, which is not snap accuracy. A three-seed audio-information probe with a positive result was never carried forward. Source: [astra/02](astra/02-pre-v3-history.md) Q2, Q4, Q7.

## 2. Where reviewers disagree, or a finding is weaker than it sounds

<a id="d-locality-counts"></a>**Disagreement, open.** Opus counts about 27 local against 13 upstream decisions after a failure, with 12 of the 13 upstream moves following a human or expert message. Astra counts 14 local against 19 upstream over 33 episodes, with 20 triggered by the agent's own evidence. The definitions differ: Astra counts an upstream diagnosis even when the implementation stayed incremental. Astra is also judging its own family's work. Both arrive at "recognised, then not followed through". I give the Opus count more weight and hold the exact ratio as unsettled.

<a id="d-grid-cause"></a>**Disagreement, open.** Opus reads the missing time coordinate as a design defect that the real-time vision does not require. Astra 03 accepts the measurements and declines the inference: the evidence does not show that millisecond resolution is defective or that a grid would cure it, and an under-trained hazard is not separated from a defective representation. Two facts cut against an easy fix: the project's own grid fitter is tens of milliseconds off, and the old grid-conditioned mapper was off grid too. No experiment changed only the time representation.

<a id="d-scale-or-objective"></a>**Open question, not a disagreement.** Whether more data and training alone would have produced ordinary charts: no evidence either way on the audio side; on the row side the 35M teacher says likelihood training alone does not fix free-running behaviour, from one seed and 16 charts, with the correction residuals confounded.

<a id="d-key1-vs-key2"></a>**Tension, for the human.** Every response quantity the lineage built is corpus rarity inside star bands. If the ranked distribution is both the proposal target (key 2) and the response reference, key 1 adds nothing to key 2. Key 1 stays distinct only if the response is defined from something else: mapper or human judgments, or play data.

<a id="d-astra-tone"></a>**Note on the Astra reports.** They are careful and mostly agree with the Opus reviews on facts. Their recurring qualification is that the lineage documents "usually state this limit themselves". The Opus reviewers agree the documents hedge and find the problem in the decisions that followed. Read Astra's direction calls with that in mind; their inventories and recomputed numbers held up wherever an Opus reviewer recomputed the same thing.

## 4. The three keys after the review

Agent reading, proposed.

- **Key 2, ordinary first.** Supported as the right data and recipe decision, and never executed. Not sufficient alone: the row half drifts on its own histories even when well fitted ([s-likelihood-vs-rollout](#s-likelihood-vs-rollout)). It needs a definition of "ordinary" ([h1](#h1)).
- **Key 1, player response state.** The machinery exists; the target does not. The first question is what the response must distinguish, from contrast cases judged by a person, before any new scorer ([h2](#h2), [h3](#h3)). Two long-jack failures were failures of integration and definition, with better candidates available; the short-LN failure belongs to both keys.
- **Key 3, evaluation.** What was missing is concrete: sensitivity on charts the human rejected, checks at the unit the human judges (a passage), a held-out failure set, seed and song variance before promotion margins, and a baseline row (R1 and ranked charts) in every table. All of it depends on a set of human judgments kept as files ([h3](#h3)).

Two things the keys do not name and the review puts beside them: how time is represented ([s-no-time-coordinate](#s-no-time-coordinate)), and how a long note is represented ([s-ln-open-state](#s-ln-open-state)). The second touches a key the human dropped.

<a id="for-the-human"></a>
## 5. Items that need the human

Each is a question the reviewers could not settle from evidence. Options are the reviewers' or mine and carry no recommendation unless stated.

<a id="h1"></a>**H1. What "ordinary" means, and where it applies first.** A star band only, or also ranked only, no loved maps, no LN-majority or tech charts, no annotated style charts? And first to the row half with real times (cheap: hours of CPU on about 1,600 charts), or to the whole audio-to-chart system? The two halves fail differently. Sources: [opus/proposal-recipe](opus/proposal-recipe.md) section 8, [astra/05](astra/05-proposal-training-recipe.md) item 7.

<a id="h2"></a>**H2. Which response state key 1 means.** (a) The formulation's canonical chart-demand response, defined from mapper evidence, physiology excluded; or (b) a model of hand load calibrated on physiology and corpus, as asked on 09-26 and 09-28. They share machinery and differ in target, validation and what a difficulty control means. Related: is a difficulty request an official star value (then every reference inherits the star model's blind spots)? Is play data (replays, hit errors) in scope? See also [d-key1-vs-key2](#d-key1-vs-key2). Sources: [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) sections 7 and 12.

<a id="h3"></a>**H3. Whether a set of the human's own accept and reject judgments comes first.** The formulation waits for mapper evidence; the evaluators have no sensitivity measurement; the 84 messages were never turned into labels and the first playtest left no file. Option: a small labelled set of windows (the retained witnesses are listed in [opus/player-response](opus/player-response.md) section 11) before any new scorer or evaluator. Open sub-question: is a chart that meets its whole-song LN ratio by moving LN into a TAP passage a control success? The gates said yes.

<a id="h4"></a>**H4. Time coordinate.** Is off-grid expression required for the first target, or a later style? Does ambient play have the full track before generation (then a whole-song timing pass is inside the vision)? Did the choice of a simple, interpretable audio encoding mean to exclude beat evidence? Note [d-grid-cause](#d-grid-cause): no experiment isolates this.

<a id="h5"></a>**H5. Long-note representation.** Is an LN as one object with a duration compatible with the incremental release the protocol allows? This reopens evidence under the dropped key on hold-versus-release ownership; the human may prefer to leave it dropped.

<a id="h6"></a>**H6. An outside yardstick.** Should an existing generator run on the same songs as a baseline (Mapperatorinator supports 4K; weights are not on the mac)? Does the README ban on the pre-V3 stack also forbid using it as a controlled baseline or reusing its timing and audio-probe assets?

<a id="h7"></a>**H7. What R1 is taken to be.** Is the Position line "R1 answers the half that chooses rows at supplied times" still acceptable when the supplied times include the mapper's release moments? Was the 09-21 approval a judgment of quality or of the restoration procedure? What was reported to the human about the 35M teacher?

<a id="h8"></a>**H8. Rules.** "No hard-coded rules" against the ranked minima (same-lane head gap 37 ms, release-to-head 25, head-to-release 21): support, learned target, or evaluation only? The fresh expert's 1 ms settings are what the literal instruction produced. Was "under 20 ms" a threshold or an example of a kind of badness?

<a id="h9"></a>**H9. Method.** Did "small runs first" still hold after day 1, and did "large steps" mean more compute or bolder architecture? Should an agent initiate direction changes, or does the triage skill's rule (the human owns direction, prefer fail-fast slices) stand? Were the two idle overnight windows a pause by the human?

<a id="h10"></a>**H10. Tooling and notes.** The mac's relay policy is on for Codex in auto-research mode, so `ens astra` worker jobs run under main-thread hooks (no message reached the probe job; none of the nine reported one). The run moved the Codex weekly quota from 76% to 83%. The push of the notes branch is not authorised for this session.

<a id="limits"></a>
## 8. Limits of this synthesis

- I read fourteen reports in full and [astra/02](astra/02-pre-v3-history.md) in part (its summary, attempts on end-to-end systems and timing, ban reasoning, overlooked items and items for the human).
- My own checks were five greps on the exported lineage-end tree: `ResponsePlanner` has no caller outside its module; the segment decoder deletes `row_consequence`; the fresh expert's `Recovery(20,1,1)` and unbounded H; the 35M teacher appears in no lineage-era document; the formulation's lines on undefined response, excluded physiology, beat coordinates and provisional rows. All five agree with the reports.
- Slices 02 and 06 have no independent reviewer. The Opus reviewers ran on the model requested; the reasoning effort could not be set.
- No reviewer listened to audio, played a chart, or obtained a new human judgment. Statements about what the human saw rest on the paraphrased feedback index.
- Most per-experiment numbers inside the Astra inventories are document claims, marked as such there.
