## Motivation and bounded claim

The 192-candidate supply study is terminal. Five LN contexts have zero attack
cost in every first-four/four-second candidate; 159 of their 160 complete
sixteen-second proposal futures also have zero attack cost. The exception is
actor Classic candidate 7, J16 .0004418993. This corrects an earlier progress
statement inferred too broadly from sparse progress logs. The observation
establishes a missing response channel, not the quality of all candidates.

Memory Classic retains median one-H LN span in 30/32 first-eight-second
proposals. Longer alternatives exist but inspected examples often move or
extend a TAP/LN block rather than establish a clear general organization repair.
Same-time comparisons with zero occupancy and identical full legal support
show large neural type-preference differences. At 91522 ms, memory candidates
6 and 28 have expected LN-head ratios .01793 and .94793, with feedback offsets
+2 and +.3203. Content history, recovery-clock values and learned state still
differ; this does not isolate one neural cause or prove an attractor theorem.

One concrete restriction is independent of that attribution. The main R1
composition branch predicts head/release family mass with actual controls,
but predicts the LN conditional with LN fraction replaced by ln_reference,
then applies a fixed logit-ratio tilt. In the plain actor, the explicit type
readout therefore cannot learn a context-dependent effect of the actual LN
request beyond this exponential-family shift. Nonlinear frontier/layout paths
remain, and the memory model can additionally pass actual controls through its
history reader, so the complete policy is not universally restricted that way.

Giving the R1 type branch its intended actual control is a representation
change, not a new shape rule, R1 count transfer or external quota. It does not
by itself fix H rhythm, learned player response, LN span preferences or long jacks.
The existing analytic tilt can remain as an initialization/edge prior while a
context-dependent readout learns its residual; removing the reference-value
substitution removes the specific restriction. Compare rather than presume gain.

Evaluation: REFINE. The additional LN-input path has no material learning or
native-quality gain in this bounded fit. Shared factual count learning improves
NLL but produces native regressions. Do not scale this count-only intervention
as though it repaired organization. This does not reject broader joint training,
larger models or the general benefit of actual control conditioning.

The structural scope matters: at a common fixed prefix, composition cancels
from odds between rows with the same head/LN/release counts. With layout and
consequence frozen, this fit cannot directly teach new within-family column or
release-identity decisions. Later prefix differences can change those odds only
indirectly. A repair aimed at these relations must train/redesign their actual
owners and verify actual reached-state behavior, not only source likelihood.

Read-only source inspection also identifies a distinct, untested control-state
question. Plain exact replay stores cumulative rows/heads, not cumulative LN
starts; finite content history cannot recover arbitrarily old scope allocation.
The disabled amount controller stores only a clipped offset. A learned factual
scope-progress input may separate whole-scope allocation from fixed prefix
balancing, but interruption semantics and native benefits still need design and
testing. This is not claimed as the cause of the current regressions or as a
replacement for player-response/LN-relationship learning.
