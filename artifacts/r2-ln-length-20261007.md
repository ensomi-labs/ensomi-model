## 0. Summary

**Cause.** Guard (iv) fails because phase N, generating from the start of a song with nothing but the head
skeleton, places long notes on very dense rows of charts whose real mappers used almost none there. Two
things produce that. The natural model has no chart-level LN commitment, so from BOS each free run's LN level
is uncorrelated with the source chart's (r = 0.00 at 48M, -0.16 at 64M over the 16 panel charts). And on its
own history its LN-birth rate drifts above the rate it gives at the same chart's real states: +41 % at 48M,
+78 % at 64M, matched for row gap. Once an LN is born on a row followed by another within 60 ms, a short
hold or a near-head release follows at about the rate real charts show in that context, or below it.
Release timing, the keep-or-release decision and the release representation are not the cause.

On 48 other fit_dev charts, where the model's free LN level happens to be *below* the sources', the same 48M
checkpoint passes both parts of guard (iv) from BOS: 0.40 % short holds against the 0.5 % ceiling, and 0.59 %
near-head releases against these sources' 0.98 %. 86-89 % of the selection-panel failures come from its three
dense 5-star charts.

**Q1, representation: no.** The cache is faithful: on all 1,100 fit_dev charts, raw `.osu` and cache agree
on every hold. The 2 ms merge and release snapping change about 1.5 % of holds of 60 ms or less, and remove
more than they create. Candidate sets always offer near-head positions and usually short-hold positions.
The model, teacher-forced, puts 14-15 % less mass on them than the sources use.

**Q2, R1: partly.** R2 does not repeat R1's timing mechanisms. Release-only events no longer sample on a free
millisecond clock, and head times are given. It does repeat R1's third mechanism: too many LN on dense
heads, driven by an LN level that feeds back through history (R1's "bistability", R2 v1's M1H).

**Q3, exposure bias: yes, but only in the LN-level channel, and it amplifies rather than creates.**
- At teacher-forced states the model is calibrated or under-predicts. Short holds implied per source LN
  birth: 4.4 % against 5.2 % real. Near-head mass is 15 % below the source frequency.
- On its own history, matched for context, its per-state release, short and near-head hazards are lower
  than teacher-forced. Its LN-birth rate is higher and, at 64M, rises along the song (0.073 to 0.128).

**Human's hypothesis (keep poorly learned, leaning to early release): refuted, strong evidence.**
- The teacher-forced release hazard by elapsed hold length is within 0.81-1.06× of the source in every
  length bin above 30 ms (59,251 held-lane states in all), and below the source at 30-150 ms.
- The only over-release is the youngest tail: ≤ 30 ms, 1.9 % against 0 (884 states), and ≤ 1/16 beat, 3.0 %
  against 1.5 % (1,244 states). It is worth under 0.1 % of holds.
- Hold-length distributions of free runs and sources overlap (median 162-167 ms against 175 ms). Holds of
  60 ms or less are 1.0-1.7 % in free runs against 5.8 % in the panel sources.

**The guard itself.** The guard cannot be met by a model that matches the data.
- Part 1's 0.5 % ceiling is below the panel sources' own 5.8 % (171 of the 179 short source holds are exactly
  60 ms, in one chart) and below the corpus rate of 2.72 %.
- Part 2 compares with a pooled 0.42 % from a mostly LN-light panel. The corpus rate is 1.96 %, and 5.5 %
  at 5 stars. Plan v4 specified "within the source band's rate".
- The claim "real charts contain no holds of 60 ms or less" is true of the 8-chart A panel it came from. It
  is false for the corpus.

**R2 v1 against v2: no regression.**
- On the same 16-chart panel, v1 at 48M makes 2.6× as many holds as phase N from BOS. Its short-hold rate is
  three times phase N's (5.3 % against 1.7 %) and its near-head rate the same (3.1 % against 3.2 %). With
  the prefix, v1 is at 7.2 % and 2.7 %, phase N at 1.5 % and 1.5 %.
- v1's LN-birth rate on own history is 2.4× its teacher-forced rate and climbs from 0.105 to 0.302 along the
  song.
- v1's "at most 0.3 %" came from its 4-chart panel. Phase N also stays at 0.26 % or less there, at every
  checkpoint.

**Remedies, ranked.**
1. A chart-level LN commitment trained into the natural model: a whole-song LN-level input with dropout,
   drawn from a data prior at generation.
2. A long-horizon own-history LN-level objective or check.
3. A data-derived minimum hold, if wanted at all, at 40 ms or with `< 60`. It addresses part 1 only.
4. Correcting guard (iv)'s references, an evaluation change. As built, a model that reproduced the panel's
   own sources would still fail.

Duration-at-birth, candidate-set changes and loss terms on release timing are not supported by the evidence.

### 2.3 Candidate sets (`cand-v1`; 234,864 source gap releases)

- Every gap's candidate set contains positions 1-40 ms before the next row: 14.1 % of all candidates.
- 73.5 % of gaps offer at least one candidate that makes the hold ≤ 60 ms: 17.1 % of candidates.
- Sources pick a near-b candidate in 5.4 % of gap releases (band 2 0.19 %, band 3 1.25 %, band 4 3.9 %,
  band 5 13.4 %).
- Sources pick a short-making candidate in 6.9 % of gap releases (9.3 % when one is offered).

So the representation can express both defects everywhere. A pointer that learned the source frequencies
would not over-produce them; one closer to uniform would, with 14 % near-head gap releases against 5.4 %.

What the model does with them, teacher-forced on 112 fit_dev charts (48M):
- **Source candidate.** The pointer gives it median probability 0.70 and ranks it first in 70 % of gap
  releases (band 2: 0.91 and 88 %; band 5: 0.63 and 66 %).
- **Near-head mass.** Summed over every held-lane state, near-head mass is 1,376 against 1,625 near-head
  releases in the sources (-15 %); 1,403 at 64M.
- **Short-hold mass.** Over states where a short release is possible, short-hold mass is 1,722 against
  1,999 (-14 %); 1,798 at 64M. In the row-gap bins that carry most near-head releases (40-120 ms), the model
  is 0.72-0.77× the source rate.

**Verdict, Q1.** Not the representation. Strength: strong for the cache, measured on every fit_dev chart.
Strong for the candidate set and pointer calibration: 14,313 source gap releases, teacher-forced at two
checkpoints. The candidates' permissiveness matters only if the pointer is miscalibrated, and it is not.

**(d) What the row decision sees [code], frozen phase-N package.**
- **Elapsed hold length.** `features.lane_query` gives every lane, at decision k: held bit, LN age
  t_k − start through `psi_pair` (ms/1000 clipped and log1p; beats clipped and log1p), last-attack and
  last-release ages, and absence bits (15 per lane).
- **Shared query.** `query_features` adds the gap t_k − t_{k−1}, the next 16 head gaps, densities over 1-32
  beats, BPM, meter and position. Both hand vectors read all four lanes in mirrored orders.
- **Path to the action scores.** `hands` fuses the query MLP with the TCN state before k (memory `none` in
  phase N; receptive field 511 decisions). `JointHead` scores each hand's 25 lane-pair codes from that hand's
  vector, plus a rank-16 bilinear coupling between hands (`model.py`, `bounded_typed_continuation/model.py`).
- So the elapsed length is an input to the keep/release choice itself, in ms and beats, not only to the
  release pointer. For the pointer, the candidate features add u − start, u − t_k and u − t_{k−1}
  (`candidates.lane_features`).
- What the row decision does not see is the candidate set of the gap it opens with codes 2-4. It sees the
  gap length and the age, which bound every candidate's hold length.
- **Loss.** `loss.window_terms`: per decision −(log P(A) + log Q(U)), uniform weight per decision with the
  fixed divisor N_bar. Keep is one value of a lane's code inside the 625-way joint softmax and is not
  weighted differently from release. There is no per-lane term, no survival or length term, and no class
  weighting (`loss.py`, `train_ce.py`).

**Verdict: refuted.**
- The model does not lean towards ending LNs early. At teacher-forced states its release hazard is
  calibrated, and slightly *low* at young ages. On its own history it releases no more than at real states in
  the same context. Its free-run hold lengths match the sources'.
- Strength: strong for (a)-(c): tens of thousands of states and two checkpoints. At 64M the
  teacher-forced ratios are 0.86-1.00 above 30 ms, with the same young tail (≤ 30 ms 0.022 against 0). v1
  is within 0.93-1.06 above 30 ms.
- The only over-release is a thin tail: gap releases of holds younger than 60 ms (8× the source rate) and any
  release at ≤ 30 ms (1.9 % against 0). Together they are worth well under 0.1 % of holds.

## 5. Q3: calibration or exposure bias?

**Teacher-forced mass against the source** (112 fit_dev charts):
- Short holds implied per source LN birth, 1 − Π(1 − p_short) over the decisions where a ≤ 60 ms release is
  possible: 4.43 % [4.62 %] against 5.16 % real (38,741 births).
- By band: 2 0.10 % / 0; 3 1.10 % / 1.07 %; 4 3.14 % / 3.64 %; 5 8.70 % / 10.31 %.
- Near-head mass: -15 % [-14 %] against the source.
- LN-birth probability at free lanes against the source, by next-row gap: ≤ 40 ms 0.072 / 0.069; 40-60 ms
  0.058 / 0.059; 60-80 ms 0.044 / 0.045.
- Per chart, the teacher-forced LN-birth probability equals the source's within 0.015 on all 16 panel charts.

So the training target is learned. Calibration does not explain the free-run excess.

**Own history against teacher forced, matched cells.** Free-run states are reweighted to teacher-forced
states in the same cells (band × age × row gap × density for hazards; band × next-row gap × density for
births), and "same chart" means the panel chart's own source states.

- Per-chart LN level from BOS against the source chart: correlation 0.00 [-0.16], SD ratio 0.43 [0.84].
  From the one-third prefix: correlation 0.84 [0.85], SD ratio 0.65 [0.80].

- Across full evaluations, the BOS LN-share excess over source swings between −0.08 and +0.15, and the

  stays within ±0.045.

**Reading.**
- The two parts of guard (iv) fail the same way. Neither is caused by drift in the release decision: per-state
  release, short and near-head hazards on own history are at or below the teacher-forced ones.
- Own-history drift acts on the LN level. From BOS the model draws a near-average LN level that ignores
  the chart (no chart identity is available). On its own history that level is then reinforced (+10 % [+38 %]
  band-matched, +41 % [+78 %] same chart, growing along the song at 64M). In dense LN-light charts this
  multiplies the opportunities for both defects.
- Exposure bias therefore amplifies the defects. It does not create a per-opportunity excess.
- Strength: moderate to strong. The matched comparisons cover 16 charts × 3 seeds × 2 checkpoints, about
  25k own-history held states and 140k free-lane states per arm.
- **Caveats.**
  - The causal step "fewer dense LN births → fewer defects" is supported by the prefix arm, an
    observational contrast, and by the per-chart pattern. No intervention was run.
  - The 112-chart teacher-forced sample overweights band 5 relative to the corpus by construction, 24 charts
    per band.

## 7. R1 comparison (Q2)

What the notes concluded about R1 and its lineage (`s-ln-open-state`, `controls-ln` §4-6, audit C2/S2/S3):
1. **Near-head releases.** Releases just before another head (6.9 % of generated LN against 1.0 % ranked)
   came from release-only events on a free millisecond clock, sampled separately from the head hazard: 61 %
   of the cases, 3× over-represented. The rest sat before near-duplicate heads.
2. **Holds of 40 ms or less.** 4-5 % of LN against none in five sources. The audit tied part of this to
   near-duplicate heads in the fresh expert; real head times on the inherited line did not reduce them.
3. **LN allocation.** Too many LN on too dense heads, with LN share bistable without the inference-time
   controller (near 0 or above 0.9). The 09-28 attribution case was 91 % LN at 69 ms spacing; 97.6 % of its
   short tails ended at the very next head. The audit's reading: "the lever is LN allocation, not release
   timing."

What R2 changed: heads are given (no near-duplicate heads), and releases are grid candidates decided jointly
with the next row ([a-coupling], [d-r2-row-decision]). The release-length question ([a-hazard]) was left to
per-row keep/release with the elapsed length as an input; a minimum hold was deferred ([d-r2-spec] 6).

What R2 repeats:
- **Mechanisms 1 and 2: no.** Generated near-head releases are release-only events in 72 % against 74 % in
  the sources, so there is no over-representation. Holds ≤ 40 ms are at the corpus rate (0.14-0.28 % against
  0.33 %). The release hazard is calibrated.
- **Mechanism 3: yes, in milder form.** The LN level is not tied to the chart and is reinforced through
  history. Phase N's +41 % to +78 % same-chart own-history LN excess is the same mechanism as R2 v1's
  teacher-forced 0.191 against own-history 0.409 (`r2-analysis-fable-judgment`, M1H). v1 shows it at full
  strength here (0.105 → 0.302 along the song).
- No R2 design choice targeted mechanism 3 in phase N. Rule L and phase C's LN-share condition address it
  only when a request is given.

<a id="s-lnlen-guard"></a>
## 8. Guard (iv) as built

These are not training issues, but no training remedy can be judged against the guard until they are settled.
- **Part 1: holds ≤ 60 ms at most 0.5 %.** The panel's own sources are at 5.8 % and the corpus at 2.72 %
  (band 4 3.1 %, band 5 7.0 %).
  - A generator that reproduced the panel exactly would fail by 11×.
  - `<= 60` counts exactly-60 ms holds, a deliberate pattern in `3b8ffb8ad8` (all 171 of its short holds).
  - The 90 short holds left under Astra's 60 ms decode mask are all holds in copied source prefixes.
- **Part 2: near-head within the source panel's pooled rate (0.42 %).**
  - The panel's band-5 sources are LN-light (11 near-head in 628 holds, 1.75 %), against 5.5 % for band 5 in
    the corpus.
  - Plan v4 §9 wrote "within the source band's rate"; `select.py` pools.
  - Against corpus band rates, phase N 48M BOS is at 0.76 / 0.47 / 0.61 / 10.8 % for bands 2-5, against 0.05
    / 0.44 / 2.14 / 5.48 %. 64M: 0.46 / 0.34 / 0.37 / 8.3 %. The remaining excess is band 5 (1.5-2×) and band 2
    (relative only: 22 of 2,911).
- **Prefix holds.** Both parts count holds the model did not make (the copied third of `prefix_natural`).
- **Panel dependence.** The verdict depends on the panel. On 48 other fit_dev charts the same 48M checkpoint
  passes both parts from BOS (§3.4).

Suggested replacements (evaluation): per-band reference rates from fit_train, or from the panel charts'
own sources paired per chart; holds `< 60` (or ≤ 40); model-made holds only; and a reported dense-context
LN-birth ratio, generated against the source, which is the quantity that actually moved.

<a id="p-lnlen-remedies"></a>
## 9. Remedies, ranked by the evidence

Costs are measured against phase N's 64M-exposure run: 35,169 s ≈ 9.8 h wall at 1,820 decisions/s (4 CPU
threads), 2,000 teacher-forced decisions/s, and the sampler's 100-220 decisions/s on one process (the
instrumented probe ran 50-370).

### R1 (recommended): a chart-level LN commitment in the natural model

**What.** Train phase N with a whole-song LN-level input:
- the source chart's LN share, or a coarse bucket of it, possibly split into dense and non-dense rows;
- dropped out to "unknown" with probability p (for example 0.3).

At natural generation from BOS, draw the level from an empirical prior P(LN level | band, skeleton
density), fitted on fit_train. This is the [a-ln-mode] "chart-level LN amount as a natural condition", moved
into phase N. A whole-song phase-C LN-share interval [0, T] is the conditioned-model version of the same
thing.

**Why.**
- From BOS the LN level is uncorrelated with the chart and compressed (r ≈ 0, SD ratio 0.43).
- With the chart's identity supplied by a prefix, dense-context LN births match the source (0.010 against
  0.012) and near-head releases halve.
- Every per-opportunity hazard is already calibrated.

**Code.** `features.query_features` gets a level channel, or the FiLM frames get a whole-song LN
interval. `data.py` draws the level and the dropout. A prior table goes in the cache (or `labels.py`), and
`sampling.py` draws the level at BOS. `select.py` needs no change.

**Cost.** The prior is minutes of cache work. The model change is small. One phase-N retrain is about 10 h.
The sampler is unchanged.

**Confirms.** From BOS with the level set to each source's share, on the 16-chart panel:
- dense-context LN-birth rate within 1.5× the source's;
- BOS near-head per band at or below the corpus band rate;
- with the level drawn from the prior, per-chart LN SD ratio in [0.7, 1.3].

**Refutes.** With the true level supplied, LN births in dense rows still run several times the source,
meaning the model does not learn density-dependent LN placement within a level.

**Pre-training check, no training.** Phase C's frozen-base FiLM path, once trained, can run the same test
with a whole-song request.

### R2: long-horizon own-history control of the LN level (exposure bias)

**What.** At 64M the own-history LN-birth rate exceeds the same chart's teacher-forced rate by 38-78 % and
rises along the song. v1 shows the same at 2.4×. Guard (iii)'s 64-decision horizon does not see it.

**Options, cheapest first.**
- **(a) Selection.** Add a whole-song own-history LN drift measure to selection: |last − first third| and
  the same-chart matched birth ratio over full free runs. This is evaluation only and costs nothing
  extra: the free runs already exist.
- **(b) Long-horizon calibration term.** From source prefix states, roll out H = 256-512 decisions without
  gradient and penalise the squared gap between the model's expected LN-birth rate on the rollout and on the
  real continuation, with gradient through the forecasts on the rollout. This is the relaxed-proxy row of plan v4
  §7.3, applied to LN level.
  - Cost: one 256-decision rollout per 16 teacher-forced windows adds about 256/150 ≈ 1.7 s to about
    16 × 256/2,000 ≈ 2 s, roughly +85 % wall, or 18 h for 64M [inferred].
- **(c) Scheduled sampling of history tokens.** Targets stay the source decisions, while a fraction of the
  history tokens come from model samples. It needs sequential sampling inside the window, 10-20× slower per
  sampled decision.
  - It weakens commitment to the realised LN level, which R1 needs; it would likely trade drift for a weaker
    style memory [inferred]. Not recommended before (b).

**Confirms.** Same-chart matched LN-birth ratio, own history against teacher forced, within ±15 %, and
first-to-last-third drift |mean| ≤ 0.02 across consecutive checkpoints.

**Refutes.** Drift falls but BOS defect rates in band 5 stay above the corpus rate. In that case R1 is the
lever.

### R3: a data-derived minimum hold in the action contract (training and decoding)

**What.** In `state.action_support_mask` and the candidate filter, forbid code 1 when t_k − start < m, gap
candidates with u − start < m, and codes 2-4 when no candidate survives. The same mask applies in the loss
normaliser and at decoding. Source holds below m in the cache must be remapped (extended to the first legal
candidate or row) or the charts dropped.

**Choice of m.**
- At m = 60 with `<=`, the contract removes 2.72 % of real holds (7.0 % in band 5), including whole
  patterns such as `3b8ffb8ad8`.
- At m = 40 it removes 0.33 % (0.89 % in band 5).
- Strict `< 60` keeps exact-60 ms quarter beats at 250 BPM.

**Evidence of need: weak.** Free runs already make fewer ≤ 60 ms holds than the corpus, and ≤ 40 ms at the
corpus rate. Astra's decode mask (`r2-minhold-20261007/report.md`) already fixes part 1, with 0 generated
releases ≤ 60 ms, 0 fallbacks and guard (i) kept. It leaves near-head releases at 1.38-1.63 %, so this
remedy does not touch part 2.

**Cost.** Cache rebuild in minutes, then about 10 h retrain. The sampler cost is nil.

**Confirms / refutes.** NLL on unaffected decisions unchanged within 0.01. Generated holds below m equal 0
by construction, so they confirm nothing. Near-head unchanged is expected, not a refutation.

### Not recommended by this evidence

- **Duration at birth, or anchor plus offset.** The length marginal already matches (survival table) and the
  release hazard is calibrated. A duration chosen at birth would not change where LN are born, which is what
  moved. It remains the untested alternative of [H5], but nothing here points to it.
- **Candidate set or pointer views.** Removing near-b candidates would remove real band-4/5 style
  (13.4 % of band-5 gap releases use them). The pointer under-uses them already, and generated near-head
  gap releases are mostly in gaps of 41 ms or less, where every candidate is near-head.
- **Loss terms on release timing or length.** They would push a calibrated model away from the data.
- **Class reweighting of keep.** Keep is not under-learned (§4).

<a id="u-lnlen-unknown"></a>
## 10. What remains unknown

- **Causal test of R1.** No intervention was run. The prefix contrast is observational: the prefix supplies
  the LN level but also the chart's style beyond it.
- **Band-5 near-head.** The one cell above source in both the selection panel and the random panel
  (4.3 % against 2.4 %). Counts are small in the random panel (20 events, one seed). Whether it is
  density-specific placement within band 5 or chart sampling is open; a larger band-5 free-run panel (about
  50 charts × 3 seeds, about 1 h of sampler time per checkpoint) would settle it.
- **Same-chart near-head hazard in the random panel's prefix arm.** Own history 0.0077 against teacher-forced
  0.0036 per held state (80 events). This is the only matched cell where an own-history release hazard
  exceeds the teacher-forced one; it was not seen on the selection panel (0.002 against 0.002).
- **Checkpoint-to-checkpoint oscillation.** Its cause is not identified; the BOS LN level swings by ±0.1 with
  flat NLL. Single-seed training run.
- **Human judgment.** Whether a human would judge the generated short LNs in dense passages as defects when
  the chart's LN level is consistent, as in LN-heavy dense ranked charts.
- **v1 comparison.** One v1 checkpoint; the lr schedule and memory are confounded.
- **Per-state "actual" near-head** for row releases uses only the next head row. Hold-level counts use the
  exact `generate.defects` definition.
- **Pointer mass q_short and q_near** at each state are computed under the most likely action with a gap code
  for that lane, averaging the two orientations with only that action's row releases placed (the lane treated
  as first). This is an approximation for decisions with several gap releases. The realised hold-level
  counts do not depend on it.

Dead ends and detours:
- The stored free runs (`freerun/<exposures>/records.jsonl`) keep only per-chart defect counts, not the
  decisions, so every free run was regenerated. They reproduce the stored counts exactly (96/96 at 48M and
  64M).
- R2 v1's own evaluation panel (4 charts) could not be compared with phase N's 16. v1 was re-run on the
  16-chart panel instead.
- No step failed. Astra's job `20261007-082909-r2-minhold-alloc` finished during this work; its results are
  quoted, not reproduced.
