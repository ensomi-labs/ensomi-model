## Decisions and priors, 2026-10-03

Source: the human's answer to the first version ([private, local](private/human-inputs/fa69c029-7080-4f45-8b54-be050497532a.md#answer-1)). Agent account; the original wording is not reproduced here.

<a id="d-failure-scope"></a>**Decision, human.** The failures to answer are Control V3's, the September controls' and the lineage evaluators', as in the table above.

<a id="d-evaluator-purpose"></a>**Decision, human. The evaluator's main purpose is to model the distribution of normal charts.** Supplying preferences is one use read off that model, not its purpose. The first version's "audit subset kept out of preference" was neither accepted nor rejected; it stays a proposal under property 7.

<a id="d-star-key"></a>**Decision, human (agreed to the agent's item).** The reference is keyed by the requested star, or the source chart's star for a generator without a star input, never by the star the output reached; a passage is compared with corpus passages of the same length from charts of that star.

<a id="d-golden-separate"></a>**Decision, human. Human-labelled golden parts in Beatmap Lens (`~/ensomi/beatmap-lens`) are for DPO rewards later, and work separately from the evaluator.** They do not fit, calibrate or select it.

<a id="d-no-cutoffs"></a>**Decision, human. No hard-coded cut-offs; calibration is a divergence between output and corpus, KL-like.**

<a id="h-body-priors"></a>**Priors, human, to be formalised and tested.**

- Chart organisation is in beats.
- Players play in physical seconds, and difficulty calculation is in seconds.
- Physical load comes from coordination, within a hand and between hands, and from strain.
- The body has a physical limit; as a gap in milliseconds approaches it, difficulty rises exponentially.
- Note types contribute different kinds of load, but an LN head and a tap are highly similar.

## Properties, converged (proposed)

Seven properties. Each names the first-version properties it absorbs (numbers at `44d820b`) and the failures it answers.

<a id="p-op-model"></a>**1. One model of normal, keyed by what was asked.** The evaluator is a conditional distribution of chart descriptions given the key: requested star ([d-star-key](#d-star-key)), the grid's canonical BPM, the condition's fixed aspects and the scope shape. Under a condition that fixes aspects, it is the distribution of the free aspects given the fixed ones, so it measures the generator's choices and not the condition. Observed and reference values come from the same code applied to corpus cases; there is no separate target, label or normaliser. Absorbs 3, 4, 5, 12. Answers `cv-pointwise`, `cv-scale`, `cv-units`, `le-label`, `le-grid`.

<a id="p-op-clocks"></a>**2. Two clocks.** Organisation is described in canonical beats: exact subdivision families (binary and triplet apart), beat phase, bars, recurrence. Load is described in seconds, because players play and difficulty is computed in seconds ([h-body-priors](#h-body-priors)). Each quantity declares its clock. Checks: under a time-stretch of chart and grid together, beat quantities are unchanged and seconds quantities change; output is identical under mirror, renotation at half or double BPM (whole or per segment), shift of chart and grid, expressive red lines and green lines, file order. Absorbs 6, 7. Answers `cv-seconds`, `cv-buckets`, `cv-mirror`, `le-ms`.

<a id="p-op-body"></a>**3. Load from the body.** Load is a function of presses and holds per finger and per hand: same-finger gaps, coordination within a hand (trills, chords, one finger held while the other presses), coordination between hands (simultaneity and alternation), and strain accumulated over time. Cost rises steeply, the prior says exponentially, as a gap approaches the body's limit; the limit and the rate are estimated from the corpus or stated as sourced priors, never set as a cut-off. A tap and an LN head enter as the same press, to be tested; a hold occupies its finger until release; a release is its own action, with its own load, placed against heads on every lane. Lanes are never pooled into one statistic. Absorbs 10, 11, part of 8 and 18. Answers `cv-pooled`, `cv-fixed-gap`, `cv-ln`, `le-same-column`, `le-hard-rule`, and the single-column run the star rating scores at 4.14 (`le-star`). Revised after linking demand and the frontier: [p-op-body-r](#p-op-body-r), with property 8, [p-op-frontier](#p-op-frontier).

<a id="p-op-felt"></a>**4. What players feel stays visible.** Descriptions are sequences: transitions between successive rows, runs and holds measured whole (across window edges, and from the given span into the scored one), holds by length in beats and release placement, at row, beat, bar, phrase and scope scale, with no smoothing before the comparison. Must-flag checks: a lane permutation other than the mirror, rows shuffled inside a bar, 1/4 positions moved to 1/3, a release moved against another lane's head, a hold's length in beats changed. Absorbs 8, 9, 10, 12, 17. Answers `cv-no-transitions`, `cv-no-memory`, `cv-smoothed`, `cv-ln`, `sc-scope-ln`.

<a id="p-op-divergence"></a>**5. Divergence, not cut-offs.** Departure from normal is a divergence between the distribution of descriptions in the output and in the keyed corpus ([d-no-cutoffs](#d-no-cutoffs)), over passages, so how often and how long enter by construction and one corpus-typical occurrence costs nothing. Both directions count: output mass where the corpus has little (defects), and corpus mass the output never reaches (missing modes, such as never writing LN). Joint descriptions are kept where marginals are known to fail: LN choice by local density, anchor by length, chord size by gap. Sparse evidence widens the estimate's interval; it never pulls a value toward neutral. Absorbs 13 to 16, 18. Answers `le-extremes`, `le-whole-chart`, `le-hard-rule`, `cv-gated`, `cv-pointwise`; follows [h-rate-not-presence](evaluation-first.md#h-rate-not-presence).

<a id="p-op-honest"></a>**6. Honest reads and scope.** Each operator declares what it reads and what it judges, with one lesion test per aspect. Output depends only on the scored and given spans; the given span is read and never scored; a run crossing into the scored span is attributed to it. Absorbs 1, 2. Answers `le-star`, `le-grid`, `sc-scope-ln`, [fm-tests-not-binding](agent-failure-modes.md#fm-tests-not-binding).

<a id="p-op-evidence"></a>**7. Calibrated, and evidence by song.** Fitted on the fit split, calibrated on the calibration split, held-out untouched until reporting, all split by song group ([p-split](evaluation-first.md#p-split)). The divergence of calibration corpus against fit corpus is the floor, per stratum (star, canonical BPM, condition, scope). Dose-response for defect families written from the human's rejections and the review before results are seen; an operator without measured sensitivity, or explained by star or by the rest of the set, is dropped. Intervals by song bootstrap; spans attributable as osu! editor timestamps; output a pure function of chart, condition, scope and reference version, with its hash. When the model supplies preferences ([d-evaluator-purpose](#d-evaluator-purpose)), a view not used for preference shows whether gains are real (proposed). Absorbs 17 to 24. Answers `cv-uncalibrated`, `cv-split`, `le-tuned`, `le-vacuous`, `le-noise`, `le-star`.

## Demand and the frontier in the evaluator

Written 2026-10-03 by main session `10fd41cc` (Claude, control plane), at the human's request to link gameplay demand and the frontier into the operator properties ([private, local](private/human-inputs/10fd41cc-e5d5-4d85-8def-2bee49dada16.md#prompt-1)), after the human placed the exponential prior with demand, the frontier and physiology ([d-astra-not-accepted](#d-astra-not-accepted)). Everything in this section is the agent's `(proposed)` design unless marked as an observation.

<a id="s-three-objects"></a>**Agent reading, 2026-10-03. Three objects share the words "demand" and "frontier".**

- The formulation's target response $\mathcal C_0(H_{\le t},t;Y,e)$ and gameplay frontier $\mathcal F_H(t):(Y,e)\mapsto\mathcal C_0$, the response of every legal future to one past. Its quantities and comparison rules are undefined. A demand state $d_\psi$ represents it, with an event update $U_\psi$ at each row and an advance $V_\psi$ through time without rows (`5c56e28:docs/formulation/gameplay-state.md`, "Target response and frontier", "Dynamics and reproducibility"). This is node [response](../RESEARCH.md#response).
- A per-star envelope: how close the charts mappers wrote at one star come to the body's limit. Astra's mean 1st percentile of same-finger gaps (204, 157, 124 and 107 ms from 2-3 to 5-6 stars, [s-astra-operators](#s-astra-operators)) is one reading. It is a property of the corpus, not a response.
- R1's `frontier2`, a policy residual on a candidate row's exact consequences. Unrelated here.

<a id="s-star-calc-linear"></a>**Observation, 2026-10-03, read by the main thread in `2044c2b:src/ensomi_model/osu_core/difficulty.py` lines 384-466 (the calculator that matches official stars within 5e-6). The osu!mania star rating keeps strain in seconds and has no limit and no hands.** Each note adds a fixed 2.0 to its column's strain, whatever its gap, or 2.5 when another lane's hold spans it. Column strain decays by a factor 0.125 per second (time constant 0.48 s), and an overall strain by 0.30 per second (0.83 s); a logistic term adds load for releases near other releases. On one column at a steady rate r, strain just after each note is $2/(1-0.125^{1/r})$: 4.9 at 4 Hz, 6.8 at 6 Hz, 12.6 at 12 Hz, so it grows linearly with rate and no gap is infeasible. That is consistent with the 4.14 stars it gives a 16 s single-column 12 Hz run (`le-star`). The human's priors keep the calculator's clock and its exponential decay, and add what it lacks: a limit, and coordination by hand.

<a id="p-demand-machine"></a>**Proposal: one demand machine carries both readings of "exponential".** State per finger (four) and per hand: strain, time of the last press, held mask, time of the last release. Replay determines it exactly; the event update and the advance are the formulation's $U_\psi$ and $V_\psi$.

- Strain decays exponentially in seconds between actions, $s(t+\Delta)=s(t)\,e^{-\Delta/\tau}$. Elapsed time is not rest for an occupied finger; whether a hold stops decay or adds load is a prior to state.
- Each action adds an impulse that rises without bound as its gap $g$ to the governing earlier action approaches a limit $g_0$ for that relation, for example $a\,e^{\kappa/(g-g_0)}$ or $a\,(g-g_0)^{-p}$. Relations, each with its own $g_0$: same finger; the other finger of the same hand; the other hand; a press while the partner finger holds; a release, against the next press on every lane. A tap and an LN head give the same press impulse (the human's prior; Astra's math note registers a test of it, P-e, not run).
- Fatigue couples the two: accumulated strain raises the effective limit, $g_0(s)=g_0+\beta s$, so the edge recedes in a sustained passage and comes back with rest. A finite reserve spent above a critical rate is the alternative form. Revised after sources: fatigue is a slower state of its own, and the reserve form has no support for tapping ([p-demand-after-sources](#p-demand-after-sources)).

<a id="p-frontier-slack"></a>**Proposal: the frontier as the evaluator reads it.** After each row the machine gives the cost $c(y,\Delta\mid d)$ of every candidate next row $y$ at delay $\Delta$. The frontier at level $\theta$ is, per lane, the earliest delay whose cost stays under $\theta$: $\Delta^*_\ell(\theta\mid d)$. With a divergent impulse it sits just above $g_0(s)$ for any reasonable $\theta$, so the edge is sharp and the choice of $\theta$ matters little. In a finished chart the next row is known: its slack $\Delta-\Delta^*_\ell$ in seconds, or its cost against $\theta$, is the per-action demand description. A chart's demand description is the trajectory of strain per finger and hand and slack per action over passages: how close to the edge, how often, how long, how it recovers. Selection and difficulty control, later, read the same machine for the candidates not taken.

<a id="p-demand-identification"></a>**Proposal: what fixes each part.**

| Part | Fixed by | Never by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure: state, relations, decay, divergence, tap = LN head | The human's priors | A fit to the corpus |
| $g_0$ per relation | Lower bound from sourced physiology (withdrawn after sources: lab maxima are sustained rates, slower than bursts ranked charts contain, [p-demand-after-sources](#p-demand-after-sources)); upper bound from what players pass, the ranked charts at the top stars (the R2 corpus holds 991 ranked or loved charts at 6 stars and above, 63 at 8 and above; `data/r2-corpus.json`, `ranked_loved_star_bands`) | Corpus gap density |
| $\tau$ | Sourced physiology; the star calculator's 0.48 s and 0.83 s as default | |

| Distribution of demand at each star | The corpus, fit split, keyed by star | |

Why the corpus cannot fix the cost law: Astra's math note, section 4 (`artifacts/eval-operators-20261003/math.md` in the code checkout, untracked): if $p\propto A\,e^{-C}$, any $C'$ with $A'=A\,e^{C'-C}$ gives the same corpus. What could fix it is play data, scores or replays against patterns; whether play data is in scope is [H2](lineage-review/synthesis.md#h2), not answered.

<a id="p-demand-role"></a>**Proposal: demand in the evaluator and in `response`.**

- Evaluator: demand descriptions are coordinates of the normal model ([p-op-model](#p-op-model)), which becomes, in part, the distribution of demand trajectories at the key star. Departure is output mass where the corpus at that star has little, such as long runs at low slack without recovery, and corpus mass the output never reaches. Under `skeleton` event times are fixed, but which finger carries each press is still the generator's choice, so demand still measures the generator.
- `response`: the same machine is the demand state. Its frontier serves selection (rank candidate futures) and difficulty control (a request read as a target distribution of demand over a scope, [d-final-conditions](r2-ln-design.md#d-final-conditions)).
- On [d-key1-vs-key2](lineage-review/synthesis.md#d-key1-vs-key2): the response gets content of its own, the structure and limit from the priors, which no corpus-rarity measure has; the evaluator borrows it as a coordinate system and keeps the corpus as its target. If demand descriptions add nothing beyond the star and the organisation descriptions, property 7 drops them.

<a id="p-op-body-r"></a>**Property 3, revised (proposed). Demand is a state with stated priors.** Load is the output of the demand machine ([p-demand-machine](#p-demand-machine)): per finger and per hand, strain in seconds with exponential decay, impulses rising without bound near a limit for each relation, coordination within and between hands, occupancy while held, releases as actions placed against heads on every lane. Lanes are never pooled. Parameters come from sources or envelope bounds ([p-demand-identification](#p-demand-identification)), never from a fit to corpus density or to the star. Checks: replay, a cached state and a split advance agree (formulation, "Dynamics and reproducibility"); mirror swaps the hands' outputs; one lesion test per relation; a 16 s single-column 12 Hz run lies past the edge at every star while the star calculator gives it 4.14; demand descriptions predict something the star does not (property 7). Answers, beyond those of [p-op-body](#p-op-body): `le-star` directly, [s-response-blind](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-response-blind), [s-star-calc-linear](#s-star-calc-linear).

<a id="p-op-frontier"></a>**Property 8 (proposed). Distance to the edge is measured at the edge.** Each action is described by its slack against the frontier at that moment ([p-frontier-slack](#p-frontier-slack)), so the description resolves where the limit lives. No quantile cells over the tail (Astra's four cells merged it, [r-astra-review](#r-astra-review)). The per-star envelope is estimated with song-bootstrap intervals that widen where evidence is thin. An action at or past the edge is not cut off: the divergence counts it at its corpus rate at the key star, over passages, so rate, duration and recovery enter ([p-op-divergence](#p-op-divergence)). Answers `le-extremes`, `cv-smoothed`, `cv-fixed-gap`.

<a id="s-demand-priors-sources"></a>**Observation, 2026-10-03, from a fresh docs-researcher subagent of this session ([demand-priors-sources](demand-priors-sources.md), about 38 sources). The main thread checked its osu! constants against the repository's calculator (they match) and did not re-check the peer-reviewed sources.**

- Sustained single-finger maximum: 5.7 to 6.9 Hz (inter-tap 145 to 175 ms), pianists up to 8.1 Hz (123 ms); index fastest, ring slowest. Peer-reviewed, several studies.
- A two-finger trill within one hand reaches about 1.5 times one finger's rate, and each finger is slower than when tapping alone. Peer-reviewed abstracts. No source gives a rate for alternation between hands.
- Fatigue in maximal tapping is central, not muscular: decline starts at 7 to 9 s, reaches 17% to 27% by 20 to 30 s and about 40% at a plateau after about 3 min; recovery is roughly linear and mostly done within 20 s. No fitted time constant. Four peer-reviewed studies; measured under continuous maximal effort, not intermittent play.
- No physiology source for a cost that rises steeply as a gap nears a limit. Critical power is established for force and power, not for tapping rate.
- Community calculators: osu! is linear in rate with exponential strain decay ([s-star-calc-linear](#s-star-calc-linear)); Etterna's MinaCalc models hands separately on 0.5 s intervals, is linear in rate, floors jack intervals at 95 ms and has a linear stamina accumulator; Quaver is near-linear and clamped; the undeployed SR Rebirth uses a same-column cost of order $\delta^{-2}$, a cross-column term below about 71 ms, and releases as actions. Tap equal to LN head, hold occupancy and release as an action are code conventions only. All constants were set by hand; no calculator has a published calibration against player scores or replays.

<a id="t-lab-vs-corpus-gaps"></a>**Tension, 2026-10-03.** Astra's mean 1st percentile of same-finger gaps at 5 to 6 stars, 107 ms ([s-astra-operators](#s-astra-operators); not accepted as an operator, a corpus measurement), is shorter than every sustained lab maximum (123 ms at best). Ranked charts contain short same-finger runs faster than people tap for seconds. Readings, not yet separated: the limit depends on run length (a two- or three-note jack is a burst); players hit jacks with wrist or arm rather than finger flexion; or the fingering assumed for the lanes does not hold. The first predicts that the shortest gaps occur only in short runs; the envelope by star and run length ([next-demand](#next-demand)) tests it.

<a id="p-demand-after-sources"></a>**Proposal revised after the sources, 2026-10-03.**

- Two history states, not one. Strain on a scale under a second (the star calculator's 0.48 s and 0.83 s; a community convention without physiological source) and central fatigue on a scale of 10 to 30 s (onset about 8 s, recovery within about 20 to 30 s; sourced). Fatigue lowers the maximal rate itself, which is the reading "the edge recedes" of [q-demand](#q-demand) item 2; the evidence is for continuous maximal tapping only.
- The limit is a function of run length: a burst limit, set from the corpus envelope at the top stars, and a sustained limit that emerges as fatigue builds. Lab maxima inform the sustained part and are not a lower bound on single gaps.
- Within a hand, alternation is a cost of its own (each finger slows in a trill), sourced in direction. Between hands, faster alternation is the human's prior without a source.
- The steep rise near the limit is the human's hypothesis, identified by neither the corpus nor the literature, and every existing calculator set its form by hand. Only player data could fix it; availability is being checked ([private, local](private/human-inputs/10fd41cc-e5d5-4d85-8def-2bee49dada16.md#prompt-2)).

<a id="p-defer-split"></a>**Agent opinion (proposed), 2026-10-03. Defer the response, keep the demand descriptions.**

- The population reading is about players, so only player data can test it; corpus and literature cannot (Astra's non-identification, [p-demand-identification](#p-demand-identification); [s-demand-priors-sources](#s-demand-priors-sources)). Aggregate data fits it better than replays: pass and fail counts per chart, and where in a chart players fail, would show how the share of players who manage falls with local demand. Replays of top players say little about that share. Self-selection (players pick charts they expect to pass) confounds pass rates; failure positions within a chart are less exposed to it.
- Without play data, defer: the cost law, fatigue as a player response, a frontier that means player capability, and difficulty control beyond the star. These are node [response](../RESEARCH.md#response); it would stay open with that as its reopening condition.
- Keep in the evaluator now, because none of it needs a cost law: per-finger and per-hand gaps for each relation, run lengths, release placement against all lanes, and history on declared time scales (under a second, and 10 to 30 s), all as descriptions whose normal distribution at each star comes from the corpus. These carry the failures that property 3 answers (`cv-pooled`, `cv-fixed-gap`, `le-same-column`, `le-star`). Drop from property 3 the impulse that diverges near a limit.
- Property 8 keeps its form with a different reference: slack against the corpus envelope at the key star, by run length (the mappers' frontier), not against a body limit. It needs no play data, and the 16 s single-column 12 Hz run lies far outside every star's envelope without one.
- What deferral costs: the evaluator's demand side is then a corpus-normality measure only, so the response has no content of its own yet ([d-key1-vs-key2](lineage-review/synthesis.md#d-key1-vs-key2)). That fits the evaluator's purpose ([d-evaluator-purpose](#d-evaluator-purpose)); difficulty control and the training scenario lose their grounding until play data arrives.

<a id="p-play-data-use"></a>**Agent reading (proposed), 2026-10-03. This changes the condition of [p-defer-split](#p-defer-split) from "no data" to "permission and design".**
