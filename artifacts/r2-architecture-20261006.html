ensomi · R2 chart generator · architecture snapshot, 2026-10-06

R2 generator architecture

R2 writes a 4K osu!mania chart one head row at a time. It is told when the rows are (the head skeleton), the BPM grid and the song length. It gets no audio and no release times. At each head row it picks what all four lanes do and, for lanes holding a long note, where in the gap before that row the note ends. Requests for LN share or difficulty apply over song-time scopes, and a locality rule decides which decisions may read them.

2,404,724 parameters 625 joint row actions + a release pointer 256-decision training windows 2 training phases: natural, then conditions

This stage: goal and status

Goal. A generator that writes natural charts when nothing is asked, and follows scoped requests when they are given, without changing what it does outside their scopes. Phase N trains the natural generator with no requests. Phase C then teaches it to follow requests for LN share and difficulty inside their scopes. Style comes next as a condition: the five Beatmap Lens pattern concepts of our section annotations (jack, stream, trill, tech, LN coordination).

Out of scope for this stage: audio, generating the head rows (their times are given), and long-range memory.

Confirmed

  • The decision unit: one decision per head row, a joint choice of lane codes, then release times from a candidate pointer (§3, §4).
  • Scoped requests, the request validator and rule L (§6).
  • Two training phases, natural then conditions, with an identity gate that keeps natural decisions out of the conditioning path (§5, §8).
  • Long-range memory is off: the landmark read is built but unused (§5).
  • Phase-N settings: peak learning rate 3e-4, 20k warm-up, cosine to 3e-5, 64M exposures, a checkpoint every 4M (§8).
  • Condition kinds for this stage: LN share, difficulty, and the five pattern concepts.

Under test

  • The phase-N run, training since 2026-10-06. Its checkpoints are evaluated as they come, and the selection rule of §9 picks the one phase C starts from.
  • The peak learning rate at full budget. In short pilots 3e-4, 1e-3 and 3e-3 tied; the run uses the lowest.
  • Whether phase-N states carry the pattern concepts. A first probe on an early checkpoint recognised them about as well as hand-made features but added nothing to them; it needs repeating on the selected checkpoint (§10).

Open

  • Phase C: frozen or KL-held base (frozen first is proposed), the loss weights λ_ln, λ_star and μ_star, its budget and learning rate, and the primary measure for checkpoint selection.
  • Difficulty read as a residual to the skeleton baseline (the current default) or as absolute star.
  • Size and form of the conditioning path for seven kinds (§5).
  • Pattern-concept data: which labelled sections train it, and how many levels each concept has.
  • The style module (§10, proposed).
  • Whether to redefine the decision unit so that no decision straddles a scope boundary (§6).

1At a glance

  • The unit is a head rowK head rows give K + 1 decisions. Decision k decides the LN releases in (tk−1, tk] and the new taps and LN heads at tk. A final EOS decision, fixed at T, only closes what is still held.
  • Rows are chosen jointlyEach lane takes one of 5 codes, so a row is one of 5⁴ = 625 actions, scored together by a low-rank joint head and masked to legal ones.
  • Releases come from a pointerRelease times are picked from a finite set of grid-snapped candidates in the gap, lane by lane, averaged over both mirror orientations.
  • Memory is a causal TCNDilated convolutions over committed rows see the last 511 rows. The TCN holds 83% of the weights. A long-range landmark read over every 64th row exists in the code but is switched off in this stage.
  • Conditions are scopedLN share and difficulty (tiled star) targets apply over [a, b) in song time. They enter by FiLM, only for decisions that rule L lets read them.
  • Training has two phasesPhase N learns natural structure by teacher-forced CE on 256-decision windows of ranked and loved 2–6★ charts. Phase C then trains condition-following on top, with the natural part frozen or held near phase N by a KL term.

3The decision unit

Decision k belongs to head row k. It decides every LN release in (tk−1, tk], on the row or inside the gap, together with the new taps and LN heads at tk. Inside the model this happens in two steps. One joint choice over the four lane codes (625 actions) fixes, for each held lane, whether it keeps holding, releases on the row or releases in the gap, and for each lane whether a tap or LN head starts at tk. Then the release time of each lane that releases in the gap is picked from the candidates, lane by lane (§4). Decision 0 has no gap. The EOS decision sits at the song length T, which is given: its row action is forced, and only the release times of lanes still held are chosen. With nothing held it outputs nothing. Every row must place at least one head. common.py, state.py, sampling.py

4Release times

A release is never a free real number. For a gap (a, b) of at least 2 ms, the candidates are the 1/32-beat and 1/24-beat grid positions strictly inside it, the midpoint, and the points 1/8 and 1/4 beat before the next row, each rounded to the millisecond. The midpoint guarantees the set is never empty. In training, source releases snap to the nearest candidate, and a release exactly on the next row becomes code 1. candidates.py, cache.py

Several lanes closing in one gap

A chord can release together or apart. The lanes that close in a gap are scored one after another as directed factors. Each factor sees where earlier releases of the same decision landed, and which lanes release on the row, through 28 relation features per candidate (4 lane slots × 7). The order is lane 0 → 3 in one orientation and 3 → 0 in the mirrored one, and the release likelihood is the average of the two orientations:

log Q = logsumexp( Σ log q_fwd , Σ log q_mirrored ) − log 2 (0 when nothing closes in the gap)

That makes a decision's probability exactly mirror-equivariant. Sampling flips a fair coin for the orientation, then samples lane by lane in its order. model.py, sampling.py, tests/r2/test_mirror.py

5The network

One forward pass scores a window of decisions in teacher forcing; generation runs the same modules one decision at a time with a cached TCN. The two hands are processed in parallel with shared weights: hand 0 lists lanes 0, 1, 2, 3 and hand 1 lists 3, 2, 1, 0, so mirroring a chart swaps the hands. model.py, common.py

Conditioning capacity for seven kinds open

Today one shared FiLM serves both kinds: 68 → 128 → 256, 42,112 parameters. Its width and depth are configurable; 512 wide with 2 layers gives 429,568. Seven kinds (LN share, difficulty, five pattern concepts) need more than today's size. Two forms are on the table:

  • Per-kind adapters. Each kind has its own MLP from its own frame to (γ, δ), with a zero-initialised output, gated by its own rule-L read bit, and the modulations are summed. A kind added later trains alone, and decisions without it stay bit-identical. Kinds that are active together interact only through the nonlinear row head and pointer downstream.
  • A wider shared FiLM over all seven frames. It can learn interactions between kinds, but it loses that plug-in isolation.

The style proposal in §10 uses the per-kind form for the five concepts and keeps today's FiLM for LN share and difficulty.

6Requests, scopes and rule L

A request

  • A scope [a, b) in song ms, 0 ≤ a < b ≤ T.
  • At most one target per property: LN share in [0, 1], difficulty as tiled star over a scope of at least 30 s, or both on the same scope.
  • Strength fixed at the default. Ranges are refused.
  • Style, demand, priority and transitions must be empty; they are reserved and raise when set (the proposed style request is in §10).

request_set.py

The request set refuses

  • a request added at or after its own start (the committed frontier must be before a);
  • two targets for the same property with overlapping scopes;
  • withdrawing or replacing a request once its scope has started.

Requests for different properties may also have different scopes that partly overlap; the record lists them as co-active. Flags mark a scope with no head row, an undefined readout, or a length outside what training covered (LN 8–512 beats or whole song; difficulty 30/60/120 s or whole song).

request_set.py

Properties are measured one way everywhere

One module, ν = r2-nu-v1, defines both properties for labels, condition frames, validation and readouts. An object belongs to the scope of its head. LN share is LN heads over heads in the scope. Difficulty is tiled star: the scope's own objects are repeated until they fill 240 s, then scored with the 2024-10-07 mania star rating, because that rating depends on length. It is undefined under 30 s. properties.py

Rule L: a decision reads a scope only if all it can produce lies inside it

Decision k produces its gap closes and its row together. If k could close a hold before a, or produce anything at or after b, a request on [a, b) would leak outside its scope. Masking single candidates cannot fix that, because the pointer normalises over all of them. So decision k reads interval [a, b) only when its row time is inside and, if a lane is held entering k, its earliest release candidate is at or after a. EOS reads only when every candidate it has is inside. locality.py

open Rule L follows from the decision unit: a decision emits times on both sides of its row. If the unit were redefined so that no decision straddles a scope boundary, rule L would reduce to "the row time is in [a, b)". Whether to do that is open.

7Generation, step by step

  1. Refuse a baseline style; check the chart seed by replay; check that every request starts after the committed frontier.
  2. Build the effective track: one interval per target, with provenance and flags.
  3. Run the TCN over the seed's history tokens and keep its cache.
  4. For each decision k: derive held lanes and clocks, read the cache, compute query features, find V_k by rule L, build the frames, and get the hand vectors z.
  5. Score the 625 row actions, mask illegal ones, and sample with the Gumbel-max trick at temperature 1 (CPU float64, no truncation).
  6. Flip a fair coin for the orientation. For each lane that closes in the gap, in that order, score every candidate with the current placements and sample one.
  7. Commit; append history token k to the TCN cache (EOS never becomes a token).
  8. After EOS: replay to hit objects, measure every target with ν, record masking and defects (holds ≤ 60 ms, releases 1–40 ms before another lane's head), and write the record.
  9. Export .osu only if the chart is complete and the legality check passes.

sampling.py, generate.py

Operating point. The default generator is the checkpoint a frozen selection rule picks (§9). A hash pins it: training recipe, selection rule, decoding and conditioning settings. Every generation record names that hash, so any output can be traced to the exact generator. operating_point.py

8Training: two phases phase N training phase C open

Data: ranked and loved 2–6★ charts, cached one .npz per chart (head times, lane codes, snapped gap releases, grid, song length) and round-trip checked. Song groups are split into fit_train and fit_dev by a hash. splits.py, cache.py

Training is split so that chart structure and condition-following are learned separately. Phase N learns natural structure with no requests at all. Phase C starts from the phase-N model and trains condition-following. It has two modes: the natural part stays frozen, or it may move but is held near phase N by a KL term. train_ce.py, loss.py

  • Fixed divisors. N̄, N̄_ln and N̄_star are expected counts per batch, measured once from the window draw. Each window can therefore be backpropagated alone and the batch gradient is still exact. loss.py, draw_sim.py
  • LN term. It scores only the tap-versus-LN part of the row: the chosen action against the legal actions with the same head mask and release types. model.py (governed_split)
  • KL term. The KL is exact over the 625 row actions. For releases, it is taken per pointer factor on the teacher-forced context and averaged over the two orientations. It has three settings: its direction (forward or reverse), which decisions it covers (natural only or all), and whether natural decisions keep their CE. loss.py (decision_kl)
  • Importance weights. w_j reweights decisions that read no request back to the natural window distribution. They matter only where natural decisions are scored. conditions.py

Phase-C windows and targets

  • Targets come from the chart itself. For each training chart, the request values are that chart's own measured LN share and difficulty over the drawn scope, so every target is realisable.
  • LN scopes. A fresh partition of the song into 8/16/32/64-beat pieces; runs of 2–8 pieces (p 0.25); or the whole song (p 0.10).
  • Difficulty scopes. 30/60/120 s cells at three phases, or the whole song (p 0.10).
  • Dropout, before selection. All requests p 0.20, a kind p 0.25, each candidate p 0.20.
  • Aligned windows. With p 0.6 the 256-decision window starts 0–32 decisions before a request's onset. LN candidates whose value differs clearly from the preceding 64 rows get weight 3.
  • Per window. 1–3 requests of each kind, consecutive with p 0.5.

conditions.py (DrawConfig, draw_window)

Difficulty as a residual

The difficulty request is given in absolute star, but the model reads it as a residual: target − b(S), where b(S) is a frozen skeleton baseline. b(S) is a quadratic ridge on 23 head-time features of the scope (300 columns), fitted on fit_train sections. It never feeds the model. The point is that the given head rows already explain most of a section's star, so the condition carries only what the arrangement can still change. The relaxed-proxy term samples the requested section with the current model, re-scores it teacher-forced, and pushes a linear map of expected chord size, same-lane repeat rate and LN share toward the residual target. A true star rating of samples is logged every 16 steps without gradient. baseline.py, proxy.py, train_ce.py

Teacher-forced at every checkpoint: natural NLL on 64 fit_dev windows without requests. Checkpoint selection: among checkpoints that pass every guard of their phase, pick the earliest within 2 SE of the lowest natural NLL. The NLL is averaged over three checkpoints, with a song-group bootstrap. Phase N is selected this way, and the checkpoint it picks starts phase C. select.py

For phase C the primary measure is open. Under a frozen base the natural NLL cannot move, so the rule becomes "earliest passing all guards". The alternatives are NLL on the decisions that read a request, or free-run adherence on a selection panel kept apart from the reported panels.

10Style module proposed · not built

Style here means the five pattern concepts: jack, stream, trill, tech and LN coordination. The annotations label each one per section as absent, present-supporting or present-prominent. A style request names a concept, a scope [a, b) and a level. The module has to act while the chart is generated, not only classify finished sections. It must also keep what holds above: rule L, the identity gate, a frozen phase-N base, and evaluation by something other than the module itself. The proposal has two parts, both built on the frozen phase-N model.

Control path: one adapter per concept

  • Frame. A readable style request gives 14 channels per role (row time t_k, candidate time u): the level as a one-hot (3), ψ distances to a and to b in ms and beats (4 + 4), progress through the scope, log of the remaining head rows, and an active bit. An explicit "absent" is a non-zero frame, so it differs from no request.
  • Form. Each concept j has its own MLP, 28 → 128 → 128 → 256 = (γj, δj), GELU, with a zero-initialised output. One LayerNorm is shared by the five. Today's FiLM is kept for LN share and difficulty, and the terms add:
z′ = FiLM_props(z, c_props) + Σ_j g_j · ( γ_j(c_j) ⊙ LN_style(z) + δ_j(c_j) ) g_j = rule-L read bit of concept j; every term uses the same unmodulated z
  • Sites. The same two as today: both hand vectors, and every release query. The row head, pointer, legality masks and normalisers are untouched, so each decision is still an exact distribution over its legal actions and candidates, and the sampler's order is unchanged. With nothing readable, z′ = z exactly.
  • Training. Phase C with the frozen base: CE on source decisions that read a labelled scope; only the adapters move. A labelled scope that no decision may read under rule L contributes nothing to the loss.
  • Size. 53,248 per concept, 266,496 for five with the LayerNorm. The generator grows from 2,404,724 to 2,671,220 parameters.

Forecast path: a prefix critic

  • What it predicts. For each concept, the level the finished section will have, given the prefix so far plus a tentative row action or release candidate. It is a forecast about the section, not the label of one row.
  • Inputs, 1,366 wide. The phase-N hand vectors before any adapter; a scope summary (mean and max of mirror-invariant TCN outputs over the scope's committed rows, and their log count) that resets with each scope; the tentative row's code embeddings; for a release, the natural pointer query, candidate embedding, placement relations and lane slot; the scope's geometry without its target. For the forecast at the end of a section, a block holding the final decision's chosen releases per lane, because EOS never enters the TCN and its releases would otherwise be invisible. It never sees the requested level.
  • Network. LayerNorm → 128 → GELU → 15, read as 5 concepts × 3 levels; 179,643 parameters, so the generator with adapters and critic has 2,850,863. Trained by masked label CE on prefixes of real charts.
  • Use. Forecasts are logged first. Optional guidance, in the form of FUDGE, multiplies the probability of each legal row action and each release candidate by exp(β · log Q(requested level)) and renormalises, on decisions that read the request. It stays off until the critic is calibrated on generated histories and its forecasts predict independent judgments of the output.
  • Why per candidate. A score that depends only on the state before the decision adds the same amount to every action and cancels in the softmax. Guidance has to score each alternative.

Data

  • 4,714 labelled sections with 15,948 concept labels, almost all from machine annotation (about 230 sections carry human labels); 2,995 fit_train and 273 fit_dev sections match charts in the R2 training cache. How many of them a decision may read under rule L is not counted yet.
  • Sections are short: median 5.5 s, most below the 30 s minimum of the difficulty measure.

How it would be tested

  • Exact invariants. With no style request, the decision law and, for a fixed seed, the chart equal phase N's. Changing a request that no decision may read changes nothing. Explicit absent differs from unspecified. Mirror equivariance holds. Frozen weights stay bit-identical through training.
  • Independent judgment. Adherence is judged by fresh blinded human ratings, or by a scorer that shares no parameters with the generator or the critic. The critic never judges output it guided.
  • Arms at matched prefixes. Phase N alone, adapters only, guidance only, and both. Each reports adherence together with playability, and short holds and releases just before another head against source charts at matched star.
  • Representation. A first probe used an early pilot checkpoint (458k exposures, memory on). For recognising finished labelled sections, its states alone did about as well as rich hand-made features (macro F1 0.58 against 0.59; 0.25 for the majority class), but adding them to the features gained nothing: −0.01, 95% interval −0.06 to +0.06, short of the +0.03 registered in advance. An early checkpoint cannot settle the question; the probe has to be repeated on the selected phase-N checkpoint.

open Which annotation layers to admit; which concepts and levels come first; phase-C and critic budgets, label balancing and the selection criterion; and whether guidance is needed at all once the adapters are trained.
