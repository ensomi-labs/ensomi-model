What we learned on 7 October, and what runs tonight

This is the sequel to the architecture snapshot of 6 October. That page described R2 and left phase N training. This one covers the day that followed. Phase N finished, and its checkpoint selection failed. The reason turned out to be the chart-level long-note level, not the release mechanics, and the guard that flagged it was itself built on a false premise. Difficulty got a new measure that works on short scopes. Tonight a fine-tune gives the natural model a chart-level long-note commitment, and a difficulty-controller pilot runs on top of it. All times are UTC.

Summary

What we learned today

  • Phase N finished at 64M exposures, and the original selection rule picked no checkpoint. Every checkpoint failed the guard on short holds and near-head releases, by 3–9× and 2–12×.
  • The cause is the chart-level LN level. From the start of a song, the model's LN amount for a chart is unrelated to the real chart's, and the model does not hold it along the song. Release timing is calibrated, the representation is faithful, and the hypothesis that "keep holding" is under-learned is refuted.
  • The guard was wrong too. Real charts do contain short holds: 2.72% of all holds, 7.0% at 5★. With per-band references, only model-made holds counted, and a new LN-drift guard, selection picks the 56M checkpoint. The 60 ms decoding mask is off by default.
  • Without a hint, phase N draws a nearly data-like LN level for the first 128 rows, then lets it wander. It does not misread its own history. Per star band, the LN-share distribution has the wrong shape. LN length is right on average, but per-chart length styles are missing.
  • Difficulty has a short-scope measure, the skeleton-relative attack strain r. Real charts sit at a median r of 1.43 at every scope length from 2 to 60 s. A tilt on the head-mask choice moves r, and an analytic controller reaches requested values. Two blind judges perceive its direction once two charts differ by about 0.2 in log r. Above the natural range, r keeps rising into unplayable four-key chord walls.

What runs tonight

  1. LN fine-tune from 56M, 12M exposures, with two chart-level inputs: whole-song LN share and median LN length in beats. At generation, both come together from one real training chart of the same star band and density. In training they are dropped to "unknown" 30% of the time. The inputs are read through zero-initialised weights, so the fine-tune starts exactly at 56M.
  2. LN evaluation of the final checkpoint: per-band share and length scorecards, persistence along the song, a fixed-level sweep and one oracle pass, all scored against criteria fixed in advance.
  3. A difficulty pilot on the fine-tuned model: a small controller learns to imitate the best analytic controller (NB) on real chart states. Requests stay inside the natural r range and at least 20% away from natural, on scopes of 2–16 s.

The three run as one unattended chain, planned to end around 23:00 UTC. Nothing on this page reports their results.

0Where we stood after the architecture page

R2 writes a four-lane (4K) osu!mania chart one head row at a time. It is given the head skeleton (the times of the rows where at least one note starts), the BPM grid and the song length, and no audio. At each head row it jointly picks what all four lanes do (625 row actions), and for lanes that are holding a long note it picks where the note ends from grid candidates in the gap before that row. The network is about 2.4M parameters, mostly a causal convolution over the last 511 committed rows. Long-range memory is off.

On 6 October, phase N was training: 64M exposures, peak learning rate 3e-4 cosine to 3e-5, a checkpoint every 4M. Its selection rule was fixed in advance. Among checkpoints that pass every guard, it takes the earliest within two standard errors of the lowest natural NLL, averaged over three checkpoints. The guards were: legality; (i) a continuation of a real chart from one third in keeps its LN share within ±0.05 of the real continuation; (iii) the model's LN forecast over 64 decisions agrees within 0.05 on real and on its own history; (iv) holds of 60 ms or less are at most 0.5% of holds, and releases 1–40 ms before another lane's head are at most the real panel's rate.

Open were phase C's form (frozen or KL-held natural model), the difficulty request (absolute star, or star read as a residual against a skeleton baseline, on scopes of at least 30 s) and the style module. The expectation was simple. Selection would pick a checkpoint that passes its guards, it would become phase C's frozen base, and phase C would train LN-share and difficulty requests on it. Neither step went as expected.

Terms used on this page

Head row, head skeleton
A time at which at least one note starts. The skeleton is the list of these times; it is given, and R2 decides what each row contains.
LN (long note), hold, tap
An LN is pressed at its head and held until its release. A tap is a single press. "Hold" and "LN" are used interchangeably.
Release
The end of an LN. R2 picks it from grid-snapped candidates in the gap before a later head row, or exactly on that row.
Short hold, near-head release
A short hold lasts under 60 ms (the original guard counted ≤ 60 ms). A near-head release ends 1–40 ms before another lane's next head, which is hard to play cleanly.
LN share, LN level
LN heads over all heads, over a span or the whole song. "Level" means a chart's whole-song LN share.
Star band
The chart's osu!mania star rating, floored and clamped to 2–5. The corpus is ranked and loved 2–6★ charts, so band 5 holds everything from 5★ up.
Phase N, phase C
Phase N trains the natural generator with no requests. Phase C then trains request-following (LN share, difficulty, later style) on top of a frozen or KL-held phase-N model.
Scope
The song-time interval [a, b) a request applies to. A locality rule lets only decisions whose output lies inside the scope read it.
Exposure, "48M"
One scored head decision in training. "48M" is the checkpoint after 48 million.
Free run: BOS run, prefix run
The model generates from its own samples. A BOS run writes the whole chart from the beginning of the song; a prefix run continues a real chart from one third in.
Teacher-forced
The model scores the real chart's decisions given the real history, as in training.
fit_train, fit_dev; panel; set F
Training and development splits by song group. The selection panel is 16 development charts used at every checkpoint. Set F is 116 development charts: the panel plus 25 per band.

1Phase N ended, and selection picked nothing

Problem

Natural NLL per decision on development charts flattened from 40M and rose slightly at the end: 2.130 (24M), 2.089 (32M), 2.071 (40M), 2.070 (48M), 2.075 (56M), 2.084 (64M). 2The short-hold investigation

Problem

Why does phase N make short holds and near-head releases, and can training remove them without decoding masks? Three candidates: the release representation (grid candidates and snapping); a repeat of the mechanisms that broke R1, our earlier model; and exposure bias, meaning training only on real histories while generating on the model's own. We added a fourth hypothesis: on a held lane, "keep holding" (code 0) is under-learned against the release codes, so LNs end early.

Expected

If "keep" were under-learned, the model's release probability on a held lane would exceed the real charts' at young hold ages, and generated holds would be shorter than real ones overall. A representation fault would show up as the training cache or the candidate sets creating short outcomes that the raw chart files do not have.

Contradiction
  • Release decisions are calibrated. measured · strong Teacher-forced on 112 development charts (59,251 held-lane states), the release probability by elapsed hold length is 0.81–1.06× the real rate above 30 ms, and below it at 30–150 ms. Hold age is an input of the row decision, and "keep" carries no different weight in the loss. The only excess is a thin tail: releases of holds 30 ms old or younger, 1.9% against 0 over 884 states, worth under 0.1% of holds. The keep hypothesis is refuted.
  • Generated hold lengths match the real ones. The median is 162–167 ms, against 175 ms in the panel's real charts and 167 ms in the corpus. Holds of 60 ms or less are 1.0–1.7% of generated holds, below the real panel's own 5.8%.
  • The representation is faithful. measured · strong Raw chart files and the training cache agree on all 358,953 development-set holds. Snapping releases to candidates removes about 1.5% of short holds and creates almost none. Teacher-forced, the model puts 14–15% less probability on short and near-head outcomes than real charts use.

Analysis

Where the defects come from. measured From the start of a song, each free run's LN amount is unrelated to the real chart's. Over the 16 panel charts the correlation is 0.00 at 48M and −0.16 at 64M; given the real chart's first third it rises to 0.84–0.85. Three dense 5★ charts whose mappers used few LNs carry 86–89% of the failures. On one of them, a 5.6★ chart with 30 LNs, the model writes about 530 per seed. Once an LN is born on a row followed by another within 60 ms, a short hold or near-head release follows at the real-chart rate for that context, or below it.

A control panel. measured · moderate On 48 other random development charts, the same 48M checkpoint passes guard (iv) as written from the start of the song: 0.40% short holds, and 0.59% near-head releases against these charts' own 0.98%. Its free LN level happens to sit below the real one there. The failure is specific to dense charts that real mappers kept light. One seed per chart.

Against earlier models. R2 avoids R1's two timing mechanisms: release-only events are 72% of generated near-head releases against 74% in real charts, and holds of 40 ms or less occur at the corpus rate. It repeats R1's third mechanism in milder form: too many LNs on dense rows, with an LN level that is not tied to the chart. On the same 16 charts R2 v1 is worse, at 5.3% short holds against 1.7%. Its earlier "at most 0.3%" came from an easy four-chart panel, where phase N also stays at 0.26% or below. Phase N is not a regression.

So guard (iv) could not be met by a model that matched the data. Its 0.5% ceiling sat below the panel's own 5.8%. Reason

The cause is the chart-level LN level. Phase N has no way to know or commit to a chart's LN amount from the start, so dense charts that real mappers kept light receive mid-range LN levels, and the extra LNs on dense rows produce the defects at ordinary per-opportunity rates. Release decisions and the representation are not at fault, and "keep" is learned. measured

Decided: the 60 ms mask is off by default (the option stays in code), guard (iv) is corrected, and the natural model gets a chart-level LN commitment, first as a fine-tune test. decided

Not established at this point: the causal step itself. The prefix contrast is observational, because a real prefix carries the LN level and also the rest of the chart's style. inferred

4The whole-song LN hint: fix, then diagnostic, then the goal

The remedy the short-hold investigation ranked first is a chart-level LN commitment: give phase N the whole-song LN share as an input, drop it to "unknown" in some training windows, and draw it from a data prior when generating from scratch. What that input is allowed to be changed twice during the day.

  1. ~12:30Proposed fix

    A whole-song LN-level input to phase N, zero-initialised, dropped to unknown in training, drawn from a prior at generation. Fine-tune about 8–16M exposures as a test; a full retrain only if it works.

  2. 12:50Diagnostic only

    We wanted the natural model to produce a normal LN distribution without being told the chart's LN share. The input stays behind an off-by-default flag, its oracle mode may be used once as a probe, and a new investigation asks why the hint-free model misses.

  3. 14:10The goal is distributional

    Generated charts should have LN share and LN length distributions like the corpus, per star band, not a per-chart match to a real chart. A chart-level variable the model draws itself may ship. A level copied from a real chart (oracle) is a hint and stays a diagnostic.

Problem

Which kind of chart-level input counts as a fair part of the natural model, and which counts as a hint that leaks the answer?

Expected

That a well-trained natural model would learn the corpus's LN distribution from the skeleton and its own history, with no chart-level input at all.

Contradiction

The data side of the new investigation showed that head times explain only about 17% of a chart's LN level (R² 0.17). About 83% of the variance is the mapper's choice. A hint-free model therefore has to draw a level and then commit to it; it cannot predict it from the skeleton. measured

Analysis

Drawing a level and conditioning the chart on it is an ordinary factorisation of the same generative model, p(level | skeleton) · p(chart | level, skeleton). It uses no information from outside the model. Copying the level from a real chart does. Training can feed each chart's true level because the level is a function of the chart, so no unsupervised latent is needed to learn the second factor. our reading

Reason

Decided: the target is per-band distributions of LN share and LN length; prior mode may ship; oracle stays a diagnostic. This made the level input in prior mode a candidate fix again, and evaluation per band against development charts became the scorecard. decided

5The hint-free LN investigation

Problem

Why does phase N not produce corpus-like LN distributions without a hint? We tested four mechanisms on 48M and 64M, with no training: how much the head skeleton reveals the level; whether the drawn level is compressed and whether it is held; whether the model misreads its own history as LN-heavier; and whether LNs are placed right within a level. LN length was added once the goal named it. Runs: set F (116 development charts) × 3 seeds from the start of the song, 348 runs per checkpoint.

6Difficulty: a short-scope measure, measured

6.1 Why exact star labels and tiled star were dropped

Problem

The plan of 6 October requested difficulty in absolute star and read it as a residual against a skeleton baseline b(S): a ridge fit of typical star on 23 head-time features, over fixed 30, 60 and 120 s cells. Star itself was computed by repeating a scope's objects until they fill 240 s and scoring that with the osu!mania star rating, which depends on length. It was undefined under 30 s.

Expected

That the residual target would carry only what the arrangement can still change, since the given head rows explain most of a section's star.

Contradiction

The star rating is a whole-song measure: strain peaks over 400 ms sections with top-weighted aggregation. Tiling a scope to 240 s is an artefact of making it run on short spans, and it gives nothing at all under 30 s. Requests have to work over varied scope lengths, and the style sections we want to combine with them are short, at a median of 5.5 s. Exact labels precomputed on real-chart cells would also condition phase C on source-chart cell values.

Analysis

A redesign that treats phase C as a finite-horizon controller trained on the outcomes of its own continuations was considered. Its cost was measured on our hardware. The sampler runs at 100–220 decisions/s per process and slows along a chart. A loop that samples a 60 s scope, scores it and re-scores it with gradients completes about 700 scopes per hour per process. That is about 20× fewer decisions per hour than teacher-forced training, and sampling is about 90% of the cost. measured Outcome training at scale would need a faster sampler first. Analytic controllers and cheap imitation come before it.

Reason

Out: exact difficulty labels of real-chart cells, and the star rating as the scoped metric. In: a skeleton-derived difficulty proxy, preferably an extension of the strain-based osu!mania algorithm that works on short intervals. An earlier record of this decision, which rejected any skeleton-derived reference, was corrected the same day. decided

6.2 The relative attack-strain proxy ras-v1

The head times define a reference workload and the arrangement R2 writes defines the actual one; the request is their ratio. Four lane strains x and one overall strain g decay between head rows with osu!mania's bases. A row's head mask q (which lanes start a note, tap or LN alike; chord size m) adds to them. Each row is charged the rise it causes in a quadratic potential:

x_l(t) = x_l(t₀)·e^(−(t−t₀)/τ_I) g(t) = g(t₀)·e^(−(t−t₀)/τ_G) τ_I = −1/ln 0.125 ≈ 0.481 s τ_G = −1/ln 0.30 ≈ 0.831 s row with head mask q ∈ {0,1}⁴, m = Σq: x ← x + q, g ← g + m Ψ = (w_I·τ_I/2)·Σ_l x_l² + (w_G·τ_G/2)·g² w_I = 1, w_G = 0.25 ΔW(q) = Ψ⁺ − Ψ⁻ = w_I·τ_I·(x⁻·q + m/2) + w_G·τ_G·(g⁻·m + m²/2) W(S) = Σ ΔW over head rows with t ∈ [a, b) W_H(S) = the same for one tap per row, cycling lanes 0, 2, 1, 3 from the chart's first row r(S) = √( W(S) / W_H(S) )
  • Why it is additive. Between rows the potential decays as dΨ/dt = −(w_I·Σx² + w_G·g²). The jump a row causes therefore equals the time integral of the squared strain it adds, until that strain has decayed. Summed over a chart, the charges equal ∫(w_I·Σx² + w_G·g²)dt, and each charge belongs to the row that caused it. W over adjacent scopes adds exactly. A burst placed just before a scope boundary is charged to the scope it is in, and the remaining budget r*²·W_H − W_committed is exact at every decision. Strain carries across boundaries; nothing resets.
  • What it keeps from osu!mania. It keeps the decay bases and the split into per-lane and overall strain. It replaces the 400 ms section peaks and top-weighted aggregation with the workload potential, and sums squared lane strains where osu! takes the highest lane. It drops osu!'s hold terms, so LN-heavy passages read as easy. Attacks only, for version 1, is our decision. decided
  • Why a skeleton-relative scale. The square root returns from squared strain to strain units. The reference is near the least workload a skeleton allows, so r ≈ 1 is close to the floor and density sits in W_H. r measures the arrangement given the skeleton, not the song's difficulty. decided our reading of the scale
  • The actuator. Every row places at least one head, so the 15 non-empty head masks cover the choice. A tilt reweights them, P(q) ∝ P₀(q)·exp(η·ΔW(q)/s_S), where s_S is the scope's mean reference charge per row. It enters as a bias on the 625 row logits, so within a head mask the base's own choice between tap and LN, and its release categories, stay natural. At η = 0 sampling is byte-identical to the base, and only decisions allowed to read the request are tilted.

Problem

Reason

6.4 The blind A/B screen

Problem

Does r order charts the way players feel difficulty? There were 48 pairs of 8–16 s windows, each pair on the same skeleton and prefix: 16 opposite-sign tilts; 16 pairs of natural phase-N samples whose r differs by at least the natural interquartile range; 8 constructed contrasts (cycled against repeated-lane singles, singles against four-key chords); and 8 near-equal controls with |Δlog r| ≤ 0.03. LN-head share was matched within 0.10. Sides were randomised and the key sealed. The pairs come from 17 real charts, and samples are reused across pairs.

Two judges answered "which side is harder: A, B or same", with a confidence and a note. The project lead used synchronised 1× playback with segment images (48 pairs in 32 minutes). That judges would follow the direction of r on clear gaps and call near-equal pairs "same". That was the screen's purpose, and it is how it came out. What we had not expected is what lies above the natural range.

Why the analysis predicts that it should hold the level inferred

In real charts, once the whole-song level is known, recent history adds almost nothing. For the next 256 rows' share, the last 512 rows alone give R² 0.53, the whole-song share 0.65, and both together 0.65, with weights 0.12 on history and 0.90 on the level. On real history the model already reproduces the data's response to recent LN history: the same slopes and the same weights on near and far history segments. Trained with the level known in 70% of windows, it should learn to lean on the level and give history a small weight. A chance excursion in a free run is then read against a fixed anchor and decays instead of propagating. Nothing systematic pushes against the anchor, because the model does not misread its own history; the only such effect is 64M's +8% in the last third of songs.

The length channel adds what share cannot carry. Length is mostly a within-chart choice (16–34% of variance between charts), but the between-chart part is real. A residual chart style of about 0.3 log units remains after skeleton and share, and it is the part generated charts are missing.

What it will not fix by itself

  • Skeleton coupling is only as fine as the prior's cells. Band × density tercile reproduces each band's distribution, near-zero mode and heavy tail included, by construction. It captures the skeleton dependence only partly: in band 5, the share of LN-free charts by density quartile is U-shaped (51 / 28 / 22 / 62%). Dense, LN-light charts like the panel's worst cases can still draw mid-range levels. The upgrade is a prior from skeleton neighbours, already calibrated (PIT SD 0.291). It acts only at sampling and needs no retraining.
  • One number for length. The median carries the chart's typical LN, not the rest of its length style. Band 2's thinly spread short holds may persist.
  • The reader may be under-learned. It enters through one zero-initialised linear map in the query path, for 12M exposures at a low learning rate. The logit channel reaches ±13.8 at shares of exactly 0 or 1, an unusual input scale.
  • It anchors two chart-level numbers, not exposure bias in general. A long-horizon objective on the model's own history is not part of tonight.
  • Phase C is untouched. Scoped LN-share requests are not trained, and how a chart-level level composes with a scoped request is not designed.
  • Band-5 near-head releases. This is the one cell above the real rate on both panels (4.3% against 2.4% on the random panel, from 20 events). A level fix may or may not remove it.
