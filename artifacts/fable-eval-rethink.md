# Rethinking chart evaluation from hit objects and physical time

Written 2026-10-03 by a fresh Fable subagent for main session `4259953e`. Labels: **[code]** read in the committed source, **[notes]** read in the research notes, **[cited]** a primary source named in the text, **[measured]** computed by the two jobs in the provenance line, **[estimate]** neither computed nor cited, **[reasoning]** my argument, **(proposed)** my proposal. Notes paths are relative to `.git/research-relay/notes/`; code paths to the repository root at `2044c2b`.

## Answer in one paragraph

Build the evaluator on the marked point process a chart is (press instants with a lane, hold intervals), score every event rather than every passage, and take the musical clock from the events themselves. A pulse estimator reading only head onsets recovers the red-line canonical tempo within 1% in 87.9% of 8 s windows and 83.9% of single-segment charts; nearly all the rest is a 3:2 ambiguity in which the chart is perfectly periodic at a pulse the mapper labelled differently, so the physical pulse is reliable and the mapper's name for "the beat" is not **[measured]**. Of five designs I recommend prototyping the hybrid first: an onset-inferred pulse field as the organisation clock, a small per-event conditional model of row content for idiom, and explicit per-finger body descriptors in milliseconds for the sub-50 ms and load defects that learned embeddings blur, aggregated as a continuous time field of surprisal whose integral over any span (weights in seconds, [d-song-null](evaluation-first.md#d-song-null)) and worst sub-span are compared with an empirical song null. A learned encoder comes second, as the organisation model and the population-coverage instrument, judged against the hybrid on the harness's dosed injections. Rate-not-presence is answered by the chain rule: a 28-press anchor costs its corpus rarity at the requested star, and that rarity is now measured (2.0% of 4.0 to 4.5 star charts contain one; 0.4% of 4 to 5 star charts contain one inside 4.5 s) **[measured]**. The R2 corpus, the split, the evaluation interface, the harness and the song-null decision survive; canonical beats and the red-line classifier become a diagnostic and a transform generator; bar passages are retired.

## 1. First principles <a id="first-principles"></a>

### 1.1 The object

An `.osu` chart is a finite set of objects (lane, head ms, release ms or none) **[code]** (`osu_core/hitobjects.py`). Everything the human has rejected is a functional of that set: anchors are same-lane press sequences, jacks and trills are same-finger and same-hand gaps, LN share, hold length and release crowding are functions of hold intervals and neighbouring heads, density is a rate. Red lines are an annotation on top: 150,997 of the corpus's 280,489 red lines are expressive and 4,619 ranked or loved charts carry several musical segments **[notes]** ([s-m0-m1](evaluation-first.md#s-m0-m1)); mid-bar musical lines occur in 19.5% of fit charts, ritardando chains in 10.4%, fold jumps in 4.2% **[notes]** ([fable-passage-boundaries](fable-passage-boundaries.md#catalogue)). The star is a deterministic functional of the objects **[code]** (`osu_core/difficulty.py`), which is why it can be a key. Audio is a signal the chart does not contain; by decision its relation to the chart is the timing work's question ([d-placement-is-timing](evaluation-first.md#d-placement-is-timing)).

The evaluation object stays the triple of [p-eval-object](evaluation-first.md#p-eval-object): chart, condition, scope. What changes is the coordinate system: the evaluator's clock is physical time, its musical clock is estimated from the objects (section 2.4), and a condition's grid is something to compare against, not to stand on.

### 1.2 Invariances and sensitivities

Exact invariances the evaluator must hold, checkable as output hashes: lane mirror; a shift of the whole chart; file order; green lines, hitsounds and every expressive red line; and renotation at half or double BPM, which becomes vacuous when red lines are not read. One covariance replaces "two clocks" of [p-op-clocks](operator-properties.md#p-op-clocks): under a uniform time-stretch of a few percent, every organisation quantity expressed in units of the inferred pulse is unchanged and every quantity in seconds moves in the stated direction. That is the live test of an onset-only design, and the harness already has the stretch transform (`harness/transforms.py`, docstring).

Sensitivities, from the failures table ([failures](operator-properties.md#failures)) and the lineage: rate and length of anchors (`le-extremes`), local LN concentration (`le-whole-chart`), releases 1 to 40 ms before a head on any lane (`le-same-column`), holds of 40 ms or less and near-duplicate heads ([s-ln-open-state](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-ln-open-state)), heads off any pulse ([s-no-time-coordinate](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-no-time-coordinate)), per-finger load (`cv-pooled`), sequence structure (`cv-no-transitions`), repetition and collapse in one chart and across a population, context breaks under partial scopes, and a realised difficulty far from the request (`le-label`).

### 1.3 What "no hard splitting" means

A statistic is free of hard splits when it is a continuous functional of the event set under moving any boundary. That means scoring events, not tiles: a local surprisal density s(t) defined at every time from the events around it, a scope's score the time-average of s over its scored spans, its worst part a scan over sub-spans **[reasoning]**. Any sub-span is then scorable, a continuation from t or a three-second edit included, and attribution is the field itself, in osu! timestamps. Scan statistics with an empirical null are standard (Kulldorff 1997, *Communications in Statistics* **[cited]**). The duration-weighted rate and the song null kernel-weighted in duration and star ([d-song-null](evaluation-first.md#d-song-null)) carry over unchanged: the weights in seconds become the measure dt, and the null is still made of whole songs, so within-song dependence is in it by construction.

### 1.4 What the hit objects do and do not determine about the clock

Job 1 **[measured]** (appendix): on 4,500 fit charts in 2,794 song groups, 8 s windows with hop 4 s, counted when they hold at least 16 head rows (94.8% of windows), the canonical BPM at which the rows are most phase-coherent, over four octaves and refined to 1e-5, agrees with the red-line canonical BPM in force within 1% in 87.9% of windows and within 0.3% in 85.9%; 10.0% are 3:2 confusions, 1.7% other. On the 3,570 single-segment charts estimated whole: 83.9% agree, 15.0% are 3:2, 1.0% other; when they agree the 90th percentile of the relative error is below 0.05%. The 3:2 cases are not noise: the example charts have coherence 0.93 to 1.00, so every row sits on a lattice whose period is two thirds or three halves of the mapper's beat. The hit objects fix the lattice; which level the mapper called "the beat" is a labelling that a 6/8 song, a shuffle or a habit can set either way. For charts with several musical segments the window agreement is 82.3%, the losses in windows straddling a tempo change. Heads within 2 ms of the inferred local lattice at denominators up to 16 are 94.8% against 98.6% under the red lines (6.54 million central heads); the per-chart median difference is zero and 77.8% of charts are within two points; at 5 to 6 stars the shares are 91.0% against 97.5% and the median coherence falls to 0.96. Successive row-interval ratios fall within 2% of a simple ratio (1, 2, 1/2, 3, 1/3, 4, 1/4, 3/2, 2/3, 4/3, 3/4) in 93.3% of pairs, 70.0% of them equal; 84.3% at 6 to 8 stars.

Reading **[reasoning]**: the physical pulse, its phase and the small-integer structure of intervals are recoverable from onsets with the precision the 2 ms snap needs, except in sparse or tempo-changing windows; the beat level is recoverable in about seven windows of eight and ambiguous by 3:2 in the rest. Organisation descriptions must therefore rest on the pulse lattice and interval ratios, never on a named beat or bar, unless a condition supplies one. Where this is wrong: if the human's rejections depend on the beat label (a 1/3 where a 1/4 belongs is a defect only relative to the beat), the ambiguity costs sensitivity for that family (D8), which the pre-registration tests.

## 2. Five designs <a id="designs"></a>

Each design is described against the same checklist: information, representation, scopes without splits, star key, scoring and calibration, failure-table coverage, interpretability, robustness under optimisation, compute on one M5, data needs, main risk.

### 2.1 Design A: learned encoder of event sequences in physical time <a id="design-a"></a>

**Information and representation.** Each event becomes a token: lane, type, hold length in log ms, time to the previous and next row in log ms, same-lane gap in log ms, with relative-time features between tokens, so tempo is learned and not read. An encoder (a small transformer, a VAE with a sequence decoder as in MusicVAE, or a contrastive encoder with two crops of one chart as positives) maps a window of about 8 s to a vector z. MusicVAE needed a hierarchical decoder to avoid posterior collapse (Roberts et al., ICML 2018 **[cited]**); a scorer never decodes, so a masked or contrastive objective is simpler and has no collapse to manage **[reasoning]**.

**Scoring.** Population: Fréchet distance between Gaussian fits of z, MMD (Gretton et al., JMLR 2012 **[cited]**), or the manifold precision, recall, density and coverage estimates (Kynkäänniemi et al., NeurIPS 2019; Naeem et al., ICML 2020 **[cited]**), which separate fidelity from coverage and so see collapse. FID-style statistics are biased in the sample size and the bias depends on the model (Chong and Forsyth, CVPR 2020 **[cited]**), so every population number needs the matched-n corpus-against-corpus floor of [a-fable-review](evaluation-first.md#a-fable-review). Per window: the density of z among cross-fitted corpus windows at the key, as a surprisal, slid with stride one event so that the chain of section 1.3 applies.

**Scopes, key, calibration.** Windows slide over any scored span and read the given span through a mask flag; the key enters the density, never the encoder (`le-star`); per-window ranks, the song null, two tails. **Catches:** idiom, lane-pattern and repetition defects (D6, D7, F1), population collapse (F2, F3). **Blurs:** a single 35 ms gap or one release 10 ms before a head is one token among hundreds, and pooled representations give rare events little weight **[reasoning]**; a learned density is not a reliable anomaly score for structured inputs (Nalisnick et al., ICLR 2019 **[cited]**). **Interpretability:** weak as a number, good as exemplars (the nearest corpus windows of a flagged span). **Under optimisation:** weak; a frozen encoder is an easy target. **Compute:** a four-layer, width-128 transformer trains in GPU hours per fold **[estimate]**. **Data:** about 25 million heads in the fit split **[notes]**, enough for a small encoder **[estimate]**. **Main risk:** the lineage's own: an under-trained learned component whose blind spots nobody can list ([s-undertrained](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-undertrained), `le-tuned`).

### 2.2 Design B: per-event conditional likelihood with a sliding context <a id="design-b"></a>

**Information and representation.** Score every event by its probability given its surroundings: p(row content at t_i | events within W seconds on both sides, key), and, when times are free, p(t_i | the rest) as a conditional intensity, which is the neural temporal point process setting (Shchur et al., IJCAI 2021 **[cited]**). A finished chart is judged, so the model is bidirectional, which is the first difference from the generator. Two model families fit the description: a smoothed back-off n-gram over mirror-canonical row tokens with interval classes in pulse units (minutes to fit on CPU), and a masked event transformer (hours on the GPU). Donahue et al. (ICML 2017 **[cited]**) found that a conditional LSTM for step selection in DDR charts substantially outperformed n-gram and fixed-window models, which is evidence that idiom in chart rows carries beyond short contexts.

**How it differs from the generator.** The generator is causal, conditioned on audio or a skeleton, trained by row cross-entropy and soon by preferences ([d-beyond-row-ce](r1-verdict.md#d-beyond-row-ce)); the evaluator is non-causal, conditioned on the requested star, fitted on the corpus alone and frozen. Both are fitted to the same corpus, so what neither can represent is invisible to both; that shared blind spot is why design C's body descriptors and the harness's external injections are needed beside it **[reasoning]**. Training circularity does not arise: the evaluator never sees generated outputs; preference pairs are scored, not fitted.

**Scoring and calibration.** Per event, a surprisal per factor (lane set, type, release offset, time) and its rank among cross-fitted corpus events at the key; for the time factor the time-rescaling theorem (Brown et al., *Neural Computation* 2002 **[cited]**) gives a per-chart goodness-of-fit test: if the intensity is right, rescaled inter-event times are independent unit exponentials. Mean surprisal rewards collapse, since the mean of −log p over a generator's events is H(Q) + KL(Q‖P), below the corpus's own H(P) when the generator's entropy is lower **[notes]** ([a-fable-review](evaluation-first.md#a-fable-review)); hence per-event ranks with both tails and a population coverage view. **Scopes:** native; scored events take context from given spans, and a condition that fixes head times removes the time factor. **Key:** star as an input. **Catches:** lane and chord choice, LN choice against density, release offsets given context, idiom and repetition within W, and the chain-rule answer to rate-not-presence (section 3). **Misses:** structure beyond W; the ms tail of releases and holds unless the time factor is modelled at ms resolution with features of the next head on every lane, and even then rare events get little gradient. **Interpretability:** good: a surprisal per event per factor is a "where", the model's expected alternatives a "why". **Under optimisation:** moderate; the low tail rewards mode-seeking, guarded by the high tail and coverage kept out of the preference signal. **Compute:** n-gram minutes, transformer hours per fold **[estimate]**. **Main risk:** the Nalisnick failure plus under-training, both invisible without the harness.

### 2.3 Design C: multiscale continuous-time descriptor fields <a id="design-c"></a>

**Information and representation.** At every event, descriptors from kernel-weighted neighbourhoods at scales of about 0.5, 2, 8 and 32 s (press rate per lane and hand, LN occupancy per lane, chord rate, dispersion of log intervals, pulse period and coherence from design D, hand balance under mirror canonicalisation, lane-transition entropy, self-similarity at lags) and exact per-event quantities that must not be smoothed (same-finger gap in ms, run length so far, release-to-next-head gap on each hand relation, hold length). The model of normal is a conditional density of the descriptor vector at the key, by nearest neighbours or kernels on subsampled cross-fitted fit events, giving a per-event surprisal and the same chain.

This resembles Control V3 ([s-control-v3](operator-properties.md#s-control-v3)), so the differences must be explicit: Control V3 was an uncalibrated per-frame regression target (`cv-pointwise`, `cv-uncalibrated`) at one smoothing scale (`cv-smoothed`), with pooled lanes (`cv-pooled`), a fixed jack gap (`cv-fixed-gap`) and no musical clock (`cv-seconds`). Here lanes are per finger, gaps are continuous, the scales include the exact event, the pulse supplies the musical clock, and the harness measures floor and sensitivity. **Catches:** density and LN concentration (D4, D9), load and sub-50 ms defects (D2 in ms, D3, F7), anchors by run length and gap (D1), off-pulse heads (D5), hand permutation through balance (D6, weakly). **Misses:** idiom in the row sequence (D7 only through entropy), repetition beyond self-similarity. **Interpretability:** best of all: every descriptor has a name, a unit, a time and a scale. **Under optimisation:** a generator can match every marginal and still be wrong in sequence ([a-fable-review](evaluation-first.md#a-fable-review)). **Compute:** minutes in numpy; densities on a few million subsampled events in under an hour **[estimate]**. **Main risk:** the add-a-descriptor-per-complaint loop (`le-tuned`) and multiplicity.

### 2.4 Design D: pulse and phase inferred from the hit objects <a id="design-d"></a>

**What it is.** Not a complete evaluator: the coordinate system the other designs read in place of red lines. For each window, the period at which the head rows are most phase-coherent (the circular statistic |mean exp(2πi t/P)| over a grid of periods spanning the canonical range and four octaves, refined locally), its phase, and its coherence in [0, 1]; per event, the residual to the local lattice in ms and the interval to the neighbouring rows in lattice units. The method is a symbolic-onset relative of inter-onset-interval clustering (Dixon 2001 **[cited]**) and of tempo estimation by autocorrelation with dynamic-programming beat tracking (Ellis, *JNMR* 2007 **[cited]**), and is far easier than the audio case because the onsets are exact. Cost: about 10 ms per window; 4,500 charts in 211 s on eight workers **[measured]**.

**What it gives.** Renotation and expressive-line invariance for free; no mid-bar, chain or fold-jump cases, because there is no segment; a continuous key covariate, the lattice period in ms, without the 80/160 fold edge the passage memo could not avoid ([p-key](fable-passage-boundaries.md#p-key)); a measurement of off-grid heads with no grid supplied (coherence falls, residuals widen; the lineage's exported 120 BPM, `le-grid`, becomes irrelevant); and, under a `Timing` condition, a diagnostic of whether the output followed the grid it was given. **What it does not give:** the mapper's beat level in about one window of eight, bar lines, and anything in windows under about 16 rows (5.2%), where any grid is weakly determined. **Where it fails:** triplet or swing passages (3:2), polyrhythm, free-time intros, windows straddling a tempo change (82.3% against 89.5% agreement **[measured]**); tempo tracking across windows would recover part of that and is not needed first **[reasoning]**. **Its parameters** (window, hop, minimum rows, coherence as confidence) are estimator settings, reported and swept like τ in the passage memo; the coherence is a continuous covariate, never a gate ([d-no-cutoffs](operator-properties.md#d-no-cutoffs)).

### 2.5 Design E: the hybrid <a id="design-e"></a>

(proposed) Design D as the clock; design B in its cheap form (a smoothed back-off model of mirror-canonical row tokens given two previous rows and the interval ratios in pulse units, forward and backward averaged, star as a kernel key) for organisation; design C's exact per-event body descriptors, each with a small explicit conditional density keyed by star and, where it matters, by the local pulse period (same-finger gap by run length so far; release-to-next-head gap by hand relation and hold length; hold length by pulse period; press rate per hand at 2 and 16 s; LN occupancy at 16 s; pulse residual and coherence). One chain: per-event surprisal per family, cross-fitted rank at the key, a time field per family by kernel smoothing of −log u in seconds, per scope the time-average and the worst 4 s and 16 s sub-span, families combined through the empirical null of the minimum p-value, both tails for the organisation family, and a coverage view on the descriptor vectors with the matched-n floor. Explicit densities say "zero corpus mass here" with a Dirichlet interval, which a smooth learned density cannot; the row model carries idiom that marginals miss; the pulse makes both independent of red lines. Design A joins later as the organisation model and the latent space for coverage, if it beats the row model on the harness (section 6).

## 3. Rate-not-presence, concretely <a id="rate"></a>

Job 2 **[measured]** (17,103 fit charts, 4,817 groups): a lane present in at least 28 of 32 consecutive head rows, the R1 rule's anchor, occurs in 0.1% of 2 to 3 star charts, 0.7% at 3 to 4, 2.7% at 4 to 5, 8.0% at 5 to 6 and 22.6% at 6 to 8; 2.0% at 4.0 to 4.5 stars, at 0.015 such windows per minute. The span of those 32 rows is 5.8 s at the median at 4 to 5 stars and 4.4 s at 5 to 6; an anchor of 28 inside 4.5 s, the human's example ([s-ranked-contains-rejected](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-ranked-contains-rejected)), is in 0.4% of 4 to 5 star charts, 3.1% at 5 to 6 and 15.5% at 6 to 8. In ms-only run terms, a same-lane run of 16 or more presses at gaps of 170 ms or less is in 0.4%, 4.3%, 24.5% and 57.5% of charts from 3 to 4 stars up to 6 to 8, and a run of 28 or more in 0.1%, 0.6%, 5.1% and 25.8%. So the ranked 4.07 star chart with a 28-attack anchor sits at about the half-percent tail of its star: inside the population, as the human said, and rare.

How each design turns that into a score for a generated chart at a 4 star request:

- **A.** The anchor window's embedding sits where the corpus's few anchor windows sit; its density at 4 stars is low, giving one low rank among the chart's windows. One such window is inside the song null; three in one song are not. Rate enters by counting windows; length enters only as far as the encoder represents it.
- **B.** The chain rule: the sum of per-event surprisals over the run is −log P(run of that length at that gap | key) under the model, since each continuation is scored given the run so far. If the model's continuation hazard matches the corpus's, a 28-run at 4 stars costs about −log(0.02) ≈ 3.9 nats more than typical rows spread over 28 events, which one song absorbs and a repeated habit does not. Length and speed enter because the context window holds the run and its gaps; a run longer than W is seen as W-long.
- **C.** The exact run-length-so-far descriptor jointly with the current gap: the 28th press at 143 ms gaps has a known corpus density at 4 stars (job 2's joint); its surprisal is high at that one event and the field s(t) peaks there, with the peak's height the rarity and its width the duration. A 28-run at 500 ms gaps sits elsewhere in the joint and costs little.
- **D.** Supplies the gap in pulse units, so the same run reads as "every eighth" at one tempo and "every quarter" at another, which the body side needs beside the ms gap.
- **E.** Both B's and C's views, so the report can say: runs of 28 or more at gaps of 170 ms or less occur in 0.6% of 4 to 5 star charts, 0.04 per minute; this chart has three in two minutes; song rank 0.003.

The same coin's other side **[measured]**: holds of 40 ms or less are 0.4% of all fit-split LN, and 3.4% of charts have any (5.6% at 4 to 5 stars, 13.7% at 5 to 6); among charts with at least ten LN the per-chart share is zero at the 90th percentile in every band and 38% at the 99th at 4 to 5 stars, 76% at 5 to 6. The lineage's 4 to 5% ([s-ln-open-state](lineage-review/synthesis.md#s-ln-open-state)) is ten times the pooled rate yet inside the top percentile of charts, so a chart-level rate must be read against the distribution of chart-level rates at the key, with local context (tempo, density, LN section or not) as covariates, never against a pooled figure. Same-lane gaps under 50 ms are 0.006 per thousand same-lane pairs, in 0.3% of charts; under 80 ms, 1.5 per thousand at 4 to 5 stars and 5.7 at 5 to 6; the per-chart minimum same-lane gap has a 5th percentile of 71 ms pooled and 59 ms at 5 to 6 stars, in line with Astra's within-song 1st percentiles ([s-astra-operators](operator-properties.md#s-astra-operators)).

The hard comparison **[reasoning]**: A and B are the designs that could learn what nobody wrote down, and both carry the risk the lineage died of, a learned component believed without measured sensitivity. C and D are the designs whose every blind spot can be named, and C alone invites the loop of adding a descriptor per complaint. E takes C's enumerable core and D's clock, adds the smallest sequence model that answers the idiom defects, and leaves the learned encoder as a measured upgrade rather than a foundation. The passage design it replaces loses nothing that the human valued: bars as attribution give way to timestamps of field peaks, and the song null of [d-song-null](evaluation-first.md#d-song-null) is kept to the letter.

## 5. What survives of the built work <a id="survives"></a>

- **R2 corpus and split** ([p-split](evaluation-first.md#p-split), `data/r2-corpus.parquet`): unchanged; every design fits on fit, calibrates on calibration, reads held-out once.
- **Evaluation interface** (`case.py`): kept. `EvalCase.grid` becomes optional and `grid_source` gains the value `inferred`; `Timing` and `Skeleton` stay as conditions because they still fix aspects, and a condition's grid is compared with the inferred pulse as a timing diagnostic rather than used as the coordinate. `Spans`, `Scope`, `Condition`, the `judges` protocol and `evaluate`'s skipping stand; `Spans.bars` becomes a harness convenience. This qualifies [d-placement-is-timing](evaluation-first.md#d-placement-is-timing): audio fit stays the timing work's question; the grid is an optional condition, not the evaluator's foundation. What is lost: the mapper's beat label in the 3:2 windows (ledger).
- **Canonical beats and the red-line classifier** ([p-canonical-beats](evaluation-first.md#p-canonical-beats), [p-red-lines](evaluation-first.md#p-red-lines)): retired as foundation; kept as (i) the generator of the must-not-flag transforms, which an onset-only evaluator passes trivially but should still run as regression tests, (ii) a diagnostic of pulse agreement (job 1's 12% disagreement, mostly 3:2, is a reportable feature of a chart), and (iii) the coordinate the harness may use to place grid-aware injections (D8, off-grid releases), since the harness knows the truth and the evaluator does not read it. [d-fold-per-segment](evaluation-first.md#d-fold-per-segment) then concerns the harness only; the key moves from canonical BPM to the lattice period in ms, continuous, with no fold edge.
- **Calibration harness** (uncommitted, docstrings read): keep the legality checker, the dosed injections D1 to D10 and F1 to F7, the must-not-flag transforms, the cross-fitted floor, the receipts and the split access log. Retire `Bars4Placeholder` and the trivial evaluator's passage dependence; the trivial family becomes head rate in an 8 s kernel window, scored by the event chain, which also removes the 6.1% of heads the placeholder never scored. Random-phase injection placement under the sealed seed keeps its purpose.
- **Song aggregation** ([d-song-null](evaluation-first.md#d-song-null)): survives as written, with "passage" read as "time": weights in seconds, song null kernel-weighted in log duration and star, worst-run view and same-shape windows for partial scopes following when partial scopes are exercised.

## 6. Recommendation and the smallest prototype <a id="recommendation"></a>

Prototype design E first, in its cheapest form (P1), and design B's transformer second (P2) as a measured replacement of P1's row model and as the latent space for coverage. Design A as a whole is not prototyped first: it is the design whose failures are least enumerable and whose compute and training discipline the project has repeatedly under-delivered. Design C alone is not enough because of the idiom defects; design D alone is not an evaluator.

**P1, the smallest prototype that shows success or failure.** The pulse field (job 1's estimator at a 1 s hop, with per-event residuals); the row model (back-off n-gram over mirror-canonical tokens with interval ratios in lattice units, forward and backward averaged, star by kernel, order and smoothing by cross-fitted predictive likelihood on fit); five body families with explicit two-dimensional densities (same-finger gap by run length so far; release-to-next-head gap by hand relation and hold length; hold length by lattice period; press rate per hand at 2 and 16 s; LN occupancy at 16 s); the event chain with five-fold cross-fitting, the song null and the coverage view. Fitted densities as JSON histograms under 4 MB for review on the control plane. Compute: the pulse field over the fit split in about 13 minutes, five folds inside an hour on the M5 CPU **[estimate]**. Builder: a Claude worker on the mac under the harness's receipts; a fresh read-only agent recomputes the headline.

**P2.** A masked event transformer (four layers, width 128, windows of ±64 events, star as input) trained on the fit split per fold, used (i) as the organisation family in place of the n-gram and (ii) pooled, as the space for density and coverage. It is adopted only if it detects D6, D7 and F1 at lower doses than the n-gram at equal false alarm, and it never enters the preference signal before a second family set written after its freeze has been run.

### Pre-registration for P1 <a id="prereg"></a>

Data: fit split, cross-fitted by song group in five folds, for every density, reference and null; calibration split for every number below, one chart per song group per draw under the sealed seed; held-out never read. Song-group bootstrap on every reported rate. One run per claim at the stated sample; a failed threshold is reported as failed; post-hoc numbers are labelled post hoc.

1. **Coverage.** Claim: every head and release of every calibration chart receives a score under the whole-chart scope. Metric: share scored. Threshold: 100% (the placeholder: 93.9% **[notes]**).
2. **Invariance.** Claim: output hashes are identical under mirror, shift, whole and per-segment renotation, inserted expressive red lines, green lines and hitsounds, and object order, on 300 calibration charts; under a ±3% stretch the organisation family's per-event surprisals are identical to 1e-9 and the headline rank moves by less than 0.05 at the median.
3. **False alarms.** Claim: the headline is calibrated. Metric: share of calibration songs with headline rank below 0.05, by star band 1 to 6 and by duration quintile. Threshold: within [0.03, 0.08] in every cell (about 190 groups each **[notes]**).
4. **Sensitivity.** Claim: at twice the corpus-plausible dose (the blockers review's definition) the headline detects, at the 5% calibration quantile, at least 80% of 300 injected calibration songs for D1 (run length and count), D3 (both hand relations), D4, D6, D7, F4 (under the continuation scope) and F7; D2 in ms at 4% of LN at 40 ms or less, the lineage's dose; D5 at σ = 5 ms. D8 (1/4 to 1/3) at twice the plausible dose: at least 50%, reported separately because of the 3:2 ambiguity. Detection is reported per star band and per lattice-period band, never pooled.
5. **Collapse.** Claim: a population in which 30% of songs are mode charts (F2) is detected by the coverage view with the matched-n floor; the D10 loop is detected by the organisation family's high tail in at least 80% of cases.
6. **The human's witness.** Claim: the charts of 3.5 to 4.5 stars with a 28-of-32 anchor (about 56 in the fit split from job 2's half-star rates **[estimate]**, scored out of fold, plus every such calibration chart) pass (headline rank at or above 0.05) in at least 90% of cases, and the same charts with the anchor window copied to k further positions show a monotone dose-response that crosses 80% detection by k = 8. The crossing k is reported, not pre-set.
7. **Stop rule.** Model selection by cross-fitted likelihood on fit only, fixed before calibration is read; calibration read once per claim; no parameter changed after reading it; the estimator settings (window, hop, minimum rows, kernel widths) swept on fit and reported.

**What would make me switch.** If claim 3 fails in two or more bands after one pre-registered bandwidth change, the chain is wrong before the design is. If D6 and D7 are detected below 50% at twice the plausible dose, the n-gram is too weak and P2 replaces it. If D8 cannot be detected and the owner says 1/3-for-1/4 is a defect they reject, add a beat-level resolver (a harmonic sum with a 3:2 prior, or tempo tracking across windows) or accept the condition's grid as an optional beat label for charts that carry one. If the coverage view misses F2, move coverage to P2's latent space. If the stretch test fails because the pulse estimator is unstable, the pulse becomes a feature with a confidence and the condition's grid returns as the organisation clock for charts that have one; that would be a real loss and goes to the owner.

## 7. Rejected ideas

- A VAE as the first evaluator: a decoder and a posterior-collapse problem with no benefit for scoring; a masked or contrastive encoder gives the same latent with fewer failure modes.
- Red lines as the base coordinate: the human's direction; 12% window disagreement with the onset pulse and the boundary catalogue.
- Bar passages of any length: the time field makes them unnecessary; attribution by timestamp is finer.
- Keying by canonical BPM: inherits the fold edge; the lattice period in ms is continuous.
- Mean surprisal as the headline: rewards collapse by the entropy term.
- Per-song histogram divergence: Astra's near-ceiling floor.
- Theoretical nulls: wrong under within-song dependence; the null is songs.
- The star the output reached as a key: decided against; a diagnostic view at most.
- Fitting or refitting any component on generated outputs: the evaluator would learn the generator's habits as normal.
- Hard minimum rows per window as a gate: the coherence is a continuous covariate; sparse windows widen intervals.
- Event-count windows: boundaries become the generator's to move (passage memo).
- One learned model for both organisation and load: rare ms events get no gradient; the body side stays explicit.
