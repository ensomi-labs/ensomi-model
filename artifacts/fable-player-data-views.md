# Player data views: what each carries for demand, frontier and physiology

**Answer in one paragraph.** Player data comes in about a dozen views, and they split cleanly by what they can identify. Chart-level aggregates (fail and exit bins, play and pass counts) say *where in a chart* plays end and *how often*, lagged by a health bar whose kernel is now known from lazer's code; they can check that the evaluator's demand descriptions order fail positions (U4) and nothing finer. The dump's player sample adds what the aggregates lacked: a skill coordinate per player (`rank_score`), attempts per player and chart, and best scores with mods, so the human's population prior becomes a testable chart-level claim ("among players of equal skill, the share that passes falls with the chart's distance to the edge, beyond star, and falls steeply") in an item-response design that is possible now, within the dump licence, on 1.44 million attempted pairs. Rate mods (0.75x, 1.5x, continuous rates in lazer) and lazer's Hold Off, No Release, Mirror and Invert mods are natural experiments that move every gap or change LN semantics while holding the pattern fixed, and the dump holds tens of thousands of within-player, within-chart pairs for the rate case. What no aggregate can identify is anything per action: the limit per relation, burst against sustained, the strain time scale, fatigue as a slow state, tap against LN head, release as an action. Those need replays, which give press and release times per lane at 1 ms for passed plays at every skill level in lazer; and even replays identify a *response function* (miss and error against slack, per relation and skill), never a latent cost, so I recommend defining the response as that function and dropping "cost law" as a target. Position: proceed now with the dump (one pre-registered analysis, section 7), keep the evaluator's demand side corpus-referenced as [p-defer-split](operator-properties.md#p-defer-split) proposes, and contact ppy for a replay pilot only if that analysis finds a demand signal beyond star.

## 2. Which view identifies which design part

<a id="p-identification-map"></a>For each part of [p-demand-machine](operator-properties.md#p-demand-machine) and [h-body-priors](operator-properties.md#h-body-priors): the view, the model under which it identifies the part, the confounds, and whether the part is identified, bounded or out of reach. "Identified" means: a held-out prediction of a player outcome improves over the star baseline with the term in and worsens with it out, by song-group bootstrap, with a lesion per term.

| Design part | View | Model and assumptions | Confounds | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| State structure (per finger and hand, held mask, last press and release) | V13 only | per-note outcome (miss, or error beyond a window) against state coordinates, within player; lane to finger fixed by the canonical profile | fingering unknown; offsets; mods | identified only through prediction; aggregates cannot see structure |
| Limit $g_0$ per relation (same finger F, other finger same hand H, other hand X, press during partner hold, release before next head) | V13 for the location; V5 to V7 for an upper bound | psychometric curve $P(\text{miss}\mid g, r, \theta)$ per relation; $g_0(\theta)$ is where it crosses a declared level; upper bound: the shortest gap of each relation in charts some sampled player passed clean | survivorship (passes only), best-of-many, fingering, rate mods (gaps must be mapped back to NM) | location bounded per skill quartile; a single population $g_0$ is not expected to exist (it is a distribution over players) |
| Burst against sustained (limit as a function of run length) | V13; V1 weakly | interaction of gap with the index $k$ of a press in its run; the burst reading predicts error rising in $k$ at fixed $g$ | health lag turns long runs into fails anyway (section 3); the corpus writes short runs at short gaps, so $g$ and run length are correlated | identified from replays if the same $g$ occurs at several run lengths; V1 can only show that long runs precede fails more than short ones |
| Strain scale $\tau$ (under a second) | V13 | profile likelihood over $\tau$ of a discounted recent-press term per finger | $\tau$ and run-length effects substitute for each other; separable where rests between runs vary | identified as a descriptive scale; "physiological" is a label the data cannot grant |
| Central fatigue (onset 7 to 9 s, recovery 20 to 30 s, [demand-priors-sources A3](demand-priors-sources.md)) | V13; V1 with a health model | time-in-dense-section term after local load is controlled; recovery after breaks of 20 s or more is a specific lab prediction | health lag mimics late-section fails; pauses; the lab measured continuous maximal effort, not play | identified from replays only if dense sections over 10 s exist with varied breaks; V1 cannot separate fatigue from health integration without a replay-fitted kernel |
| Steep rise near the limit ($\kappa$, $p$) | V4 to V8 at chart scale; V13 at action scale | chart scale: curvature of the pass log-odds against the chart's nearest-to-edge descriptor at fixed skill (section 7); action scale: curvature of the psychometric curve in slack | selection on unobservables biases levels, less so shapes within skill; chart length and density | the population statement is identifiable now; the per-action cost law is not identifiable even from replays (below); its observable shadow is |

| Tap = LN head | V10 (HO), V13 | HO within player and chart: accuracy with holds turned to taps against NM; replays: head error of LN heads against taps at matched gaps | HO plays are self-selected and few (675 rows); lazer `statistics` do not split heads and tails (keys seen: perfect, great, good, ok, meh, miss, ignore_hit, ignore_miss, combo_break) | bounded by HO; identified by replays |
| Release as an action | V10 (NR), V13 | NR within player and chart (389 rows); replays: error on the next head on any lane after a release, against matched heads without one | lenient release judgement ([C14](player-data.md#c-release-window)); early release may be strategy | identified by replays; NR is a sanity check |
| Hand coordination (H against X) | V13; V10 (MR) | relation-specific slopes; MR swaps hands and tests asymmetry, not coordination | handedness; fingering | identified by replays; MR checks the formulation's mirror property |
| Population prior (exponentially fewer players manage) | V4 + V5/V6 + V7 | item response: $P(\text{pass}_{um}) = \sigma(\theta_u - \delta_m)$, then $\delta_m$ against star and demand descriptors | self-selection, retries, mods, chart age and popularity, NF passes | identifiable now, with the caveats of section 7 |

**Why even replays do not identify a cost law.** Astra's algebra (`artifacts/eval-operators-20261003/math.md` section 4 in the code checkout, usable per [d-astra-usable](operator-properties.md#d-astra-usable)) shows that a corpus density $A e^{-C}$ cannot separate the cost $C$ from the selection $A$. Replays remove $A$ but introduce a link: what is observed is $P(\text{miss} \mid \text{state})$, some monotone function of whatever "cost" is, and any $C$ with link $\ell$ and any $C' = h(C)$ with link $\ell \circ h^{-1}$ give the same data. So "exponential near the limit" at action scale is a statement about the *response function* (miss probability and error spread against slack), and that function is identified. The design should say so: the response is $P(\text{miss} \mid \text{slack}, r, \theta)$ and $\mathrm{SD}(\text{error} \mid \ldots)$, and $\kappa$, $p$ are parameters of that function, not of a cost. What would show this reading wrong: an independent measure of effort (none available) or a derivation of the link from physiology (none found, [demand-priors-sources A4](demand-priors-sources.md)).

**What player data cannot tell us at all.** The response of a canonical player (only a self-selected population, each member choosing charts near their level); the lane-to-finger binding (every relation estimate is conditional on it; a within-player mirror-symmetry test can flag players whose errors break it, in aggregate only); anything about charts nobody passes (no replay; V1 only says where plays end); what mappers or the human consider good ([L5](player-data.md#l-not-quality)); muscular against central causes; and the stable client's health rule, which is closed source.

## 3. The health bar as a known kernel

<a id="p-health-kernel"></a>[C5](player-data.md#c-health-lag) asked for a lag model. Lazer's `ManiaHealthProcessor` (ppy/osu master, fetched 2026-10-03) states one, and its comment says it preserves stable's behaviour: no passive drain in mania (`ComputeDrainRate` returns 0; the osu! wiki's Health page agrees); a miss on a note costs $(HP+1)\times 0.0075$ of the bar ($0.0675$ at HP 8; half that on an LN head or tail); a Meh costs $(HP+1)\times 0.0016$; an Ok nothing; Good, Great and Perfect refill $0.004$, $0.005$ and $0.0055$ minus about $HP \times 0.0005$, times a per-chart multiplier the processor raises until a perfect simulated play keeps health above its thresholds; fail is health at zero (`LegacyDrainingHealthProcessor`). Consequences for V1: from full health at HP 8 about 15 misses net of refills end a play, and a Perfect refills about one forty-fifth of a miss before the multiplier (arithmetic from the constants; an estimate for stable, which is closed). Health is a leaky integrator with a slow refill, so a fail sits *inside or just after* a cluster of misses, not seconds later, unless the player entered the passage already low; the lag is bounded by the cluster, which is what the demand description is meant to locate. Two covariates must enter any V1 analysis: the chart's HP (`diff_drain`) and its OD (stable hit windows: Great $64 - 3 \times OD$ ms, Miss $188 - 3 \times OD$ ms; osu! wiki, osu!mania judgement). The first 5% of a chart holds a median zero fail mass in every star band (job), as the integrator predicts; a kernel that did not predict it would be wrong.

The job's concentration check, run before any demand description was looked at: among ranked or loved charts with at least 50 effective fails, the median normalised entropy of the fail histogram is 0.83 at 1 star falling to 0.73 at 7 stars, against a uniform-multinomial null of 0.96 to 0.99 at the same counts; the five busiest bins hold a median 26% of fails at 1 star rising to 38% at 7 stars. Fails cluster far beyond sampling noise, more so as star rises. This is a precondition for U2 and U4, not a result about demand: fails could cluster at a chart's longest section or its end for reasons other than local load. What would have falsified the precondition: entropies near the null.

## 4. From corpus envelope to a player-capability frontier

<a id="p-player-frontier"></a>[p-defer-split](operator-properties.md#p-defer-split) keeps the evaluator's frontier as the mappers' edge per star. Player data can add a second frontier with a different index: for an action class or a passage, the skill $\theta^*$ at which the probability of managing it is one half, with $\theta$ mapped to a population quantile through V4 (the random sample gives the quantile function of `rank_score` among mania players with any pp). At chart scale this is the item-response $\delta_m$ of section 7; at action scale, with replays, it is the slack at which $P(\text{miss} \mid r, \theta)$ crosses a declared level, per skill. The frontier is then a family of curves indexed by skill quantile rather than one edge, and the human's "exponentially fewer players manage" is a statement about how fast the share above $\theta^*$ falls as slack shrinks.

How it sits beside the evaluator without becoming its target ([d-evaluator-purpose](operator-properties.md#d-evaluator-purpose)): the divergence compares output with corpus in the corpus's own coordinates and never reads $\theta^*$; the player frontier is content of node [response](../RESEARCH.md#response) and the natural interface of [control](../RESEARCH.md#control) ("a request for difficulty $k$ means: the share of players at quantile $q$ who manage each passage stays near what corpus charts at $k$ give"). The one legitimate crossing is U4: whether corpus-normal demand at star $k$ predicts $\delta_m$ beyond star. If not, the evaluator's demand descriptions are a normality measure and nothing more, which property 7 already allows ([p-demand-role](operator-properties.md#p-demand-role)).

Flagged tension, not a disagreement with a decision: the formulation's canonical profile excludes individual capacity and fatigue (`5c56e28:docs/formulation/gameplay-state.md`, "Canonical gameplay profile"). A skill-indexed frontier is individual capacity marginalised to quantiles; [t-formulation-physiology](operator-properties.md#t-formulation-physiology) already notes that page needs revision, and a skill index widens it.

## 5. Concrete analyses, cheapest first

<a id="p-analysis-tiers"></a>**Tier A: on disk now, within the dump licence (statistical analysis, aggregated, unpublished).**

- **A1. Pass given attempt, by skill, against demand beyond star.** The pre-registered analysis of section 7 (V4, V5/V6, V7, V8). Decides whether the population prior has support at chart scale and whether demand descriptions carry player information beyond star (U1, U4). Misleads if the descriptors proxy length, density, age or popularity, which enter as covariates. Cost: about a day of a worker on the mac.
- **A2. Rate-mod contrasts within player and chart.** V9: 29,672 lazer pairs with NM and DT bests, 6,640 with HT. Estimator: the within-pair change in accuracy (or misses per note) from NM to DT, regressed on the chart's NM distance to the edge; the steep-rise prior predicts a much larger drop for charts whose NM gaps are already short, beyond what the official star under DT (V14) predicts. Unit: pair; grouping by song. Misleads: DT is chosen on charts the player finds easy (attenuation); scroll and reading change with rate; the DT best may be a later, better player than the NM best (V11 gives the order). Second.
- **A3. Fail position against local demand within chart.** V1 with the kernel of section 3: hazard of a play ending in bin $b$ against demand descriptions of the preceding span, with HP and OD, within chart (the audience cancels); checksum-mismatch rows excluded; x9 rows weighted by effective counts; exits apart. Unit: bin within chart; grouping by song. Misleads: bins of 1.3 to 2.5 s; mods mixed (a DT fail lands at the same progress fraction, which helps); late fails in long dense sections are health unless the kernel says otherwise. Needs the demand descriptions first; third.
- **A4. LN and lane mods.** V10 within player and chart, hundreds of rows: a direction, not a test.

**Tier B: a few API calls within terms.** B1: one call each for a handful of players to verify that failed lazer plays carry `started_at` and judgement counts (V12); if so, a bounded sample of fail moments in seconds with mods known becomes possible for consenting players, a human decision. B2: twenty to fifty replays to validate the pipeline (offset removal, MR lane mapping, trailer parsing, LN head and tail attribution) before any request at scale; I would still state the purpose to ppy first, since the line between a probe and harvesting is theirs to draw.

**Tier C: with ppy's agreement.** The per-action response study: a fixed panel of the dump's sampled players (so player and chart effects separate), 6 star bands, 30 charts per band, about 25 replays per chart stratified by skill quartile (the earlier worker's core design: 4,500 replays, 45 MB, 7.5 h at 10 per minute), plus two targeted strata: NM and DT replays of the same player and chart (V9), and HO and NR replays with their NM counterparts (V10). Unit: note within play; outcome: miss, and error beyond a window; predictors: slack per relation, run index, discounted load at several $\tau$, time in dense section, time since the last break, player $\theta$. It fixes every row of section 2 marked "replays". Misleads: survivorship truncates miss *clusters* (a pass may hold many misses, only not fifteen close together), so the curve near zero slack is a lower bound; best-of-many understates error; fingering.

**Against the project's past failures.** Control V3 called window statistics a player state without a player outcome; every analysis above has a player outcome on the left-hand side and reports the increment over star with a lesion per term, so a descriptor earns its name or is dropped. The response that was never wired in: the demand code used in A1 to A3 must be the module the evaluator and later the generator call (one implementation, its hash in the report), and `response` stays open until a consumer (selection or control) is measured on played output. Evaluators blind to rate and duration: A3 is a hazard over time by construction, Tier C is per note, and A1 carries run length and section duration, not only extremes. Constants set by hand: HP and OD come from the chart, the health kernel from code, $\tau$ from a profile likelihood, and the one declared constant in section 7 (150 ms) has a source and a sweep. Noise below margin: song-group bootstrap throughout, sibling-set contrasts, thresholds fixed before the data are read. Star re-encoding (`le-star`): star is always the baseline, and under rate mods the official star under those mods (V14).

## 6. Positions

<a id="p-positions"></a>On [q-demand](operator-properties.md#q-demand):

1. *Exponential.* The population reading is testable now (A1); exponential strain decay is a descriptive scale and stays one; a per-action divergent impulse is not an identifiable object even with replays, so the design should name the response function of section 2 instead. Changes if a physiological source for the link appears.
2. *Fatigue.* Keep two history scales as descriptors; do not model $\beta$. Only Tier C can separate fatigue from health integration and strain. Changes if A3 with the health kernel shows late-section excess fails the kernel cannot absorb, which would justify the replay request by itself.
3. *Play data in scope.* Yes, as the sole source for `response`, in the order A1, A2, A3, then ppy. The dump suffices for the population prior; replays are needed for every per-action part. On contacting ppy: after A1. If demand beyond star shows at chart scale, ask for the pilot plus the two targeted strata and, in the same message, for the failtimes semantics (bin definition, x9 sampling, lazer coverage); if not, the per-action parts stay priors and the request can wait.
4. *Feasibility beyond the key star.* Yes, as a second reading, referenced to the all-star corpus envelope now and, once A1 exists, to the skill quantile at which $\delta_m$ gives a pass probability of one half.

On [p-defer-split](operator-properties.md#p-defer-split): agree with the split, change the condition. Keep the evaluator's demand side corpus-referenced and drop the divergent impulse from property 3, as it proposes. Do not defer `response`: run Tier A now, because it is cheap, within terms and the only route to content for `response` the corpus cannot give. Reverse to full deferral if A1 returns no increment beyond star with the song-bootstrap interval covering zero, or if ppy declines replays and A2 and A3 are both null.

Disclosure: the job reported that 890,319 of 1,439,692 attempted pairs (62%) have a best score. That is one overall level and says nothing about skill or demand; the pre-registration concerns shapes within skill, which were not computed.

## 7. Pre-registration of the first analysis (A1)

<a id="p-prereg-a1"></a>Written before any pass share by skill or chart was looked at. Status: proposed; the human decides whether it runs.

**Claim.** Among players of equal skill, the probability of having passed a 4K chart under normal conditions, given that they attempted it, decreases with the chart's nearest-to-edge same-finger gap beyond what star and chart size explain (C1); and the decrease per unit gap is steeper at short gaps than at long ones, so a logistic in gap underfits (C2, the population form of "exponential"). A secondary description, untested: the effect sizes order same finger > same hand > other hand per unit gap (C3).

**Unit and frame.** A pair $(u, m)$: a sampled player $u$ with at least one attempt (V7) on a ranked or loved 4K corpus chart $m$ whose `checksum_match` is true. Outcome $y_{um} = 1$ if $u$ holds a best score on $m$ in `scores` (V6, which contains V5) whose mods include none of NF, EZ, HT, DC, DT, NC, HO, NR, IN, RD, WU, WD, AS, DA, CO; else 0. Exclusions fixed now: charts with fewer than 10 attempting sampled players; players with fewer than 5 attempted corpus charts; charts outside the fit split's song groups (`data/r2-corpus.json`, `split_groups_ranked_loved`); bands 0, 7 and 8 reported but not tested.

**Predictors.** Player: $\theta_u$, a latent skill estimated jointly (crossed random effect) with $\log$ `rank_score` as its prior mean. Chart baseline $B_m$: natural cubic spline in star (4 knots), $\log$ note count, drain length, LN share, HP, OD, year of ranking, $\log$ lifetime playcount. Demand descriptors $D_m$, computed by the repository's `evaluation/beats.py` events under the canonical binding (lanes 1, 2 left middle and index; 3, 4 right index and middle), in seconds: $D_F$, $D_H$, $D_X$, the within-chart 1st-percentile gap for the same finger, the other finger of the same hand, the other hand; $R_F$, the longest run of consecutive same-finger presses with every gap at or below 150 ms (the sourced sustained single-finger maximum, [demand-priors-sources A1](demand-priors-sources.md); 120 and 180 ms reported as sensitivity, not tested). Attempts: $\log$ `playcount` of the pair as a covariate.

**Models.** $M_0$: $\mathrm{logit}\,P(y_{um}=1) = \theta_u - B_m - b_{\text{song}(m)} + \gamma \log a_{um}$. $M_1$: $M_0$ plus linear terms in $D_F, D_H, D_X, \log R_F$. $M_2$: $M_1$ with the $D_F$ term replaced by a monotone 4-knot spline. Penalised logistic regression with crossed player and song-group effects.

**Metric and thresholds.** Five-fold cross-validation by song group (players appear in every fold; charts do not). Primary: $\Delta_1 = \overline{\ell}(M_1) - \overline{\ell}(M_0)$, held-out log-likelihood per pair. C1 holds if $\Delta_1 \ge 0.003$ nats per pair, the 95% song-group bootstrap interval excludes zero, and the coefficient of $D_F$ is negative (shorter gap, lower pass odds). The threshold is an estimate: about half a percent of the Bernoulli entropy at the disclosed overall level (0.66 nats), the smallest increment worth building on. C2 holds if $\Delta_2 = \overline{\ell}(M_2) - \overline{\ell}(M_1) \ge 0.001$ nats per pair with interval excluding zero, and the spline's slope at the 5th percentile of $D_F$ is at least twice its slope at the median, within the bootstrap interval. If C1 fails, C2 is not tested. Secondary, on the same fit: within sibling sets (V8), the within-player difference in $y$ between two charts of one song against their difference in $D_F$, as a check that the effect survives holding song and audience fixed.

**Stop rule.** One run on the fit-split songs after this specification is frozen (its hash recorded with the corpus hash `49ab9e1b…` and the dump hash `2fa58ed1…`). If C1 holds, one confirmatory run of the identical models on the calibration split; the held-out split is never read. No other model family, descriptor or threshold is tried; anything that suggests one becomes a new pre-registration.

**What each outcome changes.** C1 and C2 hold: the population prior has support at chart scale; `response` gets its first content ($\delta_m$ beyond star, indexed by skill); the request to ppy is justified. C1 holds, C2 fails: demand carries player information but a logistic in gap suffices; drop "exponential" from the design, keep the descriptors. C1 fails: demand descriptors add nothing to pass beyond star at chart scale; defer `response` as [p-defer-split](operator-properties.md#p-defer-split) proposes, without reading this as refuting a per-action limit.

**How it could mislead.** Selection on unobservables (players attempt what they expect to pass) raises levels and attenuates slopes, so a null is weaker evidence than a positive. Players who mostly play modded have attempts in the frame and no clean pass, which lowers $y$ for them; sensitivity: $y$ with rate and LN mods allowed, reported beside. A wrong fingering for a share of players attenuates $D_F$ against $D_H$. Imported stable and lazer scores of one pair are one pair. The x9 sampling and lazer fail coverage do not touch this analysis. A positive $D_F$ effect could still be a mapping-era effect if year does not absorb it; the sibling-set estimate guards against song and era.

## 8. Observations, dead ends, rejected ideas

- `scores` holds the stable bests as imports; the two score tables are one view. Rejected: treating them as independent samples.
- The stable `_high` table holds about 1.6 rows per player and chart; the natural reading is one best per mod combination; not verified in code.
- HO, NR and IN appear in the `ranked = 1` table, so lazer records them as ranked; the wiki summary does not list them and the mod sources fetched show no `Ranked` property, so the status is read from the data.
- Rate-mod use is a selection signal in itself: players move a chart toward their frontier with HT or DT. Rejected: DT share as a difficulty measure.
- Exits are 45% of plays, fails 11%. Rejected: exits as failures in any model; a separate outcome.
- `started_at` is set only on lazer rows (17% of corpus rows); dead end for stable-era sessions.
- Lazer mania `statistics` carry `ignore_hit` and `ignore_miss` in a minority of rows; what they count is not settled from the dump; a replay with its trailer would settle it.
- Rejected: fitting a cost law to replays; what replays identify is the response function, and the design should name it as the target.
- Deferred: the `top_10000` dump (1.9 GB) for the 6+ star upper bound, until A1 shows signal and the upper bound matters.
- Not done, by design: no pass share by skill or demand, no replay, no API request.
